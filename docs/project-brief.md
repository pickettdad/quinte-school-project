# Project brief — Quinte tuition-free school for students with intellectual and developmental learning needs

**Status:** Phase 2 brief, set by the founder on 2026-10-15. Supersedes the Phase 1 brief (2026-10-14) where they differ.
**Authoritative for:** project direction, design targets and founder constraints. Research files must not contradict it without recording a proposed change in `docs/open-questions.md`.

## Purpose

Design the lowest-cost, effective and reliable tuition-free school for students with intellectual and developmental learning needs in the Quinte area, using creative staffing, community partnerships, philanthropy and legitimate public or insured services.

## Direction (founder decisions)

- Full-time, five-day school replacing regular public-school attendance.
- Elementary and secondary students from the outset.
- **Opening design target: 50 students**, with room to expand. Lower-enrolment stress tests and an expansion scenario are required, but neither is the preferred opening plan. Do not default to a 12–30-student model.
- Belleville preferred hub, serving the Trenton–Stirling–Tyendinaga area.
- No tuition cost to families.
- Strong academics, communication, independence, friendships and community participation.
- Small instructional groups matched to actual skills and learning needs; groupings may differ by subject.
- Practical learning through cooking, gardening, a student market, projects and community experiences, complementing substantial direct instruction.
- Respect chronological age in activities, relationships, privacy and teenage experiences.

## Students

- Organize by learning profile, communication, social compatibility and support needs — not diagnosis alone.
- The opening school is not intended to provide every level of intensive support; define a realistic population that can take part in small-group instruction and shared activities with the resources available, and identify what added resources continuous 1:1 staffing, complex medical needs or other intensive support would require.
- Fair, individualized admissions criteria; verify accommodation obligations. Predicted economic productivity or perceived personal worth is never an admission criterion.
- Students with Down syndrome are considered through the same individualized criteria (no separate workstream).
- Assess progress and belonging; do not assume a specialized setting automatically produces better outcomes.

## Capital and premises (planning assumptions)

- Main operating scenario assumes founding donors, capital gifts and naming sponsorships cover acquisition, renovation and initial equipment **without debt**. This is a planning assumption, not secured funding.
- Keep realistic utilities, maintenance, insurance and replacement reserves. Capital funding is separate from annual operating support; never count a sponsorship twice.
- Reassess individually: Quinte Secondary School, Queen Victoria School, Sir Winston Churchill School (all declared surplus by HPEDSB, Sept 2026). Retain churches and other adaptable buildings as alternatives.
- Investigate whole-property acquisition, partial occupancy, a separately functioning wing, long-term lease within a redevelopment, and shared premises with compatible organizations. Test whether partial-occupancy costs can really be separated.
- The 2016–17 QSS School Information Profile (`reference/`) is historical evidence, not current condition.

## Transportation

- Comprehensive door-to-door transportation is **not** an opening requirement.
- Compare: family transport with chartered outings; a sponsored local operator serving concentrated pickup points; one donated/sponsored bus making successive runs; a second bus as later expansion.
- Two staggered start/end times are acceptable; design schedules, routes and staff shifts together with comparable instructional time per cohort.
- "The McDougall Insurance bus" is an illustration of the transport-sponsorship concept, not an approached or committed sponsor.

## Staffing

- Dependable paid teaching and support team, supplemented extensively by trained recurring volunteers, retired educators, college placements, visiting clinicians, partner organizations and donated professional services — with coordination, screening, training, supervision, semester gaps and backups costed.
- Quantify which paid hours volunteers could genuinely replace versus enhance. Essential teaching and care must stay reliable when volunteers are unavailable.
- Consider shared payroll, bookkeeping, facilities, procurement and administration.
- Paid executive and educational leadership. The founder may serve initially as executive director and is open to someone else leading later. Do not assume indefinite unpaid founder work; the founder's current employment is not a constraint.

## Public and insured services

- Investigate Ontario Health atHome supports for eligible private-school students (personal support, nursing, OT, PT, SLP, other), distinguishing eligibility from local availability, assessed hours and delivery.
- Investigate psychology, social work and other services delivered at school through family health benefits — voluntary, unrelated to admission, with access preserved for uninsured families. Unused benefits are never school revenue.
- Explore partnerships with developmental-service and healthcare organizations: payer, recipient, eligible activity, restrictions, approval route.

## Funding and financing

- Local fundraising capacity is to be investigated, not inferred as a ceiling from a few existing charities. The founder has substantial local business relationships and partnership experience; the largest gap is access to regional and national organizations, foundations and funds.
- Maintain a qualified prospect register (`funding/prospect-register.md`) separating committed, plausible and speculative.
- Keep exploring interest-free or forgivable financing; borrowing must never conceal a recurring operating deficit.
- Student enterprises are educational first; estimate realistic net proceeds and never rely on them for core operations.
- Maintain a short running list of government questions (`docs/government-questions.md`) for a future conversation with MPP Allsopp — no polished brief yet.

## Rules of engagement

- **No outreach** to property owners, boards, funders, businesses or officials without the founder's authorization.
- The repository is public: no identifiable child records, family benefit details or confidential donor information.
- Separate statutory requirements, professional recommendations, planning assumptions and founder decisions. Record recommendations separately from approved decisions (`docs/decision-log.md`).
- Preserve dated report snapshots unchanged (`reports/`).
