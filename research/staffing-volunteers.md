## 0. Summary

- A 50-student school at the working ratio (6 instructional groups of 8–9, each 1 teacher + 2 EAs) needs **27.2 paid FTE** at a **loaded 2026 cost of $1.668M**, plus EHT on payroll above the charity exemption (~$9K), a supply/relief budget (~$47K) and contracted clinicians (~$246K): **≈ $1.97M total people cost (≈ $39,400 per student)**; ≈ **$2.09M in 2028 dollars** at 3%/yr. [ESTIMATE — arithmetic in §1 and §9]
- Loaded-cost factors (statutory + $3,000 benefits): **1.125 for a $70K salaried teacher, 1.25 for a school-year hourly EA, 1.10 for leadership.** v1's flat 1.18/1.20 is replaced by a per-role formula (§1.2) that adds ESA public-holiday pay for school-year hourly staff, which v1 omitted. [ESTIMATE]
- The executive director and the educational leader should **not** be one person at 50 students; the volunteer coordinator should **not** be the front-office administrator. The realistic combination is a two-person "community team" (development lead 1.0 + volunteer/placement coordinator 0.6). [PROPOSAL, tested in §2]
- Volunteers (≈40 recurring, ≈120 h/week in term) can **genuinely replace about 1,800 paid hours a year (≈1.0 FTE of support work worth ≈ $55–60K)** — grounds/garden, kitchen help, office support, event labour, light maintenance — but they cost ≈ $40K to coordinate, screen and train, so the **net saving is ≈ $15–20K**. Their real value is enhancement: outing ratios, reading/tutoring, student market, fundraising reach. **No teaching, personal-care, behaviour-support or supervision-ratio hour is replaced by a volunteer or placement student.** [ESTIMATE/PROPOSAL, §3]
- Retired OCT members are the best relief pool. The OTPP 50-day re-employment limit applies only to **participating employers** (school boards, *designated* private schools, designated organizations); a new independent school that has not sought designation is not one, so retirees' days here should not count — **CONFIRM with OTPP** before relying on it. [VERIFIED rule / CONFIRM application, §4]
- Loyalist College DSW, Child & Youth Care and Social Service Worker placements are the most relevant (2–3 days/week, 6 h/day, 10–12-week blocks in Jan–Apr and Sept–Dec). Placement students are never counted in ratio and never left alone with students. [VERIFIED program structure, §5]
- Payroll (Wagepoint ≈ $2,500/yr), group benefits (pooled nonprofit program) and year-end accounting should be bought, not staffed; a 1.0 FTE in-house office administrator/registrar is still required because the front desk and student records cannot be outsourced. [VERIFIED pricing / PROPOSAL, §7]
- Leadership: paid ED (**$95,000** base) and paid educational leader (**$100,000** base), with the founder as initial ED under a written 3-year handover plan and a majority-independent board; the founder should not be a voting director while paid. [PROPOSAL, §8]

## 1. Paid core team for 50 students

### 1.1 Design basis

| Item | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Students | 50 (≈25 elementary, ≈25 secondary), two staggered cohorts for arrival/dismissal | FOUNDER DECISION (brief) |
| Working instructional ratio | 6–12 students per teacher with 2 EAs (education agent's working ratio; comparables cluster at 6–12 : 1 : 2 — v1 education note §8) | ASSUMPTION |
| Groups | **6 homeroom groups of 8–9** (3 elementary, 3 secondary); regrouping by subject across the three groups in each band | PROPOSAL |
| Adult:student in instruction | 18 instructional staff : 50 = **1 : 2.8**, before leader, float EA, rotation teacher | ESTIMATE |
| School year | 194 staff days (≈187 instructional + PD), 40 operating weeks for contractors | ASSUMPTION (Ontario public-school convention) |
| Instructional day | ≈ 5.0 h student contact; staff day 7.5 paid h (EAs) / salaried (teachers) | ASSUMPTION |
| Teacher preparation | 4 h/week per teacher (≈16% of contact time; comparable to Ontario elementary ~240 min/week) | ASSUMPTION |

Why a 7th teacher: 6 teachers × 4 h/week prep = 24 h/week of classes that someone else must teach. A **1.0 FTE practical-learning / rotation teacher** (physical education, arts, food/garden/market program) covers all prep blocks and delivers the practical curriculum the brief requires, instead of EAs or the leader covering prep informally. [PROPOSAL]

Why a float EA: EA absence runs 16–27 days/year in Ontario boards (§2.4). A permanent 13th EA who knows every student costs about the same as casual cover and is far more reliable. [PROPOSAL]

### 1.2 Loaded-cost formula (2026 rates)

All statutory rates are VERIFIED in v1 §6 (CRA CPP/CPP2/EI pages, WSIB 2026 premium rates, Ontario EHT and ESA pages; accessed Sept–Oct 2026). Benefits and holiday-pay treatment are new.

```
Gross pay            = base wage/salary  (+ ESA public-holiday pay for school-year hourly staff: 8 holidays × daily rate)
Vacation pay         = 4% × gross   (hourly staff, <5 yrs service; 6% at ≥5 yrs)   — salaried staff: vacation embedded in salary, $0
CPP (employer)       = 5.95% × (min(gross+vac, $74,600) − $3,500)      max $4,230.45
CPP2 (employer)      = 4.00% × (min(gross+vac, $85,000) − $74,600)     max $416.00   (only salaries > $74,600)
EI (employer)        = 2.282% × min(gross+vac, $68,900)                max $1,572.30
WSIB                 = 0.33% × (gross+vac)        Class D1 "Educational services" 2026 (one rate per business by predominant activity;
                                                   the driver is costed at D1 not F2 — CONFIRM with WSIB; opt-in status itself is still open, v1 legal §5)
EHT                  = $0 per employee; computed at entity level: 1.95% × (total Ontario payroll − $1,000,000 charity exemption)
Group benefits       = $3,000 per employee at ≥ 0.5 FTE (employer share; see basis below)
Loaded cost per FTE  = gross + vacation + CPP + CPP2 + EI + WSIB + benefits
```

**Group-benefits basis.** PolicyAdvisor's 2026 small-business guide (10-employee illustration, published 23 Mar 2026, modified 27 Jul 2026) puts typical health + dental + life at **$150 (basic) / $205 (standard) / $275 (enhanced) per employee per month**, entry plans from $70–90, LTD not priced; it states 10–20% of payroll for small employers and 5–10% with cost controls. [SECONDARY — broker guide, not a survey; https://www.policyadvisor.com/employee-benefits/cost-of-small-business-employee-benefits-in-canada, accessed 2026-10-15]. Planning value: **$250/month employer share = $3,000/yr per eligible employee** (standard tier, allowing for some family coverage and a small LTD/EAP add-on), bought through a pooled nonprofit program (§7). v1 used 8% of salary ($5,600 on $70K); the flat-dollar figure is a better fit for a plan that costs the same for a $37K EA as for a $70K teacher. [ESTIMATE] No pension/RRSP match is in the base; a 3% group-RRSP match would add ≈ $44K/yr at 50 students (sensitivity, §9).

**Public-holiday pay.** Eight ESA public holidays fall inside a September–June work year (Labour Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas, Boxing Day, New Year's, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day); school-year hourly staff who qualify must be paid for them in addition to their 194 worked days. [VERIFIED rule, v1 legal §5 ESA guide; day count is ESTIMATE] v1 omitted this (~$1,560/yr per EA).

## 2. Reconciling combined roles, workload and absence coverage

### 2.1 Can the executive director also be the educational leader? — No at 50 students. [PROPOSAL]

Workload test (hours/week in term, ESTIMATE):

| Educational leader | h/wk | Executive director | h/wk |
|---|---|---|---|

| Parent communication, incident follow-up, behaviour escalations, safeguarding lead | 7 | Finance: budget, cash flow, payroll approval, audit, insurance, facilities contracts | 6 |

| Absence cover decisions, timetable, PD planning, curriculum/assessment oversight | 5 | Transport, food, premises oversight; risk and emergency planning | 3 |
| Teaching/covering when tier-2/3 cover fails | 4 | | |
| **≈ 40** | | **≈ 40** | |

Combining gives one person ≈ 80 h/week in term; the first thing dropped is the fundraising calendar, which is the revenue model. At 30 students the educational-leader load falls to ≈ 30 h (4 groups, 30 IEPs) and the ED could drop to 0.8 FTE, but the two roles should still be separate people (the school's licence to operate — the principal present on day one for the Ministry validation visit, v1 legal §3 — and its revenue both depend on a person who is not stretched across both). The one combination that *can* work in a lower-enrolment stress test is **educational leader + 0.2 FTE classroom teaching** (a "teaching principal"), not leader + ED.

### 2.2 Can the volunteer coordinator be the administrator? — No; pair it with development instead. [PROPOSAL]

- The office administrator is desk-anchored 8:00–4:30: safe-arrival calls for two cohorts, phones, visitors and sign-in (safeguarding), student records/OnSIS, AP/AR entry, bus/clinician scheduling. Leaving the desk to run a volunteer orientation breaks safe-arrival and visitor control.
- Volunteer coordination is mobile and partly out-of-hours: recruiting at churches/service clubs/college fairs, evening orientations, classroom check-ins, college placement liaison, VSC paperwork, database and recognition. At 40 recurring volunteers and 8–12 placements it is ≈ 0.6 FTE (benchmark §3.2).
- A 1.0 admin + 0.6 coordination merged into one person is 1.6 FTE of work. The workable pairing is **development lead (1.0) + volunteer & placement coordinator (0.6)** as a two-person community team reporting to the ED: volunteers are the donor and ambassador pipeline, and events need both. If enrolment stays at 30, the coordinator drops to 0.4 and could be the same person as a part-time development assistant.

### 2.3 Breaks, supervision ratios and staggered cohorts [PROPOSAL; ESA rules VERIFIED v1 legal §5]

- ESA: a 30-minute eating period within every 5 hours; EAs are paid 7.5 h over an 8-h span (unpaid lunch); teachers' lunch is embedded in salary but still owed.
- Staggered day (illustrative, to be aligned with the transport agent's run times): Cohort A 8:30–3:00, Cohort B 9:15–3:45. A-shift EAs 8:00–4:00, B-shift EAs 8:45–4:45; A teachers 8:00–3:30, B teachers 8:45–4:15. Each 45-minute overlap window has one cohort's 3 teachers + 6 EAs (1:2.8) plus the float EA and leader; the other cohort's staff are in prep or meetings. The practical/rotation teacher and float EA cover the 15-minute breaks.
- Student lunch (two 45-minute sittings): with half the EAs on their own lunch, each sitting is supervised at ≈ 25 : 7 (1:3.6). Volunteers add adults but are never counted in the ratio.
- Secondary students should not supervise or care for younger students (v1 education note §3 proposal).

### 2.4 Absence benchmark, supply cost and relief-pool design

**Benchmarks (Ontario publicly funded boards):**
- School Boards' Co-operative Inc. report obtained by The Globe and Mail: average sick days 2017-18 — elementary teachers 12.76, secondary teachers 10.91, **educational assistants 17.88**, ETFO education workers 15.09, custodial 16.86 (up from 8.3 / 7.19 / 11.04 / 7.88 / 13.27 in 2010-11). [SECONDARY — newspaper report of an SBCI report; https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-ontarios-teachers-education-workers-using-more-sick-days-now-than, accessed 2026-10-15]
- Ontario average ≈ **16 sick days per school staff member (2022-23, SBCI data)**; TDSB 2023-24: EAs 27.23, custodians 25.14, elementary teachers 20.80, secondary 17.99. [SECONDARY — The Hub commentary, 13 Nov 2024, https://thehub.ca/2024/11/13/paul-w-bennett-students-skipping-class-is-nothing-new-but-teachers-missing-school-too-is-a-fast-growing-problem, accessed 2026-10-15; TDSB sick days cost "$213 million last year", CTV 11 Jun 2024]
- A small mission-driven school without a bankable sick-leave plan will likely run lower than board rates, but planning must not assume it. **Planning rates: teachers 13 days, EAs 16 days, all other staff 10 days** (sick + personal + bereavement + appointments). [ASSUMPTION]

**Expected absence-days at 50 students:** teachers 7 × 13 = 91 days; EAs 13 × 16 = 208 days; leadership/admin/other ≈ 60 days (not covered).

**Supply cost basis:** casual supply teacher $240/day + ≈ 8.6% statutory (CPP/EI/WSIB, vacation 4%) ≈ **$260/day** [ESTIMATE — v1 found no private-school daily rate; public boards pay occasional teachers roughly $250–280/day under the 2022–26 grids, not re-verified]; casual EA $26/h × 7.5 h + 4% vacation + statutory ≈ **$230/day** [ESTIMATE].

**Relief design (four tiers) [PROPOSAL]:**
1. **Float EA (permanent, 1.0 FTE, $47,265):** first call for any EA absence; otherwise assigned to the highest-need group, breaks and arrival/dismissal. Provides ≈ 180 usable cover days after its own absences — roughly the expected 208 EA absence-days, but absences cluster in flu season, hence tier 2.
2. **Casual list (paid per day, ≥ 10 names):** retired educators and EAs (§4), recent Loyalist DSW/CYC graduates, supply EAs; all VSC-screened, oriented and trained in the school's behaviour plans before first call; budget 91 teacher-days × $260 + 100 EA-days × $230 = **$46,660/yr**. Target: every teacher absence covered by a casual who has already worked with the group.
3. **Internal reconfiguration:** if no casual is available, two groups in the same band merge for the day (≈17 students : 1 teacher + 4 EAs + float = 1:2.8 — safe, lower instructional intensity); rotation blocks are cancelled and the practical teacher takes a group.
4. **Leader cover:** the educational leader teaches as a last resort; the ED never does.

**Never used as cover:** volunteers, placement students, clinicians. Essential teaching and care therefore remain reliable with zero volunteers present (§3.5).

Paid sick leave is not legislated beyond ESA's 3 unpaid days; recommended policy: 10 paid sick/personal days per year, pro-rated, non-cumulative, no retirement gratuity. [PROPOSAL]

### 3.4 Training, supervision, gaps and backups [PROPOSAL]

- **Training (7 h per volunteer; ~4 h for one-off event volunteers):** 3-h orientation (mission, roles, boundaries, privacy, emergency, AODA customer-service training which is mandatory for volunteers of a 1–19/20–49-employee organization — v1 legal §5 VERIFIED), 2-h safeguarding (two-adult rule, no personal care, duty to report under CYFSA, reporting concerns), 2-h behaviour basics (communication supports, prompting, what to do when a student is distressed: call staff). Delivered quarterly by the coordinator + educational leader; ≈ 60 h/yr of their time.
- **Supervision:** every volunteer is assigned to a named staff member; no volunteer is ever alone with a student, delivers personal care, administers medication, restrains, drives students in a private vehicle (insurance — v1 legal §10), or is counted in a supervision ratio.

## 4. Retired educators

### 4.1 OTPP re-employment limit [VERIFIED rule; CONFIRM application]

- Ontario Teachers' (OTPP): a retired member may work for a **participating employer** for **50 days in each school year** without affecting the pension; "There's currently no change to the 50-day re-employment limit." All work days count including paid non-working days (PD, sick); hourly work is converted at 5.5–9 h per day (e.g., 110 h = 20 days); on exceeding, the pension stops for every month worked after the month the limit is passed (cannot be avoided by refusing pay), resuming the earlier of a month with no re-employment or the following 1 September; employers report service, members must track and notify. [VERIFIED; https://otpp.com/en-ca/members/life-events/living-in-retirement/your-reemployment-limit/, accessed 2026-10-15]
- The limit applies to "any employer that participates in the OTPP," i.e., "school boards in Ontario, designated private schools, and designated organizations"; retirees are advised to ask whether a prospective employer participates. [SECONDARY — OECTA casebook, https://catholicteachers.ca/For-Your-Career/Professional-Advice/Casebook-Diary/Exceeding-re-employment-limits, accessed 2026-10-15]
- The temporary 95-day limit (2021–22 to 30 June 2023) has expired; the current limit is 50 days. [SECONDARY — OTF notices; current OTPP page says 50]
- **Implication:** a new independent school that is not a *designated* private school under the Teachers' Pension Act is not a participating employer, so days worked there should not count toward a retiree's 50-day limit, which makes retired OCT members a far deeper relief pool than they are for boards. **CONFIRM in writing with OTPP** (and note that if the school ever seeks OTPP designation to recruit career teachers, the limit would then bind). Retirees should also check their own pension situation; the school should put the question in its casual-staff letter.

### 4.2 OCT membership [SECONDARY/CONFIRM]

The clean structure: **founder is a paid employee, not a voting director**, with a majority-independent board, a written conflict-of-interest policy, and an independent compensation decision at fair market value.
