## 0. Summary

- **FOUNDER DECISION (2026-10-15):** comprehensive door-to-door transport is not an opening requirement; two staggered start/end times are acceptable; "the McDougall Insurance bus" is an illustration only — **no sponsor has been approached**.
- **PROPOSAL — recommended opening configuration:** one school-owned (ideally donated or sponsored) accessible 20–24-passenger bus driven by a Class E driver, making two successive runs each morning and afternoon to **concentrated pickup points** (west: Trenton + Bayside; east: Shannonville + Belleville-east), feeding two cohorts on a **65-minute stagger (Cohort A 8:25–2:25, Cohort B 9:30–3:30)**. Carries **32–36 of 50 students**; the remaining 14–18 (mostly Belleville-local families) use family transport. Stirling/Frankford and Deseronto/Marysville families drive to a pickup point or to school at opening.
- **Annual cost of the recommended configuration (ESTIMATE):** ≈ **$94,000/yr all-in** (≈ $68,000 without the on-bus attendant) plus **$35–45K capital** for a used lift bus (≈ $150–190K new); ≈ $2,600–2,900 per transported student, between the HPEDSB per-student benchmark ($1,965) and its per-km benchmark applied to our mileage ($114K).
- **Second bus (expansion):** adds ≈ $65K/yr + $35–45K capital and lifts coverage to 48–50 students and one far corridor; cheaper than staggering only if ≥ ~$40K/yr of it is externally funded, or once transported demand exceeds ~36.
- **Biggest regulatory unknown (CONFIRM with MTO):** whether a private school's own daily home-to-school bus must be a chrome-yellow "school bus" (stop arm, SCHOOL BUS signs) or may be a white accessible minibus; and what sponsor lettering Reg. 612 allows. Plan on a chrome-yellow Type A lift bus until answered.

## 2. Four configurations compared

Common assumptions: 50 students; lift bus with 16 ambulatory seats + 2 wheelchair positions per run (ASSUMPTION; a 24-seat non-lift layout carries more but excludes wheelchair users); one attendant (EA/monitor) on every run carrying students with disabilities (PROPOSAL — see §7; no statutory requirement found); pickup points are parking lots lent by churches, arenas or municipalities (PROPOSAL; permissions needed, no owner approached).

### 2.1 Configuration 1 — family transport; chartered buses for outings only

- Capital: nil (optionally one accessible 7-passenger minivan $22–60K used/new, v1 KQ7, for emergencies and small outings — PROPOSAL).
- Operating (ESTIMATE): outings only. 30 charter-days/yr × $650 (local small-market day rate within the $389–850 Toronto band) = $19,500; accessible-vehicle supplement for wheelchair users (Quinte Access/Quinte Transit bookings or an accessible charter) $3,000; total ≈ **$22–25K/yr**. Non-owned-auto insurance extension for parent/volunteer drivers (v1 legal note) ≈ $1–2K (ESTIMATE).
- Students served by the school: 0 of 50. Realistic reach (ESTIMATE): the v1 KQ5 analysis found that door-to-door sweeping is impossible anyway, so every design already asks families to drive to a point; the question is whether a daily 20–30-minute each-way drive, twice a day, for 194 days is sustainable. Planning assumption: **10–20% of otherwise-interested families cannot manage it** (two-earner households, single vehicle, rural Tyendinaga/Stirling-Rawdon), concentrated in exactly the lower-income and rural families the school most wants to reach. The v1 KQ6 survey instrument already asks "maximum acceptable one-way ride" and "willingness to drive to a pickup point"; add "could you drive both ways daily?".

## 3. The staggered schedule, designed concretely

### 3.1 The binding constraint (one bus, two cohorts, equal day length)

Let R = run time (first pickup → school) and D = deadhead (school → first pickup, no stops). Morning: run 1 arrives for Cohort A; the bus then deadheads D₂ and runs R₂ for Cohort B, so the stagger S ≥ D₂ + R₂ + unload (≈ 5–8 min). Afternoon: Cohort A (the run-1 students, who must leave first for equal day length) is driven home (R₁) and the bus deadheads back (D₁) for Cohort B, so S ≥ R₁ + D₁ + load. Hence **S ≥ max(D₁ + R₁, D₂ + R₂) + ~8 min**. The run that serves the farther points sets the stagger. This is why a single bus cannot both reach Deseronto/Stirling/Frankford and hold a one-hour stagger (ESTIMATE, arithmetic below).

A 90-minute stagger would admit E+ or N but costs 30 more minutes of thin-staffed edge time each day and pushes Cohort B to a 10:00 start — not recommended.

- **Driver absence:** two staff hold Class E before opening (driver + one of custodian/EA/coordinator); licence takes ≈ 2–3 months (MTO-approved school-bus driver improvement course, medical, knowledge and road tests; VERIFIED handbook requirements, §7). ## 4. Geographic grouping vs educational grouping

**The problem.** Cohorts assigned by bus run are geographic, not educational. Each cohort of ≈ 25 (16–18 bused + 7–9 family-transported) will span ages 6–21 and every learning profile. The brief requires small instructional groups matched to skills (groupings may differ by subject) and age-respecting activities.

**Reconciliation (PROPOSAL).**

1. **Core window 9:30–2:25 (4 h 55, ≈ 4 h 15 instructional after lunch and a break) is school-wide.** All 50 students are present; subject groupings (literacy, numeracy, communication, practical learning) are formed across cohorts on skills and compatibility, exactly as if there were one bell time. Age bands for social and practical activities are also school-wide in this window.
2. **Edge hours are cohort-only but still age-banded within the cohort.** 8:25–9:30 (Cohort A, west) and 2:25–3:30 (Cohort B, east): ≈ 25 students with the early (or late) staff team, split into an elementary and a secondary group (≈ 12/13 each). Content: arrival routines, breakfast, movement, AAC/communication practice, individual IEP goals and independent-work stations in the morning edge; practical learning, enterprise/market preparation, clubs, community walks and transition routines in the afternoon edge. Rotate the content weekly so Cohort A does not always get the "academic" edge and Cohort B the "practical" one.
3. **Use the family-transported students as the balancing lever.** The 14–18 students who do not ride the bus can be assigned to either cohort to even out age bands, support needs and social compatibility (they are not tied to a run). Pickup-point assignment can also move a few students between runs where geography is ambiguous (west Belleville ↔ east Belleville).
4. **Alternative considered — stagger by age band (elementary 8:25, secondary 9:30).** Educationally cleaner (and a later start suits adolescents — ASSUMPTION, not verified here), but both runs would then have to cover the whole catchment, which one bus cannot do (chains > 80 min). Feasible only in Configuration 4 (one bus per corridor, each doing an elementary run and a secondary run: W-elementary 8:25, W-secondary 9:30, same on E) — note it in the expansion path.
5. **Comparable instructional time.** Both cohorts: 6 h 00 bell-to-bell, 300+ instructional minutes, identical lunch/break allowance; the only asymmetry is which hour is cohort-only, handled by rotation.

**Staffing shape.** Two shift patterns, no split shifts for teaching staff: Early team 8:00–3:00 (with Cohort A from 8:25), Late team 9:00–4:00 (with Cohort B to 3:30). Overlap 9:00–3:00 is when the whole team is present; common planning time moves to Fridays 2:30–3:30 (Cohort B's practical block run by the late team + volunteers) or to a weekly early-release — PROPOSAL for the staffing file.

**Result:** S (stagger) is cheaper by ≈ **$25K/yr plus the capital**. T wins when (a) a sponsor funds the second bus's capital and ≥ ~$40K/yr of its operating cost, (b) transported demand exceeds ~36, or (c) the far corridors must be served directly (T removes the chain constraint, §3.1). Non-financial: T gives a single bell time, full-staff overlap all day and real breakdown cover; S gives a bus free for outings mid-day and a 7-hour supervised building day that some working families value. **PROPOSAL:** open with S; move to T (Configuration 4) when enrolment or a vehicle gift justifies it.

## 6. Bus availability for outings (mid-day window)

- Window: ≈ 9:30–2:10 (4 h 40) every school day in Configuration 3. The driver's split shift becomes a continuous day on outing days (7:15–4:25, ≈ 9 h on duty) — lawful, and cheaper than a charter: marginal outing cost ≈ driver 3–4 h × $26.40 + 60 km fuel ≈ **$120–140** vs $400–650 chartered. With 40 outings/yr the saving is ≈ $14–18K/yr (ESTIMATE), which pays most of the attendant's extra bus time.
- Trade-offs: outings limited to **≤ 18 students** (one group) and must end by 2:05 so the bus is back for Cohort A's dismissal; a breakdown on an outing jeopardises both afternoon runs (make the standby arrangement cover this); the driver's day lengthens and the backup driver must cover outings on the driver's days off. Whole-school outings (25–50 students) still need a charter (6/yr ≈ $4K).
- The staggered design makes this window possible; a single bell time with one bus (one run) would free the bus for a longer window (9:00–2:30) but carry only 16–18 students.

## 7. Sponsorship: advertising revenue vs a program sponsor

**Ordinary advertising (exterior/interior panels).** v1 KQ5 concluded the net from 1–3 buses is **$1–5K/yr — immaterial**; do not budget it. Whether Reg. 612 permits any non-prescribed exterior lettering remains **UNRESOLVED** (gap note §1: e-Laws JS-only, CanLII robots-blocked; this pass: e-Laws again JS-only, www.ontario.ca policy-denied to curl, and the amending O. Reg. 129/10 page likewise). Do not state what is permitted until the regulation text is read.

**A substantial program sponsorship (the "named insurer/dealership underwrites bus + driver" idea).** No sponsor has been approached (FOUNDER DECISION). Order of magnitude it would need to cover (ESTIMATE, from §2.3): **capital $40K used (or $150–190K new) + ≈ $87K/yr operating excluding depreciation → ≈ $475–525K over 5 years with a used bus, ≈ $600–650K with a new one; i.e. ≈ $100K/yr.** v1 KQ4 sized realistic local presenting-level commitments at $25–50K/yr, so a full-program sponsor at this level would be the largest corporate commitment in the local market; the likelier shape is a **split**: a dealership or fleet owner gives the vehicle (in kind) and an insurer or employer underwrites operating cost for 3–5 years, or a sponsor covers capital only. Count it once: it replaces, not adds to, the corporate-giving line (v1 KQ5).

How it would be structured (SECONDARY on CRA rules, v1 KQ4; CONFIRM with charity counsel):

| Form | Tax treatment | Recognition | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Restricted gift** (cash or vehicle) for the transportation program | Receiptable; a vehicle is a gift in kind receipted at appraised FMV (dealer inventory: deemed-FMV rules differ — CONFIRM); donated *services* (e.g. a driver's time) are not receiptable | Nominal only: "Transportation made possible by X" small decal, website, newsletter; no promotional claims, no exclusivity | Best for an insurer/employer wanting a receipt |

| **Mixed** | Split receipting: eligible amount = gift − FMV of advantage; no receipt if advantage > 80% | Documented FMV of the advantage | Common in practice |

Recognition on the vehicle: on a **chrome-yellow school bus**, exterior lettering is governed by Reg. 612 (prescribed colour, SCHOOL BUS signs, owner name — other lettering UNRESOLVED); on a **non-yellow school-purposes bus or van**, no Ontario rule restricting a sponsor decal was found, but Reg. 612 may still apply to school-purposes vehicles — **CONFIRM with MTO** before promising any sponsor a wrap. Insurer consent is needed for a wrap (v1 KQ5). A named sponsor must also be acceptable under the school's donor-recognition and naming policy with revocation clauses (v1 KQ4), and under advertising-to-children norms if the sponsor markets to students.
