# Educational design and staffing structure — 50-student opening model

**Status:** Phase 2 research, 2026-10-15. Desk research only. Builds on v1 note `reports/2026-10-14-v1-research-notes/educational_design_and_comparables.md` (sections 1–3, 5, 6, 8, 9) and v1 report sections 1–2; evidence settled there is cited, not re-researched. ## 1. Design principles carried forward from v1

1. The evidence supports *what* the school does — explicit, systematic, high-dosage small-group literacy and numeracy over years, task-analysed functional skills taught in vivo, EAs used in structured roles — more than *where* it does it (v1 §2, VERIFIED: Allor et al. 2014; Spooner et al. 2011; Hudson et al. 2018; EEF 2020). The best syntheses find no reliable academic or psychosocial advantage for either specialized or inclusive settings (v1 §2, VERIFIED: Dalgaard et al. 2022; Dell'Anna et al. 2020). The school's case rests on instructional intensity, continuity and belonging, not on a claim that congregated settings produce better outcomes.
2. Group by **learning pathway inside chronological age bands** (v1 §3: Ash Lea, Lisburne, Hazel Court, Summit, HPEDSB LSP/CIP). Pathways move; bands do not.
3. Staffing benchmark **6–12 students : 1 teacher : 2 EAs** (v1 §8, VERIFIED comparators DDSB 6:1:2, OCDSB DD ~10:1:2.4, Oak Bridge 10:1:2, Project SEARCH 10:1:2), with O. Reg. 298 s.31 maxima (DD 10; autism/multiple 6; MID 12/16; mixed 16) as a public-system reference that does not bind a private school (VERIFIED via Ministry summary; regulation text not read — e-Laws is JavaScript-gated, re-tested 2026-10-15).
4. Secondary pathways: non-credit Certificate of Accomplishment track as base; OSSC credit track only with credit-granting authority (v1 §5; OQ-4 open).
5. Design *against* the known risks of congregated settings: thinner friendship pools, downtime, loss of typical-peer language models (v1 §2, VERIFIED: Heiman 2000; Olsson et al. 2018; Matzen et al. 2010; Buckley et al. 2006).

**FOUNDER DECISION (2026-10-15):** opening design target is 50 students, elementary and secondary from the outset, five-day full-time, no tuition, two staggered start times acceptable. The v1 proposal to open with a single secondary band of 8–12 is superseded.

## 2. The 50-student structure

### 2.1 Age split and worked roster — ASSUMPTION

HPEDSB's IPRC counts put secondary IDD+MID identification above elementary even though secondary enrolment is roughly a third of the board: Oct 2024 elementary 163 / secondary 211 (44/56); Oct 2025 figures held by the project 149 / 243 (38/62) (v1 report §2, VERIFIED for Oct 2024; Oct 2025 CONFIRM). Columns are not mutually exclusive and include an unknown number of 18–21-year-olds (OQ-1). For planning, the school assumes a **40/60 elementary/secondary split** and the age distribution below. This is an **ASSUMPTION** to be replaced by the demand survey (v1 report §2) once run.

| Band | Ages (Ontario grade equivalent) | Students | Homeroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 Early | 4–7 (JK–Gr 2) | 7 | E1 |
| E2 Junior | 8–10 (Gr 3–5) | 7 | E2 |
| E3 Intermediate | 11–13 (Gr 6–8) | 6 | E3 |
| S1 Senior | 14–16 (Gr 9–11) | 10 | S1 |
| S2 Senior | 17–18 (Gr 12 / 12+) | 10 | S2 |
| S3 Transition | 19–21 (to June of the year the student turns 21) | 10 | S3 |
| **Total** | | **50** | **6 homerooms** |

Profile mix assumed for group-viability planning (ASSUMPTION, from HPEDSB category proportions and the v1 §9 suitability framework): about 55% IDD-type profiles, 30% MID-type, 15% autism or "Multiple" where the intellectual profile is the dominant educational need. Pathway distribution assumed: ~30% Formal (modified curriculum), ~55% Semi-formal (functional academics, alternative expectations), ~15% Informal/early-symbolic (emerging communication). Pre-formal/engagement-level learners are not in the opening population (section 8).

### 2.2 Why six homerooms of 6–10

- Elementary homerooms of 6–7 and secondary homerooms of 10 keep every group at or under the O. Reg. 298 DD maximum of 10 (reference only) and give **1 teacher + 1–2 EAs per homeroom**, i.e., 1:2.3 to 1:3.5 adult:student by homeroom and **1:2.9 overall** across 17 instructional adults — inside the comparator range 1:2 (DDSB) to 1:3.3 (Oak Bridge) (v1 §8, VERIFIED).
- A three-year age span per elementary homeroom and a two-to-three-year span per secondary homeroom respects the brief's chronological-age principle and keeps the EEF peer-tutoring guidance (age gap under three years) satisfiable *within* a homeroom (v1 §2, VERIFIED).
- Lean variant (sensitivity for the finance model, PROPOSAL): merge E2 and E3 into one 13-student homeroom (ages 8–13) with 1 teacher + 2 EAs, saving 1.0 teacher FTE. Acceptable only if the merged group is mostly MID-type (Reg. 298 "mixed exceptionalities" reference of 16); not recommended as the base case because a 13-student span of ages 8–13 with IDD profiles exceeds every DD-class comparator.

### 2.3 Grouping by subject — pathways within bands

Homerooms are the social, pastoral and privacy unit. Instruction re-groups within the band (elementary band = E1–E3, 20 students; secondary band = S1–S3, 30 students). Groups are re-set each term from the assessment data in section 7; movement between pathways is expected and recorded in the IEP.

| Block | Grouping rule | Group size and adults | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Literacy (daily) | By current reading/communication skill within the band, ignoring homeroom; three pathways (Formal: phonics + comprehension; Semi-formal: phonics + sight words + functional text; Informal: early symbolic, core-vocabulary AAC, shared reading) | 1–4 students per adult; elementary band runs 6–7 concurrent groups with 3 teachers + 3–4 EAs; secondary band 8–9 groups with 3 teachers + 5–6 EAs. EAs deliver scripted, evidence-based programs under the teacher's plan; teachers take the groups with the newest or hardest content. | Allor et al. 2014 groups of 1–4, 40–50 min daily (v1 §2, VERIFIED); EEF: TAs deliver structured interventions only with training and evidence-based materials (VERIFIED) |
| Numeracy (daily) | By current numeracy skill; often a different group than literacy | Same ratios; systematic instruction, time delay, task analysis | Hudson, Rivera & Grady 2018 (v1 §2, VERIFIED) |
| Communication & social (daily) | By communication mode and social goal (AAC users together for part of the week; mixed for generalization) | Groups of 3–6; SLP-designed, EA/teacher-delivered | SLP consultation model (v1 §8) |
| Practical blocks (kitchen, garden, market, projects) | By interest and social compatibility within the band; cross-band only in scheduled shared activities (section 6) | Kitchen ≤1:3 when heat/knives in use; garden/craft ~1:4–5 with two adults minimum; market/café 1:3–4 | v1 §4 (CLEAPSS P183, VERIFIED; others descriptive) |
| Community & work (secondary) | By independence level and placement readiness | 1:3–4 off-site; 1:1 job coaching "when necessary" | Project SEARCH 10:1:2; Oakfield Park (v1 §4, §6, VERIFIED) |

## 3. Daily and weekly timetable skeleton

### 3.1 Two cohorts, equal instructional time

**FOUNDER DECISION:** two staggered start/end times are acceptable. **PROPOSAL:** Cohort A runs **8:30–2:30**, Cohort B **9:15–3:15**. Each school day is 360 minutes; each cohort has the same block structure shifted by 45 minutes, so instructional minutes are identical. Which students are in which cohort is a transport decision (routes agent): the default assignment is **by band** (secondary = Cohort A, elementary = Cohort B) because it gives teens an afternoon window for placements and transit training and gives young children a later start; if routes require assignment **by geography** instead, every homeroom simply runs its own cohort's clock and the design still holds, because all shared activity sits in the **overlap window 9:15–2:30**.

| Cohort A (8:30–2:30) | Cohort B (9:15–3:15) | Block | Minutes | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8:30–8:45 | 9:15–9:30 | Arrival routine: coats, communication check-in, schedule review, morning jobs | 15 | Functional routine (instructional for this population) |
| 8:45–9:35 | 9:30–10:20 | Literacy — pathway groups | 50 | Direct instruction |
| 9:35–10:20 | 10:20–11:05 | Numeracy — pathway groups | 45 | Direct instruction |
| 10:20–10:35 | 11:05–11:20 | Break (snack, outdoor) | 15 | Break |
| 10:35–11:05 | 11:20–11:50 | Communication & social skills | 30 | Direct instruction |
| 11:05–11:45 | 11:50–12:30* | Flexible block (independence/self-care skills, music, second numeracy practice, or market prep) | 40 | Instruction |
| 11:45–12:25 | 12:30–1:10* | Lunch (shared dining room; lunch clubs on scheduled days) | 40 | Break |
| 12:25–12:55 | 1:10–1:40 | Movement / PE / wellness | 30 | Instruction |
| 12:55–1:40 | 1:40–2:25 | Practical block 1 (kitchen Mon/Thu; garden Tue/Fri; projects Wed) | 45 | Practical |
| 1:40–2:15 | 2:25–3:00 | Practical block 2 (arts, clubs, community, projects) | 35 | Practical |
| 2:15–2:30 | 3:00–3:15 | Closing routine: review, self-assessment, communication book, dismissal | 15 | Functional routine |

\* If both cohorts should share lunch for belonging reasons, Cohort B swaps its flexible block and lunch so both eat 11:45–12:25; kitchen and dining capacity then set the limit (premises agent).

**Instructional minutes per cohort: 275 timetabled instruction + 30 functional-routine minutes = 305 minutes; 55 minutes of breaks.** This is comparable to the Ontario public-school day of five hours (300 minutes) of instruction excluding recesses (O. Reg. 298 s.3 — **SECONDARY/CONFIRM**: regulation text not read; the five-hour rule is widely quoted and should be confirmed on e-Laws before the figure is used externally). Neither cohort loses minutes to the stagger.

Staff shifts (PROPOSAL, for the finance and transport agents): Cohort A homeroom staff 8:00–3:00; Cohort B staff 8:45–3:45; principal, float EA, admin and specialists span 8:00–3:45. A 7.0-hour paid day for EAs with 30 minutes before and after students; teacher prep and meetings inside the same window plus one weekly after-hours staff meeting. Volunteers and placements are scheduled into the 9:15–2:30 overlap so one briefing covers both cohorts.

ESTIMATE: Allor's benchmark (40–50 min daily reading in groups of 1–4 for four years) is met by the 50-minute literacy block for elementary and the 45-minute block for S1–S2; S3's 30-minute functional literacy block is a deliberate shift toward community time, matching Storefront's "five mornings in placements" design (v1 §3, VERIFIED).

Teacher-led direct instruction therefore occupies roughly 40% of the elementary and senior week, practical and community learning roughly 30–45%, and movement/routines the remainder. The founder's requirement of "substantial direct instruction" is met without reducing practical blocks below the comparator pattern.

## 4. Teen-specific programming, cross-age rules, privacy and dignity

### 4.1 Teen programming (S1–S3)

- **Distinct secondary identity:** own wing or floor, own lounge/common room, own washrooms and change facilities, student council, graduation ceremony, prom-style social, sports through Special Olympics Ontario-type community programs (partner to be identified — no outreach yet). Comparators that keep a separate post-16/secondary department: Ash Lea, Hazel Court, Bettridge, Summit WOTP, Halton CPP (v1 §3, VERIFIED).
- **Curriculum components not offered to elementary:** work-related learning and in-school enterprise from 14; community placements from 16 (graduated: in-school enterprise → supported community placement → more independent placement, mirroring Storefront's entry requirement of two prior successful placements, v1 §6 VERIFIED); travel training on Belleville Transit and Quinte Access; personal finance and phone/online safety; relationships, consent and sexual health using Ontario HPE expectations adapted to alternative-expectation format; self-advocacy and person-directed planning (section 5.3).
- **Transition planning from 14** (PPM 156 requires a transition plan for every IEP; tri-ministry integrated planning for DD begins at 14 — v1 §6, VERIFIED). The school adopts the same age even though PPM 156 binds boards, not private schools (SECONDARY — applicability to private schools not confirmed; adopted as practice).

### 4.2 Cross-age mentoring rules (PROPOSAL; no published policy found in v1 §3)

1. Tutoring pairs have an **age gap of three years or less** (EEF, VERIFIED) and are **reciprocal** — each student is tutor in one skill and tutee in another over a term.
2. Tutoring is **trained, scripted and time-limited** (4–5 sessions/week for up to 10 weeks, consolidation tasks only — EEF, VERIFIED), with an adult in the room.
3. **Students are never responsible for another student's safety, toileting, feeding, medication, mobility or behaviour.** Adult:student ratios are calculated without counting students. This is written policy, trained annually, and reflected in every risk assessment.
4. Cross-band contact (elementary with secondary) happens only in scheduled shared activities — market day, garden, lunch clubs, assemblies, performances — never in unsupervised or care contexts, and older students do not enter elementary washrooms or change areas.
5. Adult mentors for teens are screened volunteers (vulnerable-sector check, two-adult rule) and workplace mentors at placements; mentoring by students with ID of younger students with ID is an enrichment, not a support model (EEF TA principle that helpers "supplement, not replace", v1 §3, VERIFIED).

### 4.3 Privacy and dignity (PROPOSAL)

- Personal care only by trained paid staff (never volunteers, placements or students), two-adult awareness protocol (second adult knows where and when; door-ajar or window where safe), same-gender staff where the student or family prefers, and an individual **personal care plan** with the student's own consent language and the family's instructions.
- **Separate change and washroom facilities by band**; at least one fully accessible washroom with an adult-sized height-adjustable change table and privacy screen for the secondary band (Changing Places-type specification — premises agent to cost; this is a cost driver named in section 8). A JK-aged child and a 20-year-old must never share a change space.
- Toileting schedules and continence support are recorded in the IEP/care plan and reviewed termly; language used with and about the student is age-appropriate.
- Teens have scheduled unstructured social time, phones under a written policy rather than a blanket ban, and privacy for health and relationship topics.
- Disability-related information is on a **need-to-know** basis inside the school (OHRC: "inappropriately disclosing a student's disability to people who do not need to know" is a dignity harm — VERIFIED, OHRC 2018 policy §8.1.1, accessed 2026-10-15).

## 5. Staffing structure

### 5.1 Principles

- **Paid core is sufficient on its own** to deliver every timetabled block safely on a day when no volunteer or placement student appears. Volunteers, placements and visiting clinicians add capacity; they are never counted toward supervision ratios in kitchen (heat/knives), community, personal-care or behaviour-support contexts (FOUNDER constraint: essential teaching and care stay reliable when volunteers are unavailable).
- Teachers: OCT certification is not legally required in a private school (v1 §5, VERIFIED, ontario.ca/page/private-schools-0, updated 2 Oct 2026). **PROPOSAL:** require OCT with Special Education Part 1 or higher for the principal and prefer it for homeroom teachers; the modified-expectations credit decision and IEP quality both rest on Ontario assessment literacy (v1 §5). EAs: college diploma (DSW, Educational Support or equivalent), first aid/CPR, non-violent crisis intervention training, vulnerable-sector check.
- Educational leadership is separate from executive leadership: a **Principal / Head of School** (pedagogy, IEPs, admissions, inspection-readiness, staff supervision) and an **Executive Director** (governance, funding, partnerships, finance, premises; founder initially — FOUNDER DECISION).

### 5.6 Staffing thresholds (rules the finance model can encode)

- **Homeroom cap:** 8 for a homeroom whose majority are IDD-type or autism-with-ID profiles under age 8; 10 for all other IDD-profile homerooms; 12 for a homeroom that is mostly MID-type (Reg. 298 reference values 6/10/12–16, VERIFIED via Ministry summary). The 9th/11th/13th student in a homeroom triggers a **new homeroom: +1.0 teacher, +2.0 EAs** (+1.0 EA if the new homeroom opens with ≤6 students and no continence needs).
- **Float EA:** +0.5 per 3 homerooms (0.5 at ≤3, 1.0 at 4–6, 1.5 at 7–9).
- **Second educational leader:** +1.0 at the 7th homeroom (~60 students) or when the principal's teaching load would exceed 0.2.
- **Transition coordinator:** 0.3 at ≤12 students aged 14+, 0.5 at 13–25, 1.0 at 26+.
- **Volunteer coordinator:** 0.5 at ≤30 students, 1.0 at 31–75; +0.5 above 50 active volunteers.
- **Contracted clinicians:** SLP 0.1 FTE per 12 students (round to 0.1); OT and BCBA 0.05 per 12 students.
- **Any admitted student needing continuous 1:1, delegated nursing, two-person behaviour response or a pre-formal pathway** triggers the section 8 resources *before* admission, not after.

## 6. Secondary pathways and the 18–21 bridge

- **Base track — Certificate of Accomplishment, non-credit** (v1 §5, VERIFIED mechanics): K-coded alternative courses built from the IEP; alternative report card; an OSR-equivalent transcript maintained from Grade 9 so the certificate "accompanied by the Ontario Student Transcript" has content. Whether a non-inspected private school may issue the Ministry's certificate is OQ-4 (CONFIRM); if not, the school issues its own completion certificate with transcript and IEP, as Storefront does.
- **Optional credit route** (PROPOSAL, brief): for MID-type students who can profit from modified expectations, the OSSC (14 credits) is the realistic credential and requires Ministry inspection of the secondary program. Educational implications only: at least one OCT teacher per credit subject area, 110 scheduled hours per credit, Learning Strategies and co-op courses as the backbone, principal's documented credit decisions on modified expectations. Recommend **not** at opening; revisit after two years when the inspected-school route (legal file) and demand from MID families are both clear. Families of MID students who want credits may be better served by HPEDSB's CIP in the meantime, and the admissions file says so plainly.
- **Transition planning from 14** (section 4.1), **exit specification by 18** (v1 §6, VERIFIED timeline): current psychological assessment by 16; DSO application at 16 with school-supplied IEP and plan; ODSP at 17.5; graduated work experience; travel training; person-directed plan.
- **18–21 programming (S3)**: three mornings a week in community placements (Storefront pattern), in-school enterprise, functional academics, adult-services preparation. **Note only** (public-services agent owns funding): the activities map to Passport-eligible categories — community participation, skills development, person-directed planning (v1 §6, VERIFIED guidelines) — but Passport funds are the adult's, directed by the family, and private-school tuition is not an eligible expense; the school does not design S3 as a Passport-billed day program. Whether a non-school "day support" component could ever be a separately funded service alongside school attendance is for the public-services agent (and is a government question below).

## 7. Assessing progress and belonging

**Honest premise:** the v1 syntheses do not show that specialized placement produces better academic or social outcomes (VERIFIED: Dalgaard et al. 2022; Dell'Anna et al. 2020; Fuchs et al. 2025). The school therefore measures its own students against their own baselines and against published expectations, publishes aggregate results annually, and treats a student who is not progressing as a reason to change the program or the placement, not to re-describe the goal. Reporting follows PPR 2024 s.3.4.2 ("a statement of the methods by which the student's progress will be assessed" in every IEP — VERIFIED, v1 §5).
