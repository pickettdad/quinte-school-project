# Financing and local fundraising capacity

**Status:** Phase 2 research note, 2026-10-15. Desk research only; no outreach. Labels: **VERIFIED** (primary source read, link and access date), **SECONDARY** (reputable commentary), **ESTIMATE** (basis and formula shown), **PROPOSAL** (recommendation, not approved), **ASSUMPTION** (planning assumption), **FOUNDER DECISION**, **CONFIRM** (needs a named authority). Builds on v1 notes (`reports/2026-10-14-v1-research-notes/funding_and_financing.md` KQ3, KQ6, KQ7 and `gap_funding_verification.md` §1); settled v1 items are cited, not re-researched.

**Framing (FOUNDER DECISION, brief 2026-10-15):** the main operating scenario assumes capital is donor-funded without debt; interest-free or forgivable financing is to be explored; borrowing must never conceal a recurring operating deficit; local fundraising capacity is to be investigated rather than inferred from a few charities.

---

## Summary

1. **Interest-free institutional money does not exist for a new charity.** Every lender reached (Community Forward Fund, Vancity Community Investment Bank, credit unions, Social Finance Fund intermediaries, community bonds) charges interest, requires 1–2 years of operating history, and/or needs a repayment stream that a tuition-free school does not have. Infrastructure Ontario's eligible-borrower list excludes schools and general charities (VERIFIED). The only genuinely interest-free capital is **philanthropic debt** — donor loans, forgivable loans and donor guarantees — documented by agreement. Municipal loans are a documented local precedent (Humane Society) but are political and repayable.
2. **The region's receipted-plus-fundraising philanthropic flow is on the order of $35–50M a year (ESTIMATE),** triangulated from 33 FY2023 T3010 returns pulled through the CRA open-data API ($25.6M in the sample alone) and from Fraser Institute/StatCan donor rates. Health foundations take ≈ $12M, congregations and faith schools ≈ $15–20M, social/community charities ≈ $7–10M.
3. **A $1.0–1.5M annual operating ask is 2–4 % of that flow** — the scale of the Quinte Humane Society ($1.0M) or Trenton Memorial Hospital Foundation ($1.6M), each of which took decades to build. Reaching it within five years is **plausible only with** a development lead, signed multi-year pledges at the top of the pyramid, and the founder's network converting into 10–20 gifts of $20k+ a year.
4. **The region does contain "transformational" capacity.** One Belleville private foundation granted $5.6M in FY2023 from $109M of assets; a second granted $347k; two bequests of $1.45M seeded the Humane Society's $5M building; a single $750k foundation gift named a hospital suite. The anchor hypothesis is therefore not fanciful — but it rests on three to five relationships and must be treated as a **gate** (secured before lease and hiring), not a forecast.
5. **Anchor and broad base are complements, not alternatives.** Every comparable community-built institution found (Humane Society, hospice, Picton hospital) combined a lead gift or bequest with a broad campaign and municipal support, over 3–5 years. No comparable was found of a new local institution raising $1M+/yr of *operating* philanthropy without government or earned core revenue.

## A. Interest-free and forgivable financing options

### A.0 Ground rules that apply to every option

- **Loans are not revenue.** Loan proceeds appear in a capital schedule; repayments are an operating expense (v1 F&F KQ6 non-double-counting rule).
- **No personal guarantees.** New organizations are routinely asked for directors' personal guarantees; the board policy should be to refuse them (v1 F&F KQ6; PROPOSAL). Where a lender needs comfort, use a donor guarantee (A.3) instead.
- **Debt only for capital or timing,** never to fund a recurring deficit (§A.10).
- **CRA treatment.** A loan is not a gift. If a lender later forgives principal, the forgiven amount may be receiptable as a gift at the time of forgiveness; counsel must confirm the documentation (loan agreement with an explicit, unconditional forgiveness instrument) — **CONFIRM** (charity counsel; CRA Charities Directorate). The ITA s. 80.4 deemed-interest rules apply to shareholder/employee loans, not to a donor's interest-free loan to a charity — **CONFIRM** with counsel.

### A.1 Donor loan agreements (0 % interest) — PROPOSAL

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | Any charity; needs a board resolution and a written agreement |
| Uses | Capital (acquisition, renovation, equipment), pre-opening reserve, bridging pledges |
| Rate / fees | 0 %; legal drafting ≈ $2–5k (ESTIMATE) |
| Term | Typically 5–10 years; bullet or amortising |
| Security / guarantees | Usually unsecured and **subordinated** to any senior lender; no personal guarantees |
| Restrictions | Must not be treated as revenue; disclose in financial statements as a liability; related-party rules if the lender is a director (Ontario Not-for-Profit Corporations Act conflict-of-interest provisions — CONFIRM) |
| Repayment source | Capital-campaign pledges, bequests or a later sale of the asset — never the operating donor base already counted in the budget |
| Arithmetic | $500,000 at 0 % over 10 years = **$50,000/yr** of principal (v1). At 6 % the same loan costs ≈ $66,600/yr (monthly P&I ≈ $5,550; ESTIMATE). Each $100k borrowed at 0 % over 10 years adds $10k/yr to the fundraising requirement for a decade. |

### A.2 Forgivable loans — PROPOSAL

As A.1 but with forgiveness triggers written into the agreement (e.g., charitable registration obtained; 40+ students enrolled by a date; first audited year complete; building occupied). Until forgiven, the full principal is a liability; once forgiven it becomes a gift (receipting per A.0 CONFIRM). Advantage to the donor: the gift is only "spent" if the school reaches the milestone. Advantage to the school: the capital is in hand before the milestone. Disadvantage: auditors will present it as debt, which affects any other lender's view.

### A.3 Donor guarantee of a credit-union operating line — PROPOSAL

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | A donor (or several) signs a limited guarantee (e.g., $250k) supporting an operating line at a credit union; the school draws only to bridge pledge or grant timing |
| Cost | Prime + 1–3 % on drawn balances for weeks (v1 ESTIMATE: hundreds to low thousands per year); set-up fees |
| Security | The guarantee replaces directors' personal guarantees; the credit union may still take a general security agreement over assets |
| Receipting | A guarantee is not a gift; if called and paid, the payment may then be a gift (CONFIRM) |
| Repayment source | The specific pledge or grant being bridged |
| Lender appetite | Alterna Savings (which absorbed Quinte First Credit Union in 2020, so it holds the former local credit-union branches — SECONDARY, [Advisor.ca 2019-12-05](https://www.advisor.ca/industry-news/industry/alterna-savings-and-quinte-first-join-forces/)) lists "Community" banking with microfinance and granting programmes but its non-profit lending page could not be fetched — **CONFIRM** branch locations and non-profit line terms. Kawartha Credit Union's Quinte branch presence is unverified. Desjardins Ontario and FirstOntario have no documented Belleville presence (ASSUMPTION; not searched). FSRAO lists several closed-bond "Belleville … Credit Union Limited" entities whose current status was not checked. |

### A.8 Community bonds (Tapestry Community Capital) — VERIFIED, accessed 2026-10-15

- Eligibility: "Any charity, nonprofit, or cooperative with an engaged base of supporters and a solid financial model"; asset backing "can increase investor confidence" but is not required; issuers set their own rates and maturities; "An average community bond campaign engages 90 investors for every $1M raised"; platform total $150M across 24 issuers (Jan 2026); Tapestry's fees are not published ([Tapestry community bonds](https://tapestrycapital.ca/community-bonds/)).
- Education example: Kingsway College School (Toronto independent school, ~400 students) targeted $4M in bonds at **3 % over 5 years, $2,500 minimum**, plus $1M in donations, for a senior-school build; $2.7M pledged at time of writing ([Tapestry case](https://tapestrycapital.ca/using-community-bonds-to-change-the-landscape-of-education/); promotional, SECONDARY). KCS repays from tuition.
- Fit: a tuition-free school would repay bondholders from donations — the same dollars as the operating budget. A $2M issue at 3 % over 5 years requires ≈ $437k/yr of principal and interest (ESTIMATE: level amortisation, 2,000,000 × 0.03 ÷ (1 − 1.03⁻⁵)); a bullet structure defers the $2M to year 5, which simply moves the fundraising problem. **Only viable** for an asset with a resale or lease-income path, and only after a capital campaign has covered most of the cost. Treat as a bridge for the last 20–30 % of a building, not a funding source.

### A.9 Municipal loans and special grants — SECONDARY

The Humane Society Hastings Prince Edward's $5M (2021) / ≈ $6.5M (2019 estimate) care centre was financed by two bequests ($1.45M), a community campaign ($3.6M by Nov 2021), a $400,000 City of Belleville special grant plus >$200,000 in-kind, and **loans from the cities of Belleville and Quinte West** that its "Last Mile" $1M campaign (Dec 2022) was intended to repay ([Global News 2021-11](https://globalnews.ca/news/8367772/belleville-new-humane-society); [Global News 2022-12-22](https://globalnews.ca/news/9367264/anonymous-donor-belleville-humane-society-hastings-prince-edward); [Good News in Quinte, Issue 4](https://inquintenews.substack.com/p/good-news-in-quinte-issue-4-1228475); [CountyLive 2019-01-10](https://www.countylive.ca/quinte-humane-society-seeks-250000-from-county-toward-new-shelter/)). Loan amounts and interest terms were not retrieved. This is the only local precedent of municipal lending to a community capital project; it required a council decision in each municipality and a repayment campaign. **Speculative**; requires founder authorization before any approach.

### A.10 The rule: borrowing must never conceal a recurring operating deficit

**Test before any borrowing (PROPOSAL):** (1) the purpose is capital or timing, not operations; (2) the repayment source is identified and is *additional* to the donors already counted in the operating budget; (3) a three-year forecast **without** the loan balances; (4) the board minutes record all three.

**Arithmetic example (ESTIMATE; 50 students at ≈ $44k fully loaded = $2.2M, pending the finance model):**

| Year | Operating need | Philanthropic revenue | Deficit | Borrowed to cover | Cumulative debt | Repayment next year (0 %, 5-yr) | Next year's total need |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $2.20M | $1.80M | $0.40M | $0.40M | $0.40M | $0.08M | $2.28M |
| 2 | $2.28M | $1.80M | $0.48M | $0.48M | $0.88M | $0.176M | $2.38M |
| 3 | $2.38M | $1.80M | $0.58M | $0.58M | $1.46M | $0.29M | $2.49M |
| 4 | $2.49M | $1.80M | $0.69M | — | — | — | insolvent without a new $0.7M/yr source |

Even at 0 % interest, borrowing to cover a $400k structural gap grows the gap by ≈ 20 % a year because each loan adds its own repayment. The correct responses to a structural gap are enrolment/cost changes or confirmed revenue, never debt. By contrast, borrowing $400k to bridge a *signed* $400k pledge payable in 90 days is timing finance and costs only weeks of interest.

**Interest-free capital still creates an operating obligation:** $1M at 0 % over 10 years = $100k/yr ≈ 2.3 students' cost at $44k; it belongs in the fundraising requirement for the whole term.

### B.3 Scaling the sample to the region — ESTIMATE

- Unsampled congregations: ≈ 45 Belleville, ≈ 15 Trenton, ≈ 12 Picton charities in CRA category 0030 (places of worship) plus rural Hastings. Three sampled congregations ranged $87k–$1.14M. Assume ≈ 100 congregations × $150k median = **≈ $15M** (ASSUMPTION; sensitivity $10–20M).
- Other unsampled Belleville/Trenton/Picton charities (≈ 180: arts, cadets, legion funds, CMHA, Alzheimer Society, Habitat ReStore excluded, Quinte Health itself, Loyalist College, hospices' operating arms, smaller foundations): assume **≈ $3–5M** combined (ASSUMPTION).
- Rural Hastings (Bancroft, Madoc, Tweed, Stirling, Marmora) hospital and community foundations: not sampled; **≈ $1–3M** (ASSUMPTION; North Hastings Hospital Foundation would be the largest).
- **Bottom-up total ≈ $25.6M + $15M + $4M + $2M ≈ $45–47M**, range **$38–52M**.

**Top-down cross-check (ESTIMATE):** Hastings County ≈ 148,000 and Prince Edward County ≈ 25,700 (2021 Census; ASSUMPTION, not re-fetched) → ≈ 174,000 people; ≈ 78 % tax filers (ASSUMPTION) ≈ 135,000; 16.8 % claimed donations in 2023 (Fraser Institute Generosity Index, SECONDARY via [Wealth Professional, 2025-12-12](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/news/industry-news/wealth-grows-but-generosity-slows-as-fewer-canadians-claim-charitable-donations/391128)) → ≈ 22,700 donors; Ontario average claimed ≈ $2,600 (ASSUMPTION; national figures in the same index range $1,194–$3,622 by province) → ≈ **$59M claimed**, of which perhaps 50–65 % goes to charities in the region (the rest to national health charities, denominational bodies, universities, disaster appeals — ASSUMPTION) → **$30–38M receipted locally**, plus non-receipted events/fundraising ≈ 10–15 % → **$33–44M**.

**Conclusion (ESTIMATE):** total receipted-plus-fundraising philanthropic flow in Hastings–Prince Edward ≈ **$35–50M a year**, with ≈ $12M to hospital/hospice foundations (one-third of it in capital-campaign years), ≈ $15–20M to congregations and faith schools, and ≈ $7–10M to social, environmental and community charities. The number is dominated by a handful of institutions: six charities account for $15M of the $25.6M sample.

- A $1.0–1.5M/yr operating requirement is **2–4 % of the regional flow** and would rank the school among the five largest community-funded operations in Hastings–Prince Edward. The question is not whether the money exists but whether a *new* institution can divert or add that share.

The affinity pool is untested.

Every such campaign had a lead gift or bequest ≥ $0.75M. **Without one, $3M+ in three years is unlikely (ASSUMPTION).**

## C. Benchmarks: corporate commitments, monthly giving, gift pyramids, sponsorships, endowment

### C.1 Multi-year corporate commitments

- v1 KQ4 ESTIMATE stands: presenting-level $25–50k/yr from one or two anchor employers, $5–10k/yr from 5–10 mid-sized firms, $1–2.5k from 20–30 small businesses — ≈ $100–200k/yr when mature; count only signed pledges, discounted 10–20 % for non-fulfilment.
- Local evidence of how thin corporate budgets are spread: the Belleville Senators' entire 2024-25 community contribution was ≈ $305k across 28 raffle partners and ≈ 135 sponsored events (VERIFIED, [Sens impact report](https://bellevillesens.com/belleville-sens-release-2024-25-community-impact-report-with-305000-in-contributions/)); the 50/50 draw gave ≈ $7.3k per partner. Hospice Quinte's gala presenting sponsorship sits inside a $101k event (v1).
- Sector norm (SECONDARY, AFP/Imagine Canada practice; not fetched): corporate gifts are 3–5 % of Canadian charitable revenue; multi-year corporate pledges are typically 3 years and tied to recognition.

### C.2 Monthly giving

- CanadaHelps' Giving Report 2026 (9th edition, page modified 2026-05-12) highlights "the growing popularity of gifts of securities and monthly donations"; its figures sit in the PDF (not extracted) — [The Giving Report](https://www.canadahelps.org/en/the-giving-report/) (VERIFIED page; statistics CONFIRM).
- Arithmetic: 300 monthly donors × $50 = $180k/yr; 500 × $40 = $240k/yr. Platform fees ≈ 3–5 % (ASSUMPTION). Monthly donors retain at roughly 80–90 % a year versus ≈ 32 % for all donors (ASSUMPTION/SECONDARY; FEP, §D) — the single most effective protection against the Year 2–3 cliff.

### C.5 Named-programme sponsorships — PROPOSAL

Price at fair market value of recognition; non-receiptable where promotional value is material (v1 KQ4). Illustrative menu: kitchen/teaching-garden/transportation naming $10–50k/yr for three years; classroom naming $5–25k/yr; "sponsor a school day" (Hospice Quinte's "Sponsor a Day of Care" model, v1) at the daily operating cost (≈ $11k/day at $2.2M ÷ 194 days; ESTIMATE). Keep a board-approved naming and revocation policy. Never count a naming sponsorship in both the capital and operating plans (FOUNDER DECISION).

### C.6 Endowment realities

- 4 % prudent draw → **$25 of capital per $1 of annual income** (v1): $1.25M/yr requires ≈ $31M; one student at $44k requires ≈ $1.1M ("endow a seat"). Registered charities must meet CRA's disbursement quota (3.5 % on the first $1M of property not used in charitable activities, 5 % above — SECONDARY, CRA rules from 2023; CONFIRM current rates).
- Local reality check: the Parrott Foundation's $109M produced $3.8M of investment income in 2023 (3.5 %) — it is the only endowment of that scale in the region. The County Foundation's grants ($974k) rest on ≈ $8M of assets but include flow-through. Endowment for this school is a **10–20-year bequest strategy** (CAGP-style planned giving, SECONDARY), never an opening-plan line.

## D. Fundraising cost, staffing and donor retention

### D.1 Cost ratios (VERIFIED from T3010 line 5020 ÷ community philanthropy, §B.2)

Staffed local foundations run at 12–25 ¢ per dollar (Quinte Humane Society 11.7 %, United Way 17.5 %, BGH Foundation 18.8 %, Trenton Memorial 21.5 %, Hospice Quinte 25.2 %). Volunteer-led or capital-year foundations report 2–5 % (PECMH, Hospice PE). Two cautionary outliers: the Children's Foundation (44.9 %) and Albert College (76.2 % — an independent school whose fundraising office costs more than it raises in receipted gifts). CRA's comfort line is 35 % (v1, CG-013).

**Budget (PROPOSAL):** 30–35 % of philanthropic revenue in Years 1–3, falling to 18–22 % by Year 5. On $750k raised in Year 1 that is ≈ $225–260k of cost, shown as an expense line, never netted from donations.

### D.2 Development lead (ESTIMATE)

| Item | Year 1 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Development lead (salary $80–100k + 15–20 % benefits/EHT; see staffing stream S-S20/S-S21 for comparable salaries) | $95–120k | $100–125k |
| Part-time coordinator / database (0.5 FTE) | — | $30–40k |

| **Total** | **$135–200k** | **$190–265k** |

Hiring the development lead **before** opening (2027) is what makes the Year-1 pledge gate achievable; a founder-ED cannot run admissions, staffing and a $1M campaign simultaneously (PROPOSAL).

### D.3 Donor retention and the Year 2–3 cliff

- Fundraising Effectiveness Project, through Q3 2025 (US-based, SECONDARY via [NonProfit PRO, 2025-12-18](https://www.nonprofitpro.com/article/donor-losses-slow-as-fundraising-strength-holds-into-third-quarter/)): overall donor retention **31.9 %**; **new-donor retention 14 %**; repeat-donor retention 87.3 %; recaptured-donor retention 2 %; donors −3 %, dollars +3.7 % year over year. Dollar retention typically runs ≈ 45–50 % because large donors retain better (ASSUMPTION; FEP historical pattern).
- **Arithmetic (ESTIMATE):** Year 1 raises $1.0M from 700 donors, 600 of them new. Year 2 without acquisition: ≈ 84 new donors return (14 %) plus most repeat donors → dollar retention ≈ 45 % → **$450k**. To hold $1.0M, Year 2 must find $550k of new money. With the top 40 % of Year-1 revenue under signed three-year pledges: $400k committed + 45 % of the remaining $600k ($270k) + $350k new = **$1.02M**. Pledges at the top and monthly giving at the bottom are what flatten the cliff; events and one-off appeals do not.
- Implication for the finance model (PROPOSAL): model Year-2 philanthropic revenue as *pledged amount + 45 % of unpledged Year-1 revenue + new acquisition*, not as Year 1 plus growth.

## E. "Transformational anchor" (v1) versus a broad-based local campaign — candid assessment

**The v1 hypothesis:** the model is feasible only with a small number of very large committed donors or a capital/endowment gift (v1 KQ7).

**The founder's position (brief):** local fundraising capacity should be investigated, not inferred; the founder has substantial local business relationships; the largest gap is access to regional and national organizations.

**What the evidence shows**

| Comparable | Community | What was raised | How | Years | Government core? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Humane Society Hastings Prince Edward care centre | Belleville / Quinte West | ≈ $5M building (2021 est.; ≈ $6.5M incl. land per 2019 plan) | Two bequests $1.45M (anchor) → community campaign to $3.6M (Nov 2021) → $400k City grant + $200k in-kind → municipal loans → $1M "Last Mile" with $100k anonymous match (Dec 2022) | ≈ 2019–2023 | No (municipal animal-control contracts only) |

| Prince Edward County Memorial Hospital "Back the Build" | PEC (pop. ≈ 25,700) | $15.7M of $16.5M by June 2021 | Parrott Foundation $750k (named suite), Wellington Rotary $409k over 5 years, broad campaign; hospital affinity | ≈ 2019–2024 | Yes (province builds the hospital; community share) |

**Assessment (PROPOSAL)**

1. **Both halves of the v1 hypothesis are supported, but the "either/or" framing is wrong.** Every community-built institution in the region combined (a) one or two lead gifts or bequests of $0.75–1.5M, (b) a broad campaign of several hundred donors, and (c) municipal grants or loans for the tail, over 3–5 years. None did it with the anchor alone or the broad base alone.
2. **The anchor is for capital and reserve; the broad base is for operations.** Capital campaigns in this region reach $3.6–5M (Belleville) and $16M (Picton hospital) because they are finite and nameable. Operating philanthropy for a free school must recur, and the observed ceiling for a community-funded operation without government core revenue is ≈ $1.0M/yr (Humane Society) after decades. The plan should therefore use anchor gifts to buy the building and an 18–24-month reserve (v1 KQ7 gate) and use the broad base — plus the Year 2–3 institutional window (United Way, Azrieli, programme grants) — for operations.
3. **The founder's local relationships change the probability, not the arithmetic.** They make the $5k–$50k tiers of §C.3 reachable quickly (where most local charities struggle) and provide the introductions that the Parrott-scale and Rollins-scale foundations require. They do not change the fact that ≈ 7 gifts of $40k+ are needed every year, or that the disability affinity community has never been asked locally.
4. **The access gap to regional/national funders is real but smaller than the operating gap.** Of the national and corporate prospects in the register, only Azrieli (neurodevelopment priority, LOI process) and programme-specific funders (TD FEF, Jumpstart, Bell Let's Talk, EAF capital) are plausible, and together they would cover perhaps 5–10 % of a $2.2M budget in Year 3 (ESTIMATE). Regional and national money complements but does not replace local money.
5. **What would falsify the plan:** if, after a confidential feasibility study of 25–40 top prospects (standard practice before a capital campaign; PROPOSAL, 2027), fewer than two prospects rate at $500k+ and fewer than ten at $50k+, the 50-student opening should be re-phased or the capital assumption (no debt) revisited — not patched with borrowing.
