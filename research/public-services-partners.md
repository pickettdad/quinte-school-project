**Status:** Phase 2 research note, 2026-10-15. Desk research only; no outreach. Labels follow `CLAUDE.md` (VERIFIED / SECONDARY / ESTIMATE / PROPOSAL / ASSUMPTION / CONFIRM). All ontario.ca, ontariohealthathome.ca, passportfunding.ca and canada.ca pages cited were fetched on 2026-10-15 unless stated.
**Builds on:** v1 notes `reports/2026-10-14-v1-research-notes/funding_and_financing.md` (section 2, family programs), `legal_regulatory.md` (section 6, health; section 7, food/SNP) and `gap_legal_zoning_verification.md` (section 7, OHaH). SSAH, OAP, Passport, ACSD and ODSP amounts are **settled in v1** and are cited, not re-researched.
**Design frame (brief, "Public and insured services"):** 50 students, elementary and secondary (including some 18–21-year-olds), tuition-free, Belleville hub. The cohort is defined as students who can take part in small-group instruction; continuous 1:1 and complex medical needs are *not* the opening design (brief, "Students").

## 0. Two rules that govern everything below

1. **Restricted service funding is not operating revenue.** Every public or insured dollar examined here is attached to a *named student* and a *defined service*, is payable only to a *qualified provider*, and is *elective for the family*. It can reduce specific school costs (a contracted therapist hour, a nursing visit, a breakfast) but it cannot pay teachers, rent, utilities or administration, and it must never be budgeted as if it will arrive regardless of who enrols. The consolidated table (section 9) keeps "avoided school cost" strictly separate from "unrestricted operating revenue", which for every item in this note is **$0**.
2. **Access is independent of admission.** No family's benefits, SSAH/OAP/Passport allocation or OHaH eligibility may influence admission, placement or the core program a student receives (brief: "unrelated to admission, with access preserved for uninsured families"). The school never counts unused benefits as revenue and never asks families to "spend down" allocations with it. [FOUNDER DECISION, brief]

## 1. Ontario Health atHome — School Health Support Services for private-school students

### 1.1 Policy basis

- **PPM 81, "Provision of health support services in school settings" (19 July 1984, still in force "until revoked or modified"; the 1988 Interministerial Guidelines superseded only its speech/language parts).** It divides health support in schools three ways: *school boards* administer oral medication under parent/physician authorization and, for physically disabled pupils, provide lifting/positioning, mobility help, feeding, toileting and maintenance exercises (via aides), plus speech remediation; the *Ministry of Health (Home Care Program)* assesses needs and provides injections, catheterization, manual bladder expression, stoma care, postural drainage, suctioning, tube feeding, "intensive" physio-/occupational/speech therapy, and helps boards train and direct staff; *MCSS* covers residential/treatment facilities. PPM 81 is addressed to boards and says nothing about private schools. — [PPM 81](https://www.ontario.ca/document/education-ontario-policy-and-program-direction/policyprogram-memorandum-81) **VERIFIED** 2026-10-15.
- **Successor delivery.** The Home Care Program became CCAC School Health Support Services, then LHIN Home and Community Care, and since 2024 **Ontario Health atHome (OHaH)**, under the *Connecting Care Act, 2019*. The regulation that defines "school health professional services" and "school health personal support services" (formerly O. Reg. 386/99 under the Home Care and Community Services Act; now carried in O. Reg. 187/22) could not be read on e-Laws in this environment — **CONFIRM** wording with OHaH South East. **SECONDARY** (regulatory lineage from sector knowledge; not re-verified).
- **School-based rehabilitation (PT/OT/SLP) for *publicly funded* schools moved to Children's Treatment Centres** (MCCSS "School-Based Rehabilitation Services"). The ontario.ca children's rehabilitation page states CTCs provide PT, OT and SLP in schools for "students enrolled in Ontario public schools", up to age 19 or 21 if still in school, no referral required; **Quinte Children's Treatment Centre (QCTC)** is the listed CTC for Belleville and Trenton. The page does not address private or home-schooled students. — [Children's rehabilitation services](https://www.ontario.ca/page/childrens-rehabilitation-services) (updated 2026-07-24) **VERIFIED**. QCTC is part of Quinte Health, MCCSS-funded, free to families, runs the SmartStart Hub and Coordinated Service Planning; 613-969-7400 x2247. — [Quinte Health: QCTC](https://quintehealth.ca/care-service/quinte-childrens-treatment-centre/) (modified 2026-05-07) **VERIFIED**.

**Restrictions that matter:** (a) OHaH decides eligibility and hours, not the school; (b) services are per child and end when the need or enrolment ends; (c) OHaH will not supply a general-duty nurse, "classroom PSW" or therapy for the whole class; (d) the school remains responsible for supervision, safety, EAs, daily program implementation, equipment not supplied by OHaH, and the referral/coordination workload; (e) the school must have the "physical features" the service needs — a design input for premises (accessible washroom with adult change table, lift, clinical space).

**Practical approval route (PROPOSAL):** (1) Once the school is a registered private school (Notice of Intention filed), have the principal/special-education lead set up as referrer with OHaH South East (1-833-515-1234; Belleville office); (2) at admission, with consent, screen for OHaH-eligible needs and refer before September so care plans exist on day one; (3) agree a written protocol with OHaH on space, scheduling, staff-delegation training and communication; (4) record every care plan's hours in the staffing model as *assigned hours*, not as replacement of EA posts. No authorization for outreach exists yet — steps (1)–(3) are for after the founder authorizes contact.

## 2. Family health benefits (employer group plans) — voluntary, admission-independent

### 2.1 What plans cover

- **Coverage prevalence.** CLHIA reports life and health insurers' health benefits "cover 27 million Canadians" (2024 data; $53.3 billion in health benefits paid; paramedical claims including mental health up 16% in 2024). Against a population of ~41.5 million that is ~65% — overwhelmingly through employer-sponsored group plans. — [CLHIA release, 2025-09-23](https://www.clhia.ca/media-and-publications/news-releases/2025/Claims-in-Canada-rising-143-billion-paid-to-help-keep-Canadians-healthy-and-financially-secure) **VERIFIED** (27 million); population denominator **ESTIMATE**.
- **Practitioners and maximums.** Paramedical coverage typically lists physiotherapists, psychologists, psychotherapists, social workers, speech therapists, chiropractors, massage therapists (and often occupational therapists), each required to be "licensed, registered or certified"; some categories require a physician/NP referral. Example plan (Carleton University CUPE 2424, July 2024 at-a-glance): 80% reimbursement; speech therapists $200/practitioner/year; social worker/psychologist/psychotherapist with referral; no OT listed. — [CUPE 2424 benefits at a glance](https://carleton.ca/hr/wp-content/uploads/sites/16/2018/05/CUPE2424-Active-Benefits-at-a-Glance-July-2026.pdf) **VERIFIED** (one plan; illustrative only). Aon survey reported by Benefits Canada: nearly all surveyed employers cover psychology; 73% give psychology its own maximum; the most common psychology maximum is **$500–$1,000/year** ($1,000–$5,000 in financial services/tech). — [Benefits Canada](https://benefitscanada.com/?p=67936) **SECONDARY** (article undated in fetch). Planning range used here: **$300–$1,500 per practitioner category per year**, with SLP/OT frequently at the low end or absent. **ESTIMATE**.
- **RBT/ABA.** Rarely covered by standard plans; some larger employers add ABA/autism riders. Treat as $0. **ASSUMPTION**.
- **Where the service is delivered.** Group plans pay for an eligible service by an eligible practitioner; the physical location (clinic, home, school) is generally not a plan condition, but some plans require a referral, cap per-visit amounts, or exclude services "available under a government plan". Insurer plan booklets vary; the Carleton example has no general government-plan exclusion. **SECONDARY / CONFIRM** with the specific insurers' plan booklets at the time (families hold these; the school never needs to see them).
- **Direct billing.** Many psychologists, SLPs, OTs and physiotherapists direct-bill major carriers through TELUS Health eClaims or similar; eligibility is the practitioner's, not the location's. **SECONDARY**.
- **No double billing.** A session funded by OHaH or OAP cannot also be claimed to a group plan; practitioners are bound by their college's billing standards. Visiting clinicians must keep separate funding streams per session. **VERIFIED in principle** (college standards; OAP guideline "coordinate to avoid duplication").

### 2.2 The model (PROPOSAL)

- The school hosts **visiting registered clinicians** (psychologist/psychological associate, MSW/RSW, SLP, OT, physiotherapist) who run their own practices. The school provides a room and a schedule; families book and pay the practitioner directly (direct-billed or reimbursed by their plan); the school is never the payee and never invoices a plan.
- **Equal core program.** Every student gets the school's own communication, OT-informed and mental-health supports through staff and contracted clinicians, regardless of insurance. Benefit-funded sessions are *additional* individual therapy that families would otherwise seek downtown after school.
- **No revenue, no steering.** The school does not count, estimate per family, or budget any benefit dollars; it does not ask about coverage in admissions; a family that stops using coverage loses nothing. Clinicians see students on school time only with the principal's agreement that the pull-out does not harm instruction.
- **Uninsured families.** Where a benefit-funded clinician has a sliding scale or a pro-bono slot, that is the clinician's choice; the school may also fundraise a small restricted "therapy access fund" so uninsured students can access the same visiting clinicians — that fund *is* a school cost (restricted donation), not avoided cost.

### 2.3 Scale at 50 students (service value, not revenue) — ESTIMATE

- Families with group coverage: general population ~65%; parents of children with disabilities have lower full-time employment, so assume **45–60%** → 22–30 families. **ASSUMPTION**.
- Families who elect to use coverage at school: half of those → 11–15 (conservative) to 20–25 (moderate).
- Annual value per using family: conservative $600 (psychology $500 + small SLP/OT); moderate $1,200.
- **Service value: ~$7,000–$9,000 (conservative) to ~$24,000–$30,000 (moderate) per year**, delivered by clinicians to students, never passing through the school.
- **Avoided school cost: $0 (conservative) to ~$10,000 (moderate)** — only if the school would otherwise have paid for individual counselling/therapy sessions beyond its core clinical contracts (the v1 model's psychologist hours are for assessment and consultation, which plans generally do not fund).

## 3. Ontario Autism Program — a funding source for defined services, not tuition

Settled in v1 (cite, do not re-research): core clinical services allocations by age/intensity ($6,600–$65,000/yr); tuition, tutoring, respite and day-care fees ineligible; eligible services are ABA, SLP, OT, mental health, and recommended technology/materials; childhood-budget legacy rules allowed "specialized autism school services … autism-specific, individualized and ABA-based" delivered outside public schools. — v1 `funding_and_financing.md` §2 (ontario.ca pages verified 2026-09-10 and 2026-03-10).

**Provider-side requirements (ontario.ca, updated 2026-09-10, fetched 2026-10-15) — VERIFIED:**
- ABA must be delivered and/or supervised by a behaviour analyst, psychologist or psychological associate registered and in good standing with the **College of Psychologists and Behaviour Analysts of Ontario**; SLP by a member of CASLPO; OT by a member of COTO; mental-health services (counselling/psychotherapy) by regulated professionals with psychotherapy in scope (physicians, psychologists, psychotherapists, social workers, OTs, nurses).
- Services may be "virtually, at home, and/or in the community"; **school settings are not named** for core clinical services. Fees must be agreed in advance; families submit expense forms; third-party budget-management fees and cancellation fees are ineligible; services by a primary caregiver or by a minor are ineligible. The page does not say whether organizations (as opposed to individuals) are providers — in practice the regulated professional is the accountable provider and an organization may employ them. — [OAP core clinical services guidelines](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-autism-program-guidelines-core-clinical-services-and-supports) **VERIFIED**.
- **Entry to School** is a separate, free, lead-agency program for children aged 3–6 entering kindergarten/Grade 1 for the first time (6-month group program then individual transition support and educator consultation for the first six months in school). East-region lead is **ACT Learning Centre**, with partners including North Hastings Children's Services; the page does not say whether private-school entrants are supported. — [OAP entry to school program](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-autism-program-entry-school-program) (updated 2026-09-29) **VERIFIED**; private-school inclusion **CONFIRM** with ACT Learning Centre/MCCSS.
- Caregiver-mediated early years and foundational family services are delivered by designated lead agencies; a new school is not a candidate lead agency. **SECONDARY**.

**What the school would need to become (PROPOSAL):** not a "registered OAP provider" (no registry for organizations exists for core clinical services), but an employer/contractor of college-registered clinicians (BCBA/psychologist for ABA; SLP; OT) who provide **individual, clinically planned sessions** that a family may elect to purchase with their OAP allocation. The school's core program is never sold; group instruction, classroom ABA-informed practice and "autism-specific school services" are **not** billable under core clinical services (tuition/tutoring exclusion).

**Realistic limits and scale (ESTIMATE):** IDD cohorts commonly include a substantial autistic share; assume 15–20 of 50 students are OAP-registered. Families typically already have community providers and their allocations are small at older ages ($6,600–$18,800 "limited/moderate" for ages 10–17). Assume 5 students × $3,000 (conservative) to 10 × $6,000 (moderate) of **individual** clinician sessions delivered at school = **$15,000–$60,000 restricted service funding**, offsetting **$10,000–$40,000** of the school's contracted BCBA/SLP hours that would otherwise be used for the same individual work (the rest is incremental service, not avoided cost). Whether a school is an acceptable "community" setting for core clinical services is a **CONFIRM** (AccessOAP / MCCSS).

## 4. Passport — students aged 18–21

Settled in v1: Passport covers adults 18+ with a DSO-confirmed developmental disability who "need support to participate in the community while still in school"; minimum **$5,665/yr**, maximum **$45,604/yr**; reimbursement model; admissible uses include community participation, recreational/educational courses that are not OSAP-eligible, daily-living/life-skills programs, pre-employment supports, support-worker hours, transport to admissible activities; inadmissible: OSAP-eligible tuition, therapies, medical, housekeeping; supports may be bought from "community service providers, developmental services agencies, private providers, and adult education providers"; a ministry-funded Transfer Payment Recipient may invoice Passport directly. — v1 `funding_and_financing.md` §2 (ontario.ca Passport page verified 2026-09-10; Passport Guidelines April 2023). A funding increase took effect 2026-08-17 (amount communicated per recipient). — [passportfunding.ca](https://www.passportfunding.ca/) **VERIFIED**.

**Mechanics (VERIFIED 2026-10-15):**
- Passport for Hastings and Prince Edward is administered by the **DSO South Eastern Ontario Region Passport agency, operated by Extend-A-Family Kingston** (361 Montreal St, Kingston; 1-855-237-6737; passportinfo@eafkingston.com). — [Find your Passport agency](https://www.passportfunding.ca/find-your-passport-agency/); [DSO South Eastern Ontario Region](https://passportfunding.ca/agencies/dso-south-eastern-ontario-region).
- Claims: self-administering recipients (the person, caregiver or designated "Person Managing Funds") file through **eCLAIM or MyDirectPlan**, or a paper **Purchase of Service (POS) Invoice Form** with receipts; claims for expenses from 1 April 2026 are due within 12 months after the fiscal year-end (31 March). Alternatively a **TPA/Service Agency or Broker** can pay providers, bill Passport directly and charge an administration fee of up to 10% per claim. There is **no provider registration** with PassportONE; the *recipient* claims, with the provider's invoice/receipt as proof. — [Filing claims](https://www.passportfunding.ca/filing-claims/) **VERIFIED**.

**How a community organization becomes "admissible" (PROPOSAL, built on the verified rules):** it does not register; it issues proper invoices for an *admissible activity* (daily-living/life-skills program, pre-employment program, community participation, an educational course that is not OSAP-eligible, or support-worker hours), with dates, hours, rate and a description that matches the Guidelines' categories, and it is not the recipient's unpaid primary caregiver. Becoming a ministry-funded TPR is a separate ministry decision (v1: no open intake; speculative).

**What could be billed from this school's 18–21 program:** the adult-cohort components that are genuinely community participation, pre-employment and daily-living skills (e.g., afternoon community placements, student-market enterprise skills, travel training, cooking-for-independence), **not** literacy/numeracy instruction. A life-skills "course" by an adult-education provider is admissible on the Guidelines' wording, but the school would be stretching the category if it rebadged schooling as a course — **CONFIRM** with the Passport agency what share of a school-day program it will accept.

**Scale (ESTIMATE):** assume 8–10 of 50 students are 18–21 and DSO-confirmed (v1 ratio). Conservative: 5 students × $3,000 = **$15,000**; moderate: 8 × $5,000 = **$40,000** (floor allocation $5,665 with ~60–90% directed to the school's admissible activities; some students have higher allocations). This is restricted service funding; it offsets the paid support-worker hours in the adult cohort's community component only.

## 5. Special Services at Home — extended-day, PA-day and summer programming

Settled in v1: SSAH is discretionary direct funding to families of children under 18 with a developmental and/or physical disability; eligible uses include respite ("daytime program respite, before/after school care, care during PA Days, March break and summer break"), social-skills programs, "extra lessons/tutoring", worker training; **ineligible: private-school tuition**; the guidelines "do not address school-day education programming or respite during regular school hours"; families may buy from an agency or have an agency manage the funding; amounts are unpublished and discretionary (families report low thousands — CONFIRM). — v1 `funding_and_financing.md` §2 (SSAH Guidelines verified 2026-04-01).

**What the school could legitimately offer and bill (PROPOSAL):** a separately staffed, separately priced **before-school (7:30–8:45), after-school (3:00–5:30), PA-day, March-break and summer program**, invoiced per hour or per day to families who choose to use SSAH (or OAP respite rules, or ACSD "parental relief" money, or no program at all — same price for everyone, with a bursary for families without funding). It must be optional, outside instructional hours, and run by named workers (SSAH pays for workers; invoices should show worker, dates, hours, rate).

**Restrictions and uncertainty:** school-hours respite is unresolved (ask the MCCSS Special Agreements Officer — see government questions); SSAH and OAP respite overlap (model one per student); allocations are small and families already use them for home respite. **Scale (ESTIMATE):** 10 families × $1,000 = **$10,000** (conservative) to 20 × $1,500 = **$30,000** (moderate) per year, which **only offsets the cost of the extended-day/summer program itself** — it does not reduce school-day costs and is zero if the school does not run such a program.

## 6. Partnerships with existing organizations (generic; no outreach)

"TPA" = Ministry transfer-payment agency. A TPA's ministry funding is tied to its own service agreement, targets and reporting; it cannot simply re-direct funded staff or dollars to a private school. What it *can* do is (a) serve the same individuals in its own programs (which may be scheduled to dovetail with the school day), (b) share space, training or procurement under a written agreement at cost or in kind, (c) host placements/co-op, and (d) deliver purchased services it is allowed to sell. Any subcontract that uses ministry-funded capacity for the school's benefit normally needs the funder's consent and would be visible in the TPA's reporting — **CONFIRM** per agency.

## 7. Student Nutrition Program (Ontario) and the National School Food Program

- **Private schools can be SNP sites.** The MCCSS 2026–27 service objectives for Student Nutrition state that sites "are identified by the local lead agency and can be school-based or community locations", and that program counts "include community locations, **private schools**, and federally funded schools" (the "# of schools" reporting measure excludes them, which confirms they exist as program sites). People served: Full-Day Kindergarten through completion of the OSSD. Meals must follow the 2020 SNP Nutrition Guidelines; providers must meet food-safety and procurement expectations. — [MCCSS 2026–27 service objectives: Student Nutrition](https://www.ontario.ca/document/2026-2027-mccss-service-objectives-children-community-supports/55) (2026-05-05) **VERIFIED**. The federal action plan's SNP "participating schools" count likewise excludes "private schools, community program locations and federally funded schools" — again confirming they are counted as program sites. — [NSFP Ontario Action Plan 2025–27](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/school-food/agreements/ontario-action-plan-2025.html) (2026-06-16) **VERIFIED**.
- **Local lead agency:** Hastings and Prince Edward Learning Foundation (**Food for Learning**), 156 Ann St, Belleville (v1, verified from ontario.ca). Food for Learning ran 134 programs at 70 school sites with ~60% of its resources from MCCSS (2025). — [Picton Gazette, 2025-03-26](https://pictongazette.ca/post/hunger-pangs) **SECONDARY**. Its own site could not be fetched this pass. Whether it will approve an independent-school site is **its decision under ministry guidelines** — **CONFIRM** (also a government question: Ministry policy on independent-school sites).
- **Funding levels:** federal NSFP contributes **$44.8M/yr to Ontario in 2025–26 and 2026–27** ($35.2M then $31.1M through the 13 SNP lead agencies, alongside $28.1M provincial), targeting ~175,000–200,000 additional students and ~250 additional schools per year; federal funding is legislated to become permanent at $216.6M/yr nationally from 2029–30. No per-meal or per-student rate is published; SNP money is a **partial contribution** to food costs that programs supplement with donations and volunteers. — NSFP action plan (above) **VERIFIED**; [Toronto Board of Health backgrounder 2026](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/hl/bgrd/backgroundfile-287069.pdf) **SECONDARY** (permanent federal amount; "standardized meal costs per student"); [canada.ca NSFP](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/school-food.html) **VERIFIED** ($1B over 5 years).
- **Conditions (from the service objectives and v1):** universal — open to all children at the site; nutrition guidelines; food safety (O. Reg. 493/17 food premise; certified food handler); volunteer/parent involvement; interim and final reporting via the lead agency; no fees to students.
- **Scale (ESTIMATE):** if the school serves breakfast and lunch to 50 students on 190 days at ~$6/day food cost = ~$57,000/yr, and lead-agency support covers 25–40% of food cost (ESTIMATE: historic provincial share widely reported around 15–20% before the federal top-up roughly doubled total funding — SECONDARY, not re-verified), SNP support would be **~$14,000 (conservative, ≈$1.50/student/day) to ~$24,000 (moderate, ≈$2.50/student/day)**. Per-student-per-day rates are the lead agency's to set — **CONFIRM**.

## 8. What classroom duties remain the school's responsibility under every scenario

Nothing above funds: teaching, EAs for instruction and supervision, behaviour support in the classroom, daily implementation of therapy and care plans, personal care outside PSW visit windows, equipment not supplied by OHaH/ADP, the referral/coordination workload (realistically 0.2–0.3 FTE of a special-education lead's time at 50 students — **ESTIMATE**), food preparation labour and kitchen compliance, or the extended-day program's own staffing. Public or insured services make the school *possible* for students with delegated-act needs and reduce specific line items; they do not change the staffing floor.
