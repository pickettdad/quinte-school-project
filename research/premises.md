## 0. Summary and ranked shortlist

**What changed since v1.** (1) The design target is now 50 students expandable to ~75 (**FOUNDER DECISION**, 2026-10-15), which needs roughly **1,300–1,500 m² at opening and ~1,800–2,100 m² at 75** (ESTIMATE, §5.1). The v1 lead candidate, the 4,557 sq ft (423 m²) church at 253 Dundas St W, therefore no longer fits on its own. (2) Belleville's Zoning By-law 2024-100 is now read in full: **"School" is a permitted main use only in the CF (Community Facility) zone (and the LC Loyalist College zone, which adopts CF rules); it is not listed in any residential, commercial (C1–C6) or mixed-use (MX1/MX2) table** (VERIFIED, §1.4). Every commercial or converted-commercial candidate therefore needs a zoning by-law amendment; every former school site and the 253 Dundas church do not. (3) Nothing new has been published by HPEDSB, the Ministry, the City or local media on the three surplus schools since the 7 October 2026 announcement (VERIFIED searches, §1.1). The Minister's 120-day window to name a priority recipient or release the properties runs to roughly **early February 2027**.

### 2.2 Inconsistencies inside the SIP (and between board documents)

1. **Renewal figures do not reconcile.** The itemised five-year renewal table sums to ≈ **$1.28M**, yet the FCI box states renewal costs of **$13.29M**. The itemised list is evidently a partial extract of the VFA database; the FCI figure is the Ministry's five-year assessed need. The Oct 2016 LTCAP gives QSS **FCI 25% and $7.7M**; the May 2017 ARC Final Report gives **44% / $13.3M** (five-year) and **$14.3M (2017–22)**; v1 cited a VFA 20-year forecast of **$21.6M**. The Ministry defines FCI as five-year renewal needs ÷ asset replacement value, inflation-adjusted with Statistics Canada's institutional construction price index, with boards free to choose other horizons (Ministry of Education technical paper, 2016, https://efis.fma.csc.gov.on.ca/faab/Memos/B2016/B15_ATTACH_EN_AODA.pdf, accessed 15 Oct 2026, **VERIFIED**). The same horizon problem explains Queen Victoria's 50% vs 72%. **Any current figure must state its horizon.**
2. **Cost per student.** $140,877.87 ÷ $284.02 = 496 students, but enrolment was 580 (FTE 563; 2015-16 FTE 568). The per-student figure is therefore computed on a different denominator (possibly average daily enrolment or a prior-year count). Use the per-m² figure only.
3. **Utilities savings claim.** The ARC Final Report estimated $250,000/yr of utilities savings from consolidating Moira and Quinte, which exceeds QSS's entire 2015-16 utility bill ($140,878) — the saving must include Moira-side or non-utility items. Treat the SIP figure as the only measured one.
4. **1968 addition** appears in the data table but no 1968 block is drawn on the site plan; the floor plans are not keyed to construction years. Wing-by-year areas must come from the board's drawings.
5. **Room 156** is "Greenhouse" on the floor plan but "Technical/Vocational" in the Ministry inventory; **Room 217** is listed twice; an "Exercise Room" has no room number ("missing").
6. **The Official Plan map on p.7 is the wrong map** — it shows Pine St / Bridge St E / MacDonald Ave / Herchimer / Farley (east Belleville, the Queen Victoria–Queen Elizabeth area), not College St W. Do not cite it for QSS.
7. **Zoning legend** calls CF "Commercial Facility"; the by-law term (then and now) is Community Facility.
8. **North arrows:** the floor plans carry an "N REF" arrow pointing down the page, the site plan has none, and the Google context map is rotated; orientation of wings (which wing faces College St W, which faces the fields) must be taken from survey drawings, not the SIP.

### 2.3 What a current building condition assessment must establish for QSS (specific)

- **Mothballing regime since June 2018:** heated or drained? Minimum temperature setpoint; whether the hot-water boilers (already flagged for replacement in 2023-24) have run eight more winters, or the building was winterised (drained plumbing, sprinklers if any, heating coils). A drained 1950s building suffers freeze damage, mould and envelope decay quickly; a heated one costs $50–80K/yr (§2.5). **CONFIRM (HPEDSB Facilities).**
- **Utilities connected?** Electrical service, gas, water/sewer accounts active; condition of the main switchgear ("electrical" was a 2023-24 renewal item) and the fire alarm panel (replacement listed for 2020-21 — done before closure?).
- **Roof:** "roof coverings" partially renewed in 2015-16; age and warranty of each roof section by wing; roof area over 11,239 m² (two-storey portion smaller) is a whole-building obligation that does not shrink with occupied area.
- **HVAC and controls:** "energy supply, cooling generating system, controls and instrumentation" were 2020-21 renewal items — whether done; whether heating is a single central boiler plant with building-wide distribution (most likely for a 1954–63 school) or zoned by wing; ventilation make-up air units per wing.
- **Designated substances:** ACM was removed in 2013-14 (scope unknown — likely limited to the lobby/floor-tile areas renovated). A 1954–1968 building must be presumed to contain asbestos (pipe insulation, floor tile, mastic, plaster, ceiling tile), lead paint, PCB ballasts and possibly mercury switches: a **designated-substances survey (O. Reg. 490/09 / OHSA s.30)** is mandatory before any renovation work — **CONFIRM scope with the board's existing asbestos register**.
- **Elevator:** TSSA licence status after eight years idle; modernisation cost if the licence lapsed.

## 3. Partial occupancy — can a wing of QSS (or any large building) be separated, and what does it really cost?

### 3.1 What determines separability (SECONDARY/ASSUMPTION — building-science reasoning; code sections to be cited by the architect)

| System | What makes a wing separable | Typical 1950s–60s Ontario school reality | What it costs to fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heating** | Zoned hydronic loops with isolation valves per wing, or wing-level rooftop units; separate boiler for the wing | Usually one central boiler plant in the original block serving building-wide perimeter radiation; later additions sometimes have their own unit ventilators but share the boiler | Dedicated boiler/HVAC for a 1,500–2,500 m² wing: **$250–600K**; or accept paying a share of the central plant (ASSUMPTION) |
| **Electrical** | Sub-metering at the wing's distribution panel; separate utility service if the wing is to be a separate "building" | One main service; additions fed from the main switchboard | Sub-meters $5–15K; new separate service $50–150K (ASSUMPTION) |
| **Water/sewer** | Separate meter or sub-meter; isolation of unoccupied plumbing | One service | Sub-meter $3–10K; winterising the rest of the building is the owner's job |
| **Fire separation** | Under the Ontario Building Code (Division B, Part 3) a building may be subdivided into separate *suites* or occupancies by rated fire separations; a wing with its own exits can be treated as a separate suite, but if the remainder is vacant the whole building still needs a Fire Code-compliant fire-safety plan and working alarm | Corridors are continuous; wings connect through unrated or 45-minute separations; fire alarm is one system | New rated separations with fire doors at 1–3 corridor junctions: **$50–200K**; fire-alarm reprogramming/zoning $20–60K (ASSUMPTION) |
| **Egress** | The wing must have its own code-compliant exits (two remote exits per floor) without passing through the unoccupied part | 1950s wings usually have end-of-corridor exits — often workable | Exit doors/hardware/lighting $20–80K |
| **Elevator** | Only one elevator at QSS, at the junction of the central corridor and the east block — a two-storey wing elsewhere (e.g., the 1954 north-west block with rooms 200–217 above) would be **inaccessible on its upper floor** unless the lease includes the elevator corridor or a new lift is installed | Single elevator serving the whole building | New LULA/elevator $150–400K; or confine the school to one storey of a wing |
| **Accessibility** | Barrier-free path from accessible parking and bus drop-off to the wing's own entrance and washrooms | Accessible entrance may be at the main office, not at the wing | Ramps/auto operators/washroom retrofit $60–200K |
| **Roof and envelope** | Not separable: the roof over the unoccupied 75% still leaks onto the owner's floor, but a failed roof section next to the school's wing damages the school; masonry and windows likewise | Roof sections of differing age | Whole-building obligation — negotiate it into the lease as landlord's responsibility |
| **Maintenance/custodial** | Custodial hours scale with cleaned area; mechanical maintenance scales with equipment count, not area | — | — |
| **Site** | Parking, bus loop, snow, grounds, lighting, fencing of 6.4 ha and fields | Shared | Snow/grounds for 100 spaces + loops: $15–30K/yr whoever occupies (ASSUMPTION) |

**Conclusion:** a wing *can* be made separable, but only by converting it into a quasi-separate building (own heating, sub-metered power, rated separations, own exits and accessible entrance). That conversion costs on the order of **$0.5–1.5M** before any classroom fit-out (ESTIMATE, sum of the mid-ranges above), and it is only worth doing if the remainder of the building has an owner with its own plan.

### 3.2 Costs do not fall proportionally with floor area (explicit statement with arithmetic)

**Statement:** For an occupier taking a fraction *f* of a large building, utilities and maintenance fall far less than proportionally unless the wing is physically isolated **and** the owner mothballs or separately lets the remainder. With the remainder heated to freeze protection and no isolation, **occupying 25% of QSS carries roughly 45–70% of whole-building utilities and 50–75% of maintenance**; with full physical isolation and the remainder mothballed at the owner's cost, the occupier's own utilities approach 25–35% of the whole-building figure plus any negotiated share.

**Worked example (QSS, f = 25% ≈ 2,800 m²; whole-building 2026 utilities $166K: gas $66K, electricity $83K, water $17K — §2.4 central case, 40/50/10 split).**

*Case A — no physical isolation, central plant heats everything to normal (single boiler, no zone valves):* the school is the sole occupant and the owner passes through all utilities → **100%** ($166K) for 25% of the space.

*Case B — zone valves on the wing, remainder held at freeze protection (~45% of normal gas), electricity base loads in the remainder at ~20% of normal:*
- Gas: 0.25 × 1.00 + 0.75 × 0.45 = 0.25 + 0.34 = 0.59 → $66K × 0.59 = $39K
- Electricity: 0.25 × 1.00 + 0.75 × 0.20 = 0.25 + 0.15 = 0.40 → $83K × 0.40 = $33K
- Water: 0.25 + 0.05 = 0.30 → $17K × 0.30 = $5K
- Total $77K = **46% of the whole-building bill for 25% of the area** (1.86× proportional). If the lease makes the school carry all of it, the effective cost is $27.50/m² of occupied space.

*Case C — wing fully isolated (own boiler, sub-meters), remainder mothballed at the owner's expense:* school pays 0.25 × (gas + water) plus electricity for its own wing, with a small-wing inefficiency premium of ~15% → 0.25 × $166K × 1.15 ≈ **$48K (29%)**, after spending $0.5–1.5M on isolation (§3.1).

*Maintenance (ESTIMATE):* whole-building maintenance for a 1950s school is dominated by fixed items (roof, boilers, elevator, fire alarm, site paving, snow and grounds ≈ 60–70% of the bill) with only 30–40% scaling with cleaned/used area. At f = 25%: 0.65 × (share negotiated, 0–100%) + 0.35 × 0.25 ≈ **9%** if the landlord carries all fixed items, **74%** if the school is the sole occupant and carries everything. Lease drafting, not floor area, decides this.

**Generic rule for any large building (PROPOSAL for the finance model):** occupied-share utilities = *f* + (1 − *f*) × *m*, where *m* is the mothballed-remainder factor (0.2 for electricity, 0.45 for gas, 0.05 for water, 1.0 if the remainder is heated normally and shared). Maintenance share = *F* × *s* + (1 − *F*) × *f*, where *F* ≈ 0.65 is the fixed fraction and *s* is the negotiated share of fixed costs (0 in a gross lease, 1 as sole occupant). Replacement reserve on a leased wing = 1–2% of the *wing's* fit-out value only, because the base building reserve is the owner's.

### 5.4 Why the ranking changed from v1

v1 ranked the church first because the design target was 12–30 students. At 50→75 (FOUNDER DECISION) the church is a *core plus expansion* play, and the two former elementary-scale options — Sir Winston Churchill (whole) and a QSS wing (shared) — move ahead on fit even though their availability is less certain. The practical recommendation (PROPOSAL) is a **two-track plan**: (1) keep 253 Dundas St W (or a comparable listed CF-zoned property) as the *controllable* path that can open in September 2028, and (2) run the surplus-school track in parallel through the government question in §8 and a founder-authorised approach to HPEDSB/City once the Minister's direction is known (~Feb 2027), switching only if SWC or a QSS wing becomes genuinely available on a 2028 timeline.

## 6. Building condition assessment — checklist and cost

### 6.1 What a current BCA must establish (any candidate; QSS-specific items in §2.3)

1. **Scope and standard:** ASTM E2018 property condition assessment (non-intrusive) with a 20–25-year capital reserve forecast; two-person team (building/structural + mechanical/electrical), matching the Ministry's own condition-assessment practice (VERIFIED, Ministry 2016 technical paper). Deliverables: component inventory, condition rating, remaining life, cost and year for each repair/replacement, FCI at 5 and 20 years.
2. **Structure and envelope:** foundations, slab moisture, masonry, lintels, parging, roof (by section: membrane type, age, warranty, drainage, infrared moisture scan), windows/doors (glazing, frames, hardware), sealants.
3. **Mechanical:** boilers (age, efficiency, TSSA status), distribution (zoning, valves, pumps, piping insulation — designated-substances overlap), ventilation and make-up air, cooling, controls/BAS, domestic hot water, plumbing fixtures and backflow prevention, sprinklers/standpipe if any.
4. **Electrical:** service size and condition, switchgear, panels, wiring type (aluminium branch wiring in 1960s–70s buildings), lighting, emergency lighting, fire alarm (ULC verification date), generator/UPS, data.
5. **Elevator/lifts:** TSSA licence, modernisation needs.
6. **Life safety and code:** occupancy classification for the proposed use (Group A-2 school), fire separations, exits and travel distances, barrier-free path of travel, washroom fixture counts for the new occupant load, fire-safety plan requirements — the BCA must flag "change of use" triggers for an architect's code review.
7. **Designated substances and environmental:** designated-substances survey (asbestos, lead, silica, mercury, PCBs), mould, radon screening, Phase I ESA (fuel tanks, transformers, fill), site drainage.
8. **Accessibility audit** against the 2024 Building Code barrier-free requirements and AODA design-of-public-spaces (exterior paths, parking, play spaces).
9. **Energy:** 24 months of utility bills; metering arrangement; sub-metering feasibility; mothballing history.
10. **Site:** paving, curbs, bus loop geometry, lighting, fencing, play fields, trees, storm management, surveys/PIN, easements, zoning confirmation (Find My Zoning), heritage status (1912 Queen Victoria).
11. **For partial occupancy:** separability study (§3.1) with costed options.
12. **Record review:** board's VFA record (as of 11 Dec 2025), asbestos register, roof reports, fire inspection reports, TSSA records, permits since 2016.

### 6.2 Typical cost in Ontario

- **Comparator (VERIFIED):** PSPC awarded CIMA Canada Inc. a building condition assessment of 181 Queen Street (Ottawa) covering mechanical/electrical capacity, emergency power and structural load, with cost estimates, for **$36,431** (contract 12 Jun 2024–12 Dec 2024), https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/tender-opportunities/award-notice/cw2378091 (accessed 15 Oct 2026). Municipal RFPs (e.g., Muskoka Lakes RFP 2024-09, 30 facilities, 25-year reserve forecast, Township template; https://www.muskokalakes.ca/media/bs4frfj4/rfp-2024-09-building-condition-assessments.pdf, accessed 15 Oct 2026) confirm the standard scope but publish no fee. **VERIFIED (scope); no Ontario per-m² fee source found.**
- **Planning allowances (ESTIMATE, unsourced — CONFIRM with two quotes):** BCA with 20-year reserve forecast for a 1,500–3,500 m² former elementary school or church: **$8,000–18,000**; for all of QSS (11,239 m²): **$25,000–45,000**; designated-substances survey $4,000–12,000 (small) / $10,000–25,000 (QSS); Phase I ESA $3,000–6,000; roof infrared scan $2,000–5,000; elevator/TSSA review $1,500–3,000; architect's code and accessibility review for change of use $5,000–15,000; separability study for a wing $5,000–10,000. **Total due-diligence budget per serious candidate: $25,000–60,000 (small building) to $60,000–110,000 (QSS).** These are pre-acquisition capital costs (§7).

## 7. Capital planning assumption and how the numbers are presented

- **FOUNDER DECISION (2026-10-15):** the main operating scenario assumes founding donors, capital gifts and naming sponsorships cover acquisition, renovation and initial equipment **without debt**; this is a planning assumption, not secured funding; capital funding is separate from annual operating support and no sponsorship is counted twice.
- **Presentation rule for `finance/`:** report **Capital requirement** = due diligence (§6.2) + acquisition (or lease premium/condo unit) + fit-out/renewal + modular/addition + initial equipment (teaching kitchen, change facility, furniture, IT, garden, bus bay) + soft costs 15–20% (architect, engineers, permits — site plan $5,660, building permit, ZBA where needed) + contingency 15–25% on 1950s buildings. Report **Annual occupancy cost** (operating line) = utilities + maintenance/repairs + custodial supplies + insurance + property tax (if any) + grounds/snow + **replacement reserve contribution (1–2% of insured replacement value, or 1.5–2% of own fit-out for a leased wing)** + rent/common-area charges where leased. Do not net sponsorship against either line; show it as a source.
- **Illustration (ESTIMATE, rank 3 path — 253 Dundas St W + modular, 50 students):** capital = $45K due diligence + $950K purchase + $500K church fit-out + $2.2M modular cluster/services + $250K equipment + 18% soft ($710K) + 15% contingency ($700K) ≈ **$5.3M**; annual occupancy ≈ **$140–180K** (§5.3), of which $52–70K is reserve. For rank 1 (SWC whole, if available): capital ≈ $2.0M purchase + $2.3M renewal/fit-out + $250K equipment + soft/contingency 33% ($1.5M) + $60K due diligence ≈ **$6.1M**; annual ≈ **$210–380K**. For a QSS wing lease: capital ≈ $1.5M fit-out + $0.5–1.0M isolation (if borne by the school) + $250K equipment + soft/contingency ≈ **$3.0–3.8M**; annual **$120–350K** depending entirely on the lease's cost-sharing (§3.2). These are inputs for `finance/assumptions.md`, not decisions.
