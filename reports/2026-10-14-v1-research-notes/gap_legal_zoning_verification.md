## 3. Property tax: Assessment Act s.3(1) educational exemption; Municipal Act s.361 charity rebate; London Jewish Community Village (2020)

### Takeaway
Statute text remains UNRESOLVED (e-Laws JS-only; CanLII blocked); the earlier pass's SECONDARY sources (Carters bulletins; Law Times) remain the best available and already cover paragraph 5 wording, the ownership-vs-lease issue, and the London Jewish Community Village holding. Whether Belleville has extended the s.361 rebate beyond the mandatory 40% minimum was not checked (no City page on it was reachable; search budget exhausted).

### Cited Findings
- **SECONDARY (earlier pass)** Assessment Act s.3(1) para. 5 exempts land "owned, used and occupied solely by a non-profit philanthropic, religious or educational seminary of learning" or land leased and occupied by such a body "if the land would be exempt when occupied by the owner"; limited to buildings and up to 50 acres. — [Carters, London Jewish Community Village v MPAC, 26 Nov 2020](https://www.carters.ca/?p=8797)
- **SECONDARY (earlier pass)** *London Jewish Community Village v MPAC* (decision 5 Nov 2020): where land is leased to a seminary of learning the exemption applies only if the owner's hypothetical occupation would also be exempt, judged on present circumstances. — [Carters, 26 Nov 2020](https://www.carters.ca/?p=8797)
- **SECONDARY (earlier pass)** *St. George and St. Rueiss Coptic Orthodox Church v MPAC*, 2016 ONSC 1723 (school agreed to be a "seminary of learning"; MPAC conceded the school "would likely be exempt" if it separately owned the facilities). — [Carters Bulletin 382](https://www.carters.ca/pub/bulletin/charity/2016/chylb382.pdf); [Law Times, 16 May 2016](https://www.lawtimesnews.com/case-law/ontario-civil-decisions/tax-authority-not-entitled-to-assess-portions-of-building-in-which-school-operated/265085)
- **SECONDARY (earlier pass)** *Keewaydin Camps v Temagami* (2007): para. 5 requires the organization's primary purpose to be educational. — [Carters Bulletin 130, Jan 2008](https://www.carters.ca/pub/bulletin/charity/2008/chylb130.htm)
- **SECONDARY (earlier pass)** Registered charities may receive a minimum 40% rebate of property tax on commercial/industrial-class premises (including tax passed through by landlords). — [University of Waterloo SAF, 21 May 2024](https://uwaterloo.ca/school-of-accounting-and-finance/ontario-property-tax-exemption-npos)

### Inferences
- Owning the building (or leasing from an owner that is itself exempt, e.g. a church) is the robust route to exemption; leasing from a taxable commercial landlord likely leaves the school paying tax, with only a 40% s.361 rebate available once it is a registered charity and the premises are in the commercial class.

## 6. Private-school inspection fee; who may issue the OSSC and Certificate of Accomplishment

### Takeaway
VERIFIED: the current ontario.ca private-schools page (updated 2 Oct 2026) and the 2013 Manual (file dated 25 Nov 2021, still the version linked by the Ministry) both confirm that "schools pay a fee each time we inspect them" but neither publishes the amount; the Manual says only that inspection fees are "significantly higher" than the $300 NOI fee. Neither source addresses the OSSC or the Certificate of Accomplishment for non-inspected schools; they do establish that only inspected schools may grant OSSD credits or use Ministry course titles/codes.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED** Ontario.ca "Private schools" (published 20 Jan 2022, updated 2 Oct 2026): the Ministry inspects all private high schools seeking or holding authority to grant OSSD credits; "Schools pay a fee each time we inspect them" (no amount given); inspectors review calendars, course outlines, lesson plans, student work and records and visit classrooms but "do not assess premises, health and safety practices, or staffing"; schools may grant credits only after a successful inspection and the Ministry can revoke; re-inspection intervals of 2–3 years, next school year, or within the same year depending on results; "not all private schools" can issue OSSD credits; the page links only the 2013 Manual. — [Ontario.ca Private schools](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools)
- **VERIFIED** Private Schools Policy and Procedures Manual (2013 edition; file dated 2021-11-25): s.1 "Payment of Inspection Fees" (pp.13–14) — "Inspected private schools receive an invoice following each inspection" under Education Act s.16(7); invoices issued by the Centralized Collections Unit of Ontario Shared Services; interest charged after 30 days; s.2 (p.18) — "Inspection fees are significantly higher" than the $300 NOI fee; s.4 "The Inspection Fee" (p.26) — fee covers Ministry cost of inspection and report; non-payment "may jeopardize the private school's credit-granting authority". s.1 (p.6): "Non-Inspected private schools include all private elementary schools" and secondary schools not offering OSSD credit courses. s.1 (p.12): a school "not authorized to offer OSSD credits" may not use Ministry course titles, descriptions or codes on transcripts or report cards. s.2 (p.18): validation after a denied inspection request allows operation "as a non-inspected private school only". s.4 (p.23): "The Minister grants diplomas and Ontario Scholar Program certificates on the recommendation of the principal" (inspected schools). — [Private Schools Policy and Procedures Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- **VERIFIED (earlier pass)** Last published fee figures: $2,450 flat (2012/13), planned $4,050 by 2014/15. — [Auditor General 2013, s.3.06](https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en13/306en13.pdf)
- **VERIFIED (earlier pass)** OS K–12 (2024) s.6.3 OSSC = 14 credits; s.6.4 Certificate of Accomplishment for students leaving school without OSSD/OSSC, "must be accompanied by the Ontario Student Transcript". — [OS K–12 diploma and certificate requirements](https://www.ontario.ca/document/ontario-schools-kindergarten-grade-12-policy-and-program-requirements/diploma-and-certificate-requirements-related-procedures)

### Inferences
- Because the OSSC is a credit-based credential (14 credits) and credits can only come from an inspected school, a non-inspected private school cannot issue an OSSC. The Certificate of Accomplishment is non-credit, but OS K–12 frames it as a document issued by the principal with an Ontario Student Transcript, and the Manual reserves Ministry-form diplomas/certificates to inspected schools; the safest reading is that a non-inspected school may issue its own leaving certificate but should not purport to issue the Ministry's Certificate of Accomplishment without confirming with the Ministry's Private Schools unit.
