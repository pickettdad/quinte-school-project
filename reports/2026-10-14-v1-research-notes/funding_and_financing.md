Research date: 14 October 2026. Desk research only. Status labels used throughout: **[Verified]** = primary/official source fetched and dated; **[Estimate]** = arithmetic or sector inference by the researcher; **[Proposal]** = a structuring idea, not an existing program; **[Needs confirmation]** = could not be verified from a primary source in this pass (reason given). Important access note: ontario.ca e-Laws pages (Assessment Act, Reg. 612, Municipal Act), CanLII, CRA Charities Listings (JavaScript app), charitydata.ca and several canada.ca sub-pages could not be fetched from this environment (robots/JS/403), so statute text and T3010 comparables are reported from secondary sources and flagged as such. Framing rule for the revenue plan (applies to every section): government *family* benefits (OAP, SSAH, ACSD, Passport, ODSP, CDB, CDB-child) are **not school revenue** and cannot pay tuition; only specific purchased services the school is qualified to deliver can be invoiced to a family's allocation, and only if the family chooses that. Tax credits are not cash. Grants are time-limited. Loans are not revenue.

## Key Question 1 — Government programs the SCHOOL could receive (Ontario, federal, municipal)

### Takeaway
No Ontario or federal program provides ongoing operating funding to a private/independent school; everything available to the school entity is project-based, time-limited, discretionary, and mostly requires 12+ months of incorporated/registered financial history (which pushes most applications to 2027–2028 at the earliest). The realistic confirmed-eligible menu is: OTF Seed/Grow/Capital (after one year of financials), City of Belleville Social Infrastructure/Community Impact grants (small), Canada Summer Jobs (seasonal wages only), the federal Enabling Accessibility Fund (capital retrofits; intake closed for 2026, next intake unknown), and property-tax relief under the Assessment Act (fact-dependent). MCCSS developmental-services transfer-payment status for a school is unproven and should be treated as speculative.

**Property tax — Assessment Act s. 3(1) and Municipal Act s. 361**
- Assessment Act s. 3(1) para. 5 exempts "[l]and owned, used and occupied solely by a non-profit philanthropic, religious or educational seminary of learning" or land leased and occupied by such a body "if the land would be exempt when occupied by the owner"; limited to buildings and up to 50 acres. — quoted in [Carters, London Jewish Community Village v MPAC (Nov 26, 2020)](https://www.carters.ca/?p=8797) [Verified secondary; statute text itself not fetched]
- In *St. George and St. Rueiss Coptic Orthodox Church v MPAC*, 2016 ONSC 1723, the parties agreed the (separately incorporated, church-controlled) school was a "seminary of learning" under para. 5; MPAC argued the separately incorporated school neither owned nor exclusively occupied the land; the court decided mainly on the place-of-worship exemption and "common patrimony," and MPAC conceded the school "would likely be exempt" if it separately owned the facilities it occupied; the court also said narrow construction of exemptions no longer applies. — [Carters Bulletin 382, Mar 31, 2016](https://www.carters.ca/pub/bulletin/charity/2016/chylb382.pdf); [Law Times, May 16, 2016](https://www.lawtimesnews.com/case-law/ontario-civil-decisions/tax-authority-not-entitled-to-assess-portions-of-building-in-which-school-operated/265085) [Verified secondary]
- *Keewaydin Camps v Temagami* (2007): para. 5 requires the organization's **primary purpose** to be educational; "the presence of some learning is not enough." — [Carters Bulletin 130, Jan 2008](https://www.carters.ca/pub/bulletin/charity/2008/chylb130.htm) [Verified secondary]
- *London Jewish Community Village v MPAC* (Nov 5, 2020): where land is **leased** to a seminary of learning, the exemption applies only if the *owner's* hypothetical occupation would also be exempt, judged on present circumstances, not intentions. Practical consequence: a school leasing from a commercial landlord will generally not be exempt; a school that owns its building, or leases from an owner that is itself exempt, has the stronger case. — [Carters, Nov 26, 2020](https://www.carters.ca/?p=8797) [Verified secondary]
- Registered charities may be eligible for a minimum 40% rebate on property tax paid (incl. tax passed through by landlords) in commercial/industrial classes; non-charity NPOs may qualify for municipality-specific rebates. — [University of Waterloo NPO consulting, May 21, 2024](https://uwaterloo.ca/school-of-accounting-and-finance/ontario-property-tax-exemption-npos) [Verified secondary; Municipal Act s. 361 text not fetched]
- ONN notes "some of the exemptions listed [in s.3] are obscure" and encourages nonprofits to claim them with MPAC; case study of an exemption granted after challenge. — [ONN, Jul 24, 2023](https://theonn.ca/?p=27464) [Verified]

### Inferences
- **Timeline inference [Estimate]**: if the corporation is incorporated/registered by early 2027, the first OTF application the school could realistically file is the Capital or Seed intake in early-to-mid 2028 (one full fiscal year of statements), and Grow (which requires a "proven track record") not before 2029–2030. OTF therefore cannot fund Year-1 operations and should be shown as $0 in the opening budget, with Seed/Capital as a plausible 2028–29 line only if a 5-year lease or ownership is in place.
- **Sector fit [Needs confirmation]**: OTF funds "human and social services"; a disability-support charity whose primary activity is a school may be treated as education (outside OTF's four sectors). Frame any OTF ask around after-school/life-skills/community-participation programming and accessibility capital, not classroom instruction. The eligibility-policy page that would settle this was inaccessible.
- **EAF [Estimate]**: EAF is capital-only, intermittent, and the 2026 CFP excluded organizations that do not already employ a person with a disability; it is a 2027–2029 possibility for accessibility retrofits of a leased/owned building, not operating revenue. The CFP page's $500k–$1M project size suggests the 2026 round targeted larger projects; small-project rounds (historically up to ~$100k–$200k grants) recur irregularly.
- **Belleville municipal grants [Estimate]**: realistic annual ceiling ≈ $20,000 (Social Infrastructure) + ≤$10,000 (Community Impact, incl. in-kind) ≈ $25–30k per year, non-recurring by policy and competitive against ~40 United Way agencies; treat as "plausible, non-recurring."
- **Property tax [Estimate]**: exemption under para. 5 is plausible if the school owns its premises and its primary purpose is education; it is doubtful under a commercial lease (London Jewish Community Village). If not exempt, the 40% charity rebate applies only if the property is in the commercial/industrial class and the school is a registered charity. Value depends on premises (other stream); do not book as revenue, book as an avoided cost.
- **CSJ [Estimate]**: at Ontario minimum wage (~$17.60/h as of Oct 2025 — rate not re-verified here) a 100% subsidy on, e.g., 2 students × 8 weeks × 35 h ≈ $9,900/year of wage relief for summer programming only; it cannot fund school-year staff.
- **MCCSS TPA status [Proposal / speculative]**: becoming a ministry-funded transfer-payment recipient for respite or community participation would require a ministry decision to fund a new agency in Hastings; no open intake exists. Treat as speculative and exclude from the base case.

## Key Question 2 — Programs available to FAMILIES and what the school could legitimately bill for

### Takeaway
Every family-directed program examined explicitly or effectively excludes private-school tuition; the only lawful route for family funding to reach the school is as a *purchased service* that the program allows and the school is qualified to deliver: respite/before-and-after-school care and skill-building programs (SSAH, 0–17; OAP childhood-budget rules for respite), activity-based or life-skills programs for 18+ students (Passport, including via a ministry-funded Transfer Payment Recipient), and — only with regulated clinicians on staff or contract — OAP core clinical services (ABA, SLP, OT). ODSP, the Canada Disability Benefit, Child Disability Benefit, ACSD, DTC and RDSP are income supports or tax measures to the family and should be shown as $0 school revenue.

### Cited Findings

**Special Services at Home (SSAH) — under 18**
- Eligibility: child under 18 with a developmental and/or physical disability needing support beyond age expectations, living at home; documentation from a regulated health professional. "Discretionary program where funding is provided according to available resources"; amount set by ministry at eligibility review; no published amounts. — [SSAH Program Guidelines](https://www.ontario.ca/document/special-services-home-program-guidelines) [Verified, 2026-04-01]
- Eligible: special clothing, sensory items, "extra lessons, tutoring for learning, learning tools, social skills programs," help with self-care/daily routines; respite including "daytime program respite, before/after school care, care during PA Days, March break and summer break"; caregiver training; worker training. **Ineligible: "private school tuition for the child"**, food, transportation, therapies funded elsewhere (ABA, SLP, OT, PT, mental health), assistive devices, home/vehicle modifications. Families may hire directly, buy support from an agency's workers, or have "an agency manage your funding on your behalf." — same source [Verified, 2026-04-01]; agency-management option also at [SSAH page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/special-services-home) [Verified, 2026-04-01]
- The guidelines "do not address school-day education programming or respite during regular school hours." — same source [Verified]
- Holland Bloorview tipsheet: approval does not guarantee funding; the amount "may not cover all expenses"; eligible categories "functioning, family, friends, fun, fitness, future planning." — [Holland Bloorview SSAH tipsheet](https://hollandbloorview.ca/services/family-workshops-resources/family-resource-centre/online-family-resources-centre/special-services-at-home-funding-tipsheet) [Verified, undated, PDF folder Aug 2024]

**Medical Expense Tax Credit — tuition for care/training (ITA 118.2(2)(e))**
- Folio S1-F1-C1 ¶1.56–1.59: fees to a school/institution for "care, or care and training" qualify where the patient has a mental or physical impairment, requires the equipment/facilities/personnel the school specially provides, and an appropriately qualified person (may include the principal) certifies in writing that the impairment is the reason; the certification must name the specific school; fees may qualify "even if some could be seen as tuition" (citing *Rannelli v MNR*, 91 DTC 816); if attendance is only beneficial rather than required, it does not qualify. Folio last modified Aug 15, 2025. — [CRA Folio S1-F1-C1](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-1-individuals/folio-1-health-medical/income-tax-folio-s1-f1-c1-medical-expense-tax-credit.html) [Verified, 2025-08-15]

### Inferences
- **What the school could bill for [Estimate / conditional]**:
  1. *SSAH (ages 4–17)*: before/after-school care, PA-day/March-break/summer respite and "social skills programs"/"extra lessons" purchased from the school as an agency — allowed; **core school-day instruction is not**, and the guidelines are silent on school-hours respite (ask the Special Agreements Officer). Allocation amounts are unpublished and discretionary (families in the sector commonly report low-thousands per year — **[Needs confirmation]**). Realistic: a few hundred to low thousands per family per year, only from families who choose to spend it with the school.
  2. *OAP (autistic students only)*: the school can be paid (a) for respite/camps/skill-building under childhood-budget rules, or (b) for ABA/SLP/OT/mental-health services under core clinical services **only if delivered or supervised by college-registered professionals** (behaviour analyst/psychologist for ABA; registered SLP/OT). A school-run "specialized autism school service" is explicitly purchasable only when "autism-specific, individualized and ABA-based" — this is the one line item where something close to program fees is allowed, but it applies only to the OAP childhood-budget rules, not to core clinical services (which exclude tuition and tutoring). Budgets are per-child ($6,600–$65,000 by age/intensity) and are also what families use for their private therapists, so the school would be competing for the same dollars.
  3. *Passport (students 18–21)*: Passport explicitly covers adults "still in school" who need community-participation support; a school's 18+ life-skills/pre-employment/community-participation day program is squarely admissible ("daily living programs," "pre-employment supports," "activity-based day" programs, adult education providers). A family can pay the school by reimbursement; or the school could seek status as a ministry-funded TPR (no open process found). Floor $5,665/person/yr is automatic; higher amounts require an application and are capped at $45,604.
  4. *ODSP/CDB/ACSD/Child Disability Benefit*: income supports to the person/family; could in principle fund transportation or meals the family pays for, but the school has committed to covering those, so model $0.
  5. *DTC/RDSP/METC*: tax measures. The METC for "care and training" would only matter if the school charged fees, which it will not; note it only as the reason a future fee-charging pivot would be partially tax-relieved for families.
- **Double-counting guard**: SSAH, OAP and Passport are mutually exclusive by age (SSAH/OAP under 18, Passport 18+) and OAP respite overlaps SSAH respite; model only one program per student and only for purchased services outside core instruction.
- **Order-of-magnitude [Estimate]**: if 4 of 20 students are 18+ with Passport at the $5,665 floor and families direct ~60% of it to the school's day program ≈ $13,600/yr; if 8 of 16 under-18 families spend ~$1,500 of SSAH/OAP on the school's after-school/summer respite ≈ $12,000/yr. Total family-program billing ≈ $20–30k/yr at 20 students — roughly 3–4% of a ~$800k budget. Treat as "plausible" only after a provider agreement model is drafted; exclude from the committed base.

## Key Question 4 — Corporate partnerships and sponsorship (incl. CRA receipting rules, in-kind)

### Takeaway
Corporate money arrives in two legally distinct forms the school must keep separate: *donations* (receiptable, reduced by any advantage; no receipt if the advantage exceeds 80%) and *sponsorships* (promotional value received, not receiptable, deducted by the business as advertising, reported by the charity on T3010 line 4630). Local corporate giving is real but mostly event-sized ($10–100k presenting sponsorships at the top end); multiyear commitments exist (UWHPE's campaign cabinet shows which employers engage) but must be documented as pledges and discounted for non-fulfilment. No rule was found binding independent schools' naming/sponsorship policies beyond CRA and general law.

### Cited Findings
- CRA split receipting: eligible amount = FMV of gift − FMV of advantage; **if the advantage exceeds 80% of the gift, no gift (no receipt)**; de minimis — advantages up to the lesser of $75 or 10% can be ignored, but never for cash-equivalents or the object of the event (dinner, green fees). — [CRA Split receipting](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/issuing-receipts/split-receipting.html) [Verified, 2018-05-22]
- Donation vs sponsorship (practitioner summary, updated Sept 7, 2026): a simple name/logo acknowledgment with no promotional claims generally has no FMV (full receipt possible); prominent naming rights or exclusive audience access are commercial arrangements and "should not receive a charitable receipt"; a sponsorship is deductible to the business as a promotional expense under ITA s. 9 (hospitality components 50%-limited under s. 67.1); a business cannot claim both a receipt and a deduction for the same payment; charity reports sponsorship revenue on **T3010 line 4630** per CG-013. — [Charity Law Group](https://www.charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/donation-vs-sponsorship-how-should-our-business-fund-our-charity-donations) [Verified secondary, 2026-09-07]
- CG-013 classes corporate sponsorship programs and cause-related marketing as fundraising activities (their costs count in the fundraising ratio). — [CRA CG-013](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/fundraising-registered-charities-guidance.html) [Verified]
- Local examples of corporate sponsorship: Klemencic Homes as presenting sponsor of Hospice Quinte's $101k gala; Occasions by the Bay donated food. — [InQuinte, Nov 21, 2025](https://www.inquinte.ca/2025/11/casino-royale-raises-101000-for-hospice-quinte) [Verified]

- Second Harvest Food Rescue redirects surplus food to non-profits "in every province and territory"; registration via foodrescue.secondharvest.ca; schools not mentioned; eligibility/cost not stated on the page. — [Second Harvest](https://secondharvest.ca/foodrescue) [Verified, 2026]
- Ontario Student Nutrition Program lead agencies "build sponsor partnerships" and the 2026 "Healthy Students Brighter Ontario" campaign directs public donations to four organizations. — [Student Nutrition Program](https://www.ontario.ca/page/student-nutrition-program) [Verified, 2026-06-11]
- City of Belleville grants may be delivered as in-kind municipal resources (waived facility fees, equipment, staff support), capped within the $10,000 Community Impact limit. — [OpenCouncil](https://opencouncil.ca/belleville-grants/); [Belleville Grant Policy](https://www.belleville.ca/en/city-hall/resources/Documents/Community-Funding-and-Grants/Grant-Policy_October-2025.pdf) [Verified]

### Inferences
- **Receipting design [Proposal]**: offer two parallel menus — (1) "Named gift" recognition (donor wall, newsletter, logo among supporters) that stays within the de minimis/no-FMV zone and is fully receiptable; (2) "Sponsorship" packages (event naming, bus-side recognition, website ads) priced at FMV, invoiced with HST where applicable, no receipt. Mixed packages must be split-receipted with a documented FMV of the advantage.
- **Multiyear corporate commitments [Estimate]**: realistic local tiers — presenting-level $25–50k/yr from one or two anchor employers (e.g., a dealership group, a builder, a bank branch network), $5–10k/yr from 5–10 mid-sized firms, $1–2.5k from 20–30 small businesses — totalling roughly $100–200k/yr once mature; year 1 should assume one-third of that. Only signed multiyear pledge agreements should count as "committed," and even then apply a 10–20% non-fulfilment discount (sector practice, unverified).
- **In-kind [Estimate]**: food via Second Harvest/Gleaners (not verified locally), donated professional services (legal/accounting), and donated premises reduce costs but are not payroll funding; show them as avoided cost with a cap, never as cash revenue. Gifts-in-kind of property can be receipted at FMV; donated *services* cannot be receipted (CRA rule — standard, not re-fetched here [Needs confirmation of citation]).
- **Naming rules [Verified by absence]**: no Ontario statute or regulation binding independent schools' sponsorship/naming policies was found; public board policies do not apply. The constraints are CRA (above), the school's own governance policy (recommended: a donor-recognition and naming policy approved by the board, with revocation clauses), and Ontario advertising-to-children norms if marketing to students.

## Key Question 5 — Bus advertising and sponsorship revenue

### Takeaway
I could not retrieve the text of R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 612 (School Buses) in this pass, so the precise list of permitted exterior markings is **unverified**; what is verified is that Ontario school-bus advertising has existed historically (a 1997 consortium deal covering ~2,500 Southwestern Ontario buses with exterior and interior panels, 50% of gross revenue to boards/operators), that no Canadian examples or rates appear in current references, and that the realistic net from 1–3 buses is small. ### Inferences
- **Scale arithmetic [Estimate]**: the Michigan district figure (~$70k/yr for a district presumably running on the order of 40–60 buses) implies roughly $1–2k per bus per year gross in a U.S. market with exterior ads permitted; after a 50% operator/vendor share (the 1997 Ontario model) and in a small Ontario market, 1–3 buses would yield perhaps **$1,000–$5,000/yr net** — immaterial to a $700k–$1.3M budget. Do not build a revenue line on it.
- **Regulatory posture [Needs confirmation]**: Reg. 612 prescribes required markings (chrome-yellow colour, "SCHOOL BUS" signs, owner name) for vehicles operated as school buses; whether non-prescribed exterior lettering/advertising is prohibited must be read from the current consolidation. - **Insurance [Estimate]**: exterior wraps alter the insured vehicle and may require the operator's insurer's consent; interior cards have negligible insurance impact.
- **Sponsored-bus model [Proposal]**: a business underwrites the annual transportation cost (likely $40–80k/yr for one contracted route — cost from the operating-cost stream) in exchange for a modest "Transportation made possible by X" decal and website mention. If the recognition is nominal, issue a full receipt as a donation; if the sponsor wants a wrap or exclusivity, treat as sponsorship (no receipt, business deducts as advertising). This is simply a restricted gift, not new revenue — do not count it in addition to corporate giving.

## Key Question 6 — Interest-free and other financing

### Takeaway
No source offers a brand-new charity with no revenue an interest-free institutional loan; the federal Social Finance Fund is explicitly repayable, market-return-seeking, routed through intermediaries, and (per ONN) "not set up" for small early-stage nonprofits; Community Forward Fund lends to nonprofits but publishes no rates/terms and is a $12M national fund; community bonds are a proven debt tool but presuppose an asset or revenue stream and an engaged investor base. Interest-free money is therefore realistically **philanthropic debt** — a donor/foundation loan, forgivable loan or guarantee — structured by agreement. Borrowing should be shown separately from revenue; $500k interest-free over 10 years is $50k/yr of principal that the operating budget must still generate.

### Cited Findings
- Social Finance Fund: $755M; three fund managers (Realize Capital Partners $135M, Boann Social Impact $154M, CAP Finance $89M per ONN); nonprofits "can't apply directly" — they approach intermediaries; capital is repayable, program runs to March 31, 2039; initial investments outside Québec target intermediaries with "potential for market-rate returns"; ONN: "the current structure of the SFF is not set up to address these barriers" for nonprofits; small/early-stage nonprofits take longest to evaluate; Investment Readiness Program ended March 31, 2024; named intermediaries are in MB, BC, QC or national (Jubilee Fund, New Market Funds, Windmill Microlending community bonds, Thrive Impact Fund) — **none Ontario-specific**. — [ONN SFF Briefing Note, Jan 2025 (PDF)](https://theonn.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/SFF-Briefing-Note-External-JAN-2025.docx-1.pdf) [Verified]; [ESDC SFF page](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/social-innovation-social-finance/social-finance-fund.html) [Verified, 2025-11-17]; [Tapestry, "Demystifying the SFF"](https://tapestrycapital.ca/demystifying-the-social-finance-fund/) [Verified, 2026-04-10]
- Community Forward Fund: $12M fund lending "to non-profit organizations, charities and non-profit social enterprises" across Canada; examples include working capital for a co-op grocery, renovation of a heritage storefront, land/construction for transitional housing; intake via a Borrower Intake Form; **no rates, terms, sizes or security requirements published**. — [Community Forward Fund](https://communityforwardfund.ca/) [Verified, undated site]

### Inferences
- **Source-by-source assessment [Estimate]**:
  - *SFF intermediaries (Realize/Boann-backed funds)*: market-rate or near-market debt; expect a track record requirement; not interest-free; effectively unavailable before the school has 2–3 years of audited operations and a repayment source. Exclude from the base case.
  - *Community Forward Fund*: plausible for a facility purchase/renovation once there is a lease/ownership and a revenue plan; expect interest (unpublished), term and possibly security over the asset. Not interest-free.
  - *Community bonds*: feasible only for an asset (buying/renovating a building) with a credible repayment stream; a school with zero fee revenue would be repaying bondholders from donations — a weak case; expect 2–5% coupons (typical of Ontario issuers — unverified) and $50k+ in structuring/administration costs over the life of the raise (unverified).
  - *Credit-union community loans (Kawartha CU etc.)*: not reached; credit unions do lend to nonprofits and commonly bridge confirmed grant receivables (assignment of the grant agreement) — this is the standard mechanism for financing OTF/EAF holdbacks (OTF holds back 10%; EAF/contribution agreements pay on claims). Expect prime-plus pricing and a board resolution; personal guarantees are sometimes requested from directors of new organizations — **flag and refuse**.
  - *Infrastructure Ontario*: historically limited to listed public-sector client categories (municipalities, universities, LTC homes, hospices, community health hubs, social housing, etc.); private schools/charities generally are not listed — **[Needs confirmation]**.
  - *BDC/Futurpreneur*: for-profit lenders; not applicable (not re-verified).
  - *Foundation PRIs / recoverable grants / philanthropist loans [Proposal]*: the only realistic *interest-free* capital. Structure as a written loan agreement (principal, 0% interest, term, repayment schedule, subordination, forgiveness triggers tied to milestones such as charitable registration or enrolment), with the lender able to forgive and receipt the forgiven principal as a gift at that time (CRA treatment of loan forgiveness as a gift should be confirmed by counsel — **[Needs confirmation]**).
  - *Forgivable loans / guarantees [Proposal]*: a donor guarantee of a credit-union line (e.g., $250k) lets the school bridge timing gaps without a personal guarantee from directors.
- **Affordability arithmetic [Estimate]**: $500,000 interest-free over 10 years = $50,000/yr principal (≈ 6% of an $800k budget; ≈ 1.4 students' cost at $35k). At 5% interest, the same loan amortised over 10 years costs ≈ $63,600/yr (monthly P&I ≈ $5,300). $250k at 0% over 5 years = $50k/yr. Any loan therefore converts a one-time capital need into a *recurring* fundraising obligation and must be added to the KQ7 annual gap for the term.
- **Bridging mechanics [Estimate]**: confirmed grants are paid on milestones/holdbacks; a credit-union operating line secured by an assignment of the grant agreement covers 60–90 days of timing; cost ≈ prime + 1–3% on the drawn amount for weeks, i.e., hundreds to low thousands of dollars — budget as a finance expense, not a funding source.
- **Non-double-counting rule**: show borrowing in a separate "sources of capital" schedule (loan proceeds in, repayments out) and never in the operating revenue table.

## Key Question 7 — Zero-cost-to-families gap logic, comparables and reserve targets

### Takeaway
At $35–45k fully loaded per student plus roughly $6–10k per student for transportation and meals, the annual cash requirement is ≈ $0.5–0.65M at 12 students, $0.8–1.1M at 20 and $1.2–1.65M at 30; with family-program billing and government grants contributing at most ~5–10% in a good year, essentially **all** of it must be raised philanthropically every year. That is 25–70% of what the entire United Way HPE campaign raises across 40 agencies, and several multiples of what established local charities net from their flagship events. The honest conclusion is that the model is feasible only with a small number of very large committed donors or a capital/endowment gift, and a pre-opening reserve of 18–24 months of operating cost.

**Comparison with what Belleville sustains [Estimate]**
- The 20-student requirement (~$0.8–1.0M/yr) equals **35–45% of the entire UWHPE campaign** and roughly **8–10 Hospice-Quinte-scale galas per year**. Hospice Quinte needed 40 years, a province-funded core (Ontario Health funds residential hospice beds — not re-verified here) and a donor wall of top-tier donors to reach its current event scale.
- Local disability agencies (Community Living Belleville and Area, Community Visions) are funded overwhelmingly by MCCSS transfer payments, with donations as a supplement — the opposite of this school's model. Their T3010s (Gap) will likely show receipted gifts in the low six figures at most against multi-million government revenue; that is the realistic ceiling for disability-cause philanthropy in this market absent a transformational donor.
- Local Christian schools (Quinte Christian High School, Belleville Christian School) run on tuition plus congregational giving; their T3010 "other revenue" vs "receipted gifts" split (Gap) is the best local proxy for how much a committed affinity community in Belleville donates per student per year — expect low thousands per family, not $35k.

**Committed-recurring-funding and reserve targets before opening [Estimate / Proposal]**
- *Go/no-go gate*: signed multi-year pledges (3+ years) covering at least **60–70% of Year-1 cash need**, plus an **18–24-month operating reserve** in cash or irrevocable pledges, before signing a lease and hiring. Rationale: (i) no government operating stream exists; (ii) OTF/United Way eligibility only begins after 1–2 audited years, so Years 1–2 are 100% donor-dependent; (iii) CRA expects a justified reserve policy (CG-013 App. C); (iv) sector practice commonly cites 3–6 months as a *minimum* reserve for established charities — a start-up with no earned revenue and a duty of care to students who cannot easily be moved mid-year warrants far more. The 18–24 month figure is the researcher's proposal, not a published standard.
- At 12 students that gate is ≈ **$0.8–1.3M in reserve plus ~$350–450k/yr in multiyear pledges**; at 20 students ≈ **$1.3–2.1M reserve plus ~$550–700k/yr pledged**.
- *Endowment equivalence* (4% draw): fully endowing the 20-student school would need ≈ **$20–25M**; endowing one student (~$43k incl. transport/meals) needs ≈ **$1.1M**. Use "endow a seat" as the bequest/major-gift framing, but do not count any of it before the gift is received.

**Non-double-counting rules for the revenue plan**
1. Family programs (SSAH/OAP/Passport) appear only as "purchased services revenue," only for the specific non-instructional services, and only one program per student.
2. Grants appear in the year received, with term and end date, never annualised into a run-rate.
3. In-kind appears as a reduction of the matching expense line with a cap, never as cash.
4. Sponsorship (non-receipted, line 4630) and donations (receipted, line 4500) are separate lines so the fundraising ratio can be computed.
5. Loan proceeds appear in a capital schedule; repayments appear as an expense in the operating budget.
6. Tax credits (DTC, METC, charitable donation credit) appear nowhere as revenue — they are donor/family incentives only.
