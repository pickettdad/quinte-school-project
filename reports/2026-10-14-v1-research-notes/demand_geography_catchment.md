### Inferences
- **[ESTIMATE] The 2025 figures are plausible and internally consistent with 2024.** Changes Oct 2024 → Oct 2025: IDD 293 → 305 (+12); IMD 81 → 87 (+6); CA 561 → 655 (+94, +17%); M 299 → 311 (+12); CLD 550 → 544 (−6). The large autism jump is the only cell worth querying (could be real growth or a coding change).
- **[ESTIMATE] These are IPRC-identified students only.** The table is labelled "IPRC exceptionality counts"; IEP-only students appear separately as "No Exceptionality" (1,591 in Oct 2024). Identified ≈ 2,140 column-sum vs 3,093 − 1,591 = 1,502 students with an exceptionality on an IEP. The 2,140 vs 1,502 difference (≈ 640) is consistent with the footnote: Multiple students' 2nd/3rd exceptionalities are added into other columns, so **each Multiple student may be counted two or three times across columns**. Treat the column sum as > unique students. **Needs confirmation** with HPEDSB Student Services whether IDD/IMD columns include Multiple students' secondary codes.
- **[ESTIMATE] Secondary counts almost certainly include 18–21-year-olds.** Secondary IDD (171–187) exceeds elementary IDD (118–122) even though secondary enrolment is roughly 30–35% of the board. Two mechanisms: identification accumulates with age, and students with IDD commonly remain in LSP/CIP to age 21 (Ontario Education Act entitles attendance to 21). Nothing in the fetched documents states the age cut-off — **needs confirmation**.
- **[ESTIMATE] HPEDSB identifies intellectual exceptionalities at roughly 2.5× the provincial rate.** HPEDSB (IDD+IMD) 374 ÷ 15,513 = **2.41%** (Oct 2024) vs Ontario 19,085 ÷ 2,095,193 = **0.91%** (2024–25). Autism 561 ÷ 15,513 = 3.6% vs 1.69%; Multiple 1.93% vs 0.72%; IEP rate 19.9% vs 17.3%. Likely drivers: the Multiple double-counting footnote, local identification practice, and regional socio-demographics. For a demand estimate this means the board figure is an **upper-bound indicator** of the IDD/MID population, not a conservative one.
- **[ESTIMATE] Geographic distribution by municipality is not published.** Program locations are the best proxy: Belleville hosts 7–8 of 21 elementary resource programs and 2 of 7 LSP/CIP sites (Centennial, Eastside) plus Bayside SS (physically in Quinte West, administered as Belleville); Trenton hosts 4 elementary programs and Trenton HS (2 LSP/3 CIP). Roughly 60–65% of congregated seats are within the Belleville–Trenton corridor.

## KQ3. Population and age structure of the catchment; prevalence triangulation

### Takeaway
The core catchment (Belleville, Quinte West, Stirling-Rawdon, Tyendinaga Township, Tyendinaga Mohawk Territory, Deseronto) held ≈115,000 people in 2021, ≈67% of the HPEDSB territory (Hastings census division + Prince Edward County ≈171,450). An estimated ≈21,500 residents aged 4–21 live in that core, ≈30,000–31,000 in the whole board territory. At the generally accepted ~1% intellectual-disability prevalence this implies ≈215 core / ≈300 board-wide children with ID — **below** the board's identified IDD+MID count (374–392), consistent with HPEDSB's high identification rate and the Multiple double-count.

## KQ4. Demand funnel: identified students → potentially interested families (range, not point estimate)

### Takeaway
Working from the board counts as broad indicators, the pool of families who might **seriously consider** a free, specialised IDD/MID school in Belleville is plausibly **≈15–60 families under a 30-minute bus scenario, ≈25–100 under 45 minutes, and ≈35–130 under 60 minutes**; the subset likely to actually enrol at opening, before any marketing or interest testing, is roughly a third to a half of that. These are **interest-pool** figures, not enrolment demand; enrolment demand can only be established by the interest-testing method in KQ6.

### Cited Findings
(All inputs are cited in KQ1–KQ3 and KQ5; this section is arithmetic on those inputs.)
- Board-wide HPEDSB IPRC counts Oct 2025 (client, unverified) / Oct 2024 (verified): IDD 305 / 293; IMD 87 / 81; Multiple 311 / 299; Autism 655 / 561. — [SEAC Feb 27 2025](https://www.hpedsb.on.ca/common/pages/GetFile.ashx?key=sktYARW1)
- HPEDSB congregated capacity 2025–26: 21 elementary resource programs; 12 LSP + 14 CIP secondary classes. — [HPEDSB program summaries](https://www.hpedsb.on.ca/programs___learning/programs/special_education_programs)
- Ontario independent-school share 6.4%; OAC exclusion/modified-day rates 6% / 19%. — [Cardus](https://www.cardus.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Summary_Who-Chooses-Independent-Schools-Ontario_Cardus.pdf); [OAC survey](https://educationnewscanada.com/article/education/level/k12/3/1121857/ontario-autism-coalition-releases-special-education-survey-report.html)

### Inferences — the funnel (every step is an [ESTIMATE]; assumptions stated)
**Step A — Primary pool, whole HPEDSB territory (Hastings CD + PEC):**
- A1. HPEDSB IDD + IMD = **392** (2025 basis; 374 on verified 2024 basis).
- A2. HPEDSB "Multiple" with an intellectual component: Multiple = 311; assume 30–50% have IDD/MID as one component → 93–156; but the footnote says 2nd/3rd exceptionalities are already reflected in category totals, so part of this is already inside A1. Net addition assumed **40–100**.
- A3. Autism with co-occurring ID not already coded IDD/Multiple: not added (would be double-counting under HPEDSB's footnote); noted as an adjacent segment of up to ~100–200 students (≈30% of 655) that the school's intake policy may or may not accept.
- A4. ALCDSB in catchment: **45–120** (4,865 × 0.91%–2.41%).
- A5. Quinte Mohawk School, French schools, private, home-schooled: **5–20**.
- **A = 392 + (40–100) + (45–120) + (5–20) = 482–632 → use 480–630.**

**Step B — Geographic reach under multi-pickup bus scenarios** (shares of the territory's identified students, youth-weighted; see KQ5 for why bus reach < drive-time radius):
- 30-min bus (≈15–20 min drive radius: Belleville urban + Bayside/Foxboro/Corbyville/Cannifton/Point Anne/Rossmore/Shannonville fringe): Belleville 4–21 share 10,259/30,300 = 34%, plus fringe → **35–40%**.
- 45-min bus (≈25–32 min drive radius: + Trenton, Frankford, Stirling, Marysville/TMT, Deseronto, Roslin/Plainfield, Rossmore/Ameliasburgh): core catchment share 71% nominal; multi-pickup routing means a bus cannot sweep all of Trenton *and* reach Belleville in 45 min → effective **55–65%**.
- 60-min bus (≈35–45 min drive radius: + Tweed, Madoc/Centre Hastings, Picton/Bloomfield/Wellington, Napanee (non-HPEDSB), Brighton edge; Marmora/Bancroft out): **75–85%**.

**Step C — Fit with a small school at opening** (excludes students whose medical/behavioural complexity or age the school cannot serve in year 1; includes both panels): **60–80%**.

**Step D — Propensity to seriously consider leaving the public/Catholic system for a free specialised school.** Anchors: 6.4% of Ontario families already choose independent schools (general population, mostly fee-paying); ≈25% of special-ed families report exclusion or modified days (OAC, self-selected sample); the proposed school is free, removing the largest barrier. Assume **15–30% would seriously consider**; **5–12% would enrol at opening** absent further information (families in satisfied LSP/CIP/resource placements are unlikely movers; those on modified days, excluded, or in regular classes without adequate support are the likeliest).

**Arithmetic (low × low … high × high):**
| Scenario | A (pool) | × B (reach) | × C (fit) | = addressable | × D-consider (15–30%) | × D-enrol (5–12%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 min | 480–630 | 0.35–0.40 | 0.60–0.80 | 101–202 | **15–61** | **5–24** |
| 45 min | 480–630 | 0.55–0.65 | 0.60–0.80 | 158–328 | **24–98** | **8–39** |
| 60 min | 480–630 | 0.75–0.85 | 0.60–0.80 | 216–428 | **32–128** | **11–51** |

Worked example, 45-min low case: 480 × 0.55 = 264; × 0.60 = 158; × 0.15 = 24 interested families; × 0.05 = 8 enrolments. High case: 630 × 0.65 = 410; × 0.80 = 328; × 0.30 = 98; × 0.12 = 39.

**Interpretation:** a 45-minute catchment gives a plausible interest pool of a few dozen families and an opening cohort in the high single digits to high thirties — enough to justify interest testing, not enough to assume a 40–60-student school without evidence. Competition is mainly the board's own congregated programs (≈26 secondary classes and 21 elementary programs — rough capacity 350–450 seats, i.e., comparable in size to the entire identified IDD+MID+Multiple pool), the ALCDSB Kingston-based Life Skills centres (distant for Belleville families), home-schooling, and (for 18–21s and adults only) Loyalist CICE and Community Living day programs.

**Sensitivity:** the result is most sensitive to D (family propensity), which no desk source pins down for this population — hence KQ6. If HPEDSB's counts double-count Multiple students heavily, A could be ≈20% lower; if ALCDSB identifies at HPEDSB rates, A is at the top of range.

### Gaps
- No local or Ontario data on the share of IDD/MID families who would leave a public placement for an independent school; D is assumption-driven.
- No LSP/CIP enrolment, class-size or waitlist data to size "unmet" vs "served" demand.

## KQ6. Testing family interest later: method, sample sizes, privacy/ethics

### Takeaway
The right instrument is a short anonymous online survey distributed through intermediaries that already reach IDD/MID families (Community Living Belleville & Area, Community Living Quinte West's Family Engagement Network/TAY program, QCTC, the HPEDSB and ALCDSB SEAC member associations, parent Facebook groups), paired with a separate opt-in expression-of-interest (EOI) list and two or three small focus groups. Because the whole target population is only ≈480–630 families, **80–130 completed surveys from IDD/MID families and 40–60 named EOIs within the 45-minute zone** would be decision-grade evidence for a 20–30-student opening cohort.

### Inferences / Proposal ([PROPOSAL] unless marked)
- **Instrument 1 — anonymous online survey (8–12 questions, ≤5 min, plain language, French option).** Screen: child aged 3–21 with IDD/MID/Multiple (self-report; optional IPRC category); current placement (regular class, resource program, LSP/CIP, Catholic, home-school, other); municipality/first three postal-code characters only; satisfaction/exclusion items mirroring the OAC survey (modified day? sent home? exclusion?); interest in a free specialised independent school in Belleville (5-point scale); maximum acceptable one-way ride (20/30/45/60+ min); willingness to drive to a pickup point; deal-breakers (transport, faith, loss of board services, credits/OSSC path, 18–21 continuity); open comment. No child names, no DOB, no diagnoses beyond category.
- **Sample size [ESTIMATE]:** population N ≈ 480–630 families. For a proportion at ±10 percentage points, 95% CI: n₀ = 1.96²×0.25/0.10² = 96; finite-population correction at N = 550: 96/(1+95/550) ≈ **82**. For ±7.5 pp: n₀ = 171 → **≈130**. Target **80–130 completes from eligible families**; also collect from autism-without-ID and other special-ed families but analyse separately. Because distribution is through intermediaries (non-probability), report results as indicative with the response channel logged.
- **Instrument 2 — opt-in Expression of Interest list** (separate form, explicit consent, named contact, child's age band and municipality, preferred start year). Decision rule [PROPOSAL]: ≥40–60 EOIs inside the 45-minute zone, of which ≥25 say "likely/very likely", supports planning a 20–30-student opening cohort (assumed EOI→enrolment conversion 40–50%); fewer than 25 EOIs signals a 30-minute/Belleville-only or later-start option.
- **Instrument 3 — two or three focus groups (6–8 parents each; Belleville, Trenton, east county/TMT)** recruited from EOI respondents, plus one with transition-age (18–21) families via CLQW's TAY program, to test program model, transport tolerance and reasons for staying in board programs.
- **Distribution plan:** ask (not require) Community Living agencies, QCTC's family newsletter, Autism Ontario Quinte-area contacts and SEAC member associations to circulate a link; local Facebook parent groups; a short radio/print notice. Do **not** ask boards or Tri-Board for family lists — boards cannot release student data under MFIPPA/Education Act, and a board may decline to promote a competing school; a SEAC delegation is the appropriate channel to inform them.
- **Privacy and ethics:** keep survey and EOI as separate forms so survey remains anonymous; store EOI data in Canada; state purpose, retention (e.g., 24 months), and withdrawal rights; no incentives tied to disclosing diagnosis; obtain written consent for focus-group recording; avoid collecting data on children under 18 beyond age band; Indigenous engagement on TMT should follow MBQ's own protocols (council/education department approval before surveying on-territory families) — [PROPOSAL; principle consistent with federal OCAP norms, not verified against an MBQ policy].
- **Timing:** run in late winter/spring (Feb–Apr) when families are receiving IPRC/placement decisions for the next September and before Tri-Board's August 5 application deadline.

## Cross-cutting notes for other researchers (brief)
- **Funding/legal:** 18–21-year-olds are likely a material share of secondary IDD counts (KQ1) — matters for Private School Notice of Intention scope and any OSSC/Certificate-of-Accomplishment pathway; TMT families are federally funded via ISC/MBQ (KQ2); Tri-Board has no at-cost/private-school provision in policy (KQ5) so transport is an operating cost line; HPEDSB's Multiple footnote means board counts should never be presented as "unique students" in any public document.
- **Premises:** program-location map (KQ1) shows the Belleville–Trenton corridor already hosts most congregated seats; a hub on Hwy 2/62 or near the 401/Hwy 62 interchange minimises satellite-pickup run times (KQ5).
- **Bus pricing:** scenarios assume 2–4 accessible mini-buses with satellite pickups (KQ5); Tri-Board's 2026 operator panel (Sharp, Voyago, Switzer-Carty, Attridge, Landmark) are the likely local contractors.
