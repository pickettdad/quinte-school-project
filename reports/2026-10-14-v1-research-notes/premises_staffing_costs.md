## Key Question 1 — Surplus/vacant school-board property: process, priority list, and whether an independent school could acquire or lease

### Takeaway
O. Reg. 444/98 was replaced by **O. Reg. 374/23 (effective 31 Dec 2023)**: the Minister of Education now decides who a surplus school is offered to (priority: other school boards → Ontario Crown → bodies serving "provincial priorities" such as long-term care or affordable housing), all at fair market value; only if no priority recipient is named does the board get Minister's permission to sell or lease on the open market, where a non-profit independent school could bid like anyone else. Critically for this project, **HPEDSB declared three Belleville properties surplus effective 28 Sept 2026 — Queen Victoria School (46 Pine St), Quinte Secondary School (45 College St W) and Sir Winston Churchill PS (301 MacDonald Ave)** — and has asked the Ministry to direct next steps. The Community Planning and Partnerships Guideline expressly makes "JK–12 private schools" ineligible partners for space in operating schools.

### Cited Findings

**The regulation and the process**
- The province's plain-language page says a board that is not using a property and will not use it within 10 years "must sell it"; the province first assesses whether the property "meets the needs of other school boards in the area" or "can be used for other provincial priorities, such as long-term care homes or affordable housing"; if so, the board and that party negotiate "at fair market value"; otherwise "the school board must sell or lease (if applicable) the property according to the regulation"; proceeds must be reinvested in school repair/renewal. The page cites O. Reg. 374/23 "as of December 31, 2023" and does not mention private schools or non-profits. — [Ontario.ca, Selling or leasing surplus school board property](https://www.ontario.ca/page/selling-leasing-surplus-school-board-property) (fetched 14 Oct 2026) [VERIFIED]
- BLG's analysis: Bill 98 (Better Schools and Student Outcomes Act, 2023) passed 8 June 2023; O. Reg. 374/23 replaced 444/98 on 31 Dec 2023. Dispositions started under 444/98 on or before 30 Dec 2023 continue under the old process for three years after the 180-day proposal period; any still undisposed by **28 June 2027** must restart under the new regulation. s.1(2) allows expedited disposition (land swap/leaseback) at FMV with Minister approval; s.2 makes disposition mandatory for property unused and not needed for pupil accommodation in the next 10 years (exemption possible if board shows need beyond 10 years, s.2(2)); s.5: the Minister decides which bodies receive an offer, in priority (1) school boards, (2) the Crown in right of Ontario, (3) "persons or bodies needed for provincial priorities as have been identified for the purpose". BLG notes category 3 "can include public or private persons or bodies" and that no Ministry guidance on category 3 had been issued; the old regulation allowed private disposition only after a longer list of public bodies declined. — [BLG, New acquisition and disposition process for school board property in Ontario (Mar 2024)](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2024/03/new-acquisition-and-disposition-process-for-school-board-property-in-ontario) [VERIFIED]
- A board regulation (Greater Essex County DSB, revised 16 Apr 2024) describes the 374/23 mechanics: for a discretionary disposition the Minister has **120 days** after the board's notice to name the person/body the property must be offered to, or to tell the board it may offer it to anyone; for a mandatory disposition the 120-day window runs from the Minister's notice. The board and the named offeree have **90 days** from the Minister's notice to reach agreement, extendable once by a further 90 days by mutual agreement; the board must notify the Minister within 14 days of agreement/extension/failure. A qualified appraiser must appraise under CUSPAP (Appraisal Institute of Canada); **all dispositions must be at fair market value**. — [GECDSB, Disposal of Surplus Properties R-BA-20](https://www.publicboard.ca/en/about-gecdsb/Policies-and-Regulations/Regulations/Disposal-of-Surplus-Properties-R-BA-20.pdf) [VERIFIED]
- Waterloo Region DSB's disposition page: Minister identifies priority recipients (school boards; Crown; provincial-priority bodies); "If no priority entity is identified, the Minister informs the board that it may offer the property to any person or body", at FMV, via public tender on bidsandtenders.com or a realtor. — [WRDSB, Properties for sale/lease – open market](https://www.wrdsb.ca/planning/disposition) [VERIFIED]
- Hamilton-Wentworth DSB: Minister "may direct a school board to sell at fair market value, either to a specific party or on the open market"; public entities (municipalities, coterminous boards, colleges, universities) "can tell the Ministry directly that they are interested in future surplus property"; properties not directed to a specific party are offered on the open market. — [HWDSB, Properties for Sale](https://home.hwdsb.on.ca/properties-sale) [VERIFIED]
- For context on the superseded 444/98 process (still governing legacy dispositions until June 2027): HWDSB's procedure lists 16 "preferred agents" in order (coterminous boards, Section 23 program providers, CMSM, college, Collège Boréal, university, children's mental health lead agency, LHIN, public health, Ontario Crown, municipality, Indigenous organizations, federal Crown), a 180-day circulation (90 days for expression of interest + 90 days for a bona fide offer), a public information session within 60 days, FMV, and a Phase 2 open-market period of three years if no preferred agent buys. — [HWDSB Property Disposition Procedure](https://www.hwdsb.on.ca/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Property-Disposition-Procedure.pdf) [VERIFIED]; Ministry memo SB29 (19 Sept 2016) lists the 14 categories of entities that had to be notified simultaneously from 1 Sept 2016. — [Ministry of Education memo 2016:SB29](https://efis.fma.csc.gov.on.ca/faab/Memos/SB2016/SB29_EN_AODA.pdf) [VERIFIED]

**Community Planning and Partnerships (space in operating schools)**
- The Ministry's 2015 Guideline: boards should review facilities "60 percent utilized or less for two years and/or have 200 or more unused pupil places"; "Boards, in compliance with local bylaws, may consider both for-profit and non-profit entities"; but **"Entities that provide competing education services such as tutoring services, JK12 private schools or private colleges, and credit offering entities that are not government-funded, are not eligible partners."** "Boards are not expected to take on additional costs to support facility partnerships"; "the fees charged to partners should cover the operations and capital cost" including administrative costs and property taxes if applicable; partners pay for minor renovations for safety, washrooms and suitability. — [Community Planning and Partnerships Guideline, March 2015 (PDF)](https://efis.fma.csc.gov.on.ca/faab/Memos/B2015/Community%20Planning%20and%20Partnerships%20Guideline%20-%20Final%20-%20March%202015%20ENG_AODA.pdf) [VERIFIED]
- Halton DSB's policy (revised June 2024) repeats the exclusion verbatim ("JK-12 private schools, or private colleges" not eligible) and requires partners to show financial viability, carry $5M liability insurance, and complete vulnerable-sector checks; cost-recovery fees cover operating and capital costs. — [HDSB CPP policy](https://www.hdsb.ca/media/1whdhsis/communityplanningandpartnerships.pdf) [VERIFIED]
- HPEDSB runs Procedure 570 "Community Planning and Partnership" (based on the 2015 Guideline), holds annual public partnership meetings (19–20 Nov 2025 at York River PS, Kente PS and Prince Charles PS Trenton), and lists existing partnerships as child care, "a community commercial kitchen" and municipal libraries; an Expression of Interest form exists. The page does not name schools with unoccupied space or state exclusions. — [HPEDSB CPP page](https://www.hpedsb.on.ca/community/services_available/community_planning_and_partnerships) [VERIFIED]; March 2025 meetings: [HPEDSB, 24 Feb 2025](https://www.hpedsb.on.ca/board/board_resources/newsroom/2025/community_planning_and_partnership_meetings_2025) [VERIFIED]

### Inferences
- An Ontario non-profit independent school **cannot** be a CPP "partner" occupying classrooms in an operating HPEDSB or ALCDSB school; the Guideline's wording is categorical. (A non-school-day arrangement — e.g., a therapy or respite program — is outside this stream.) [VERIFIED wording; application to this project is inference]
- Under 374/23 the only route to a surplus school is: wait for the Minister's 120-day decision; if the Minister names a priority recipient (another board, the Crown, or an affordable-housing/LTC body — the common outcome in this region, see Picton QE, Queen Mary, Pinecrest), the independent school is out; if the Minister releases the property to the open market, the school could buy or lease at appraised FMV like any bidder. Earliest realistic open-market availability for the three Belleville properties: Minister notice (≤120 days from Sept/Oct 2026) → ~Feb 2027; if a priority recipient is named, +90 to +180 days of negotiation → mid/late 2027 before any open-market release. That is tight but not impossible for a Sept 2028 opening if the property were suitable. [ESTIMATE from GECDSB timelines]
- None of the three properties is a natural fit at this scale: Quinte SS is ~121,000 sq ft with $13–21M of renewal needs (Q2 below); Queen Victoria is a 1912 building with FCI 50% and $7.0M renewal needs. A realistic version is **anchor-tenant/condominium space in a third-party redevelopment** of one of these sites (the Picton QE model, where a 19,951 sq ft community hub is planned alongside housing), which would require a developer partner and is speculative. [PROPOSAL]
- Regional precedent shows school-board surplus sales in this area take 2.5–6 years from closure to sale and have all gone to municipal/public buyers at FMV. [VERIFIED facts; inference]
