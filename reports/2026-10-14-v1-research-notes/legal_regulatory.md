## 1. Corporate structure (ONCA vs CNCA; two-entity option)

### Takeaway
An Ontario not-for-profit can be incorporated as of right under the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010 (ONCA) through the Ontario Business Registry for $155 with a 5-business-day online service standard; a charitable corporation is automatically a "public benefit corporation" with mandatory audit at $500k+ revenue, a one-third cap on employee-directors, and (for registered charities) a bar on paying directors without court order. The federal CNCA route and the two-entity (operating charity + foundation) structure were not verifiable from primary sources in this session and are flagged as gaps.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** Not-for-profit incorporation under the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010: $155; service standard 5 business days online, 15 business days by mail. Corporations Information Act annual return for Ontario corporations: $0, immediate online. Page updated 29 Sept 2026. — [Ontario.ca, Cost and time required to register…](https://ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit)
- **[Verified]** ONCA "Guide" (published 10 Nov 2016, updated 15 Oct 2024): every charitable corporation is a public benefit corporation (PBC); a non-charitable corporation becomes a PBC if it receives >$10,000 in a financial year from non-member donors or government grants. PBC financial review: revenue ≤$100k — members may waive both audit and review engagement; >$100k and <$500k — may waive audit but must have review engagement; ≥$500k — audit mandatory. Waiver requires an extraordinary resolution (≥80% of members at a special meeting, or written agreement of all voting members) and lasts until the next annual meeting. Minimum three directors; in a PBC no more than one-third of directors may be employees of the corporation or its affiliates. Directors must act honestly and in good faith in the corporation's best interests with the care, diligence and skill of a reasonably careful person; not generally personally liable for corporate debts, with exceptions (e.g., improper distributions, unpaid employee wages); due-diligence/good-faith-reliance defence available. Members may remove a director by ordinary resolution at a special meeting; 10% of members may requisition a special meeting; annual meeting required, at which members appoint the auditor or review-engagement provider. The transition period for pre-ONCA corporations ended 18 Oct 2024. — [Ontario.ca, Guide to the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010](https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-not-profit-corporations-act-2010)
- **[Commentary]** Miller Thomson (article dated 10 Feb 2022): under ONCA, incorporation is "as of right" — a certificate issues on filing Articles of Incorporation with required information and fee via the Ontario Business Registry; Public Guardian and Trustee (PGT) pre-approval is no longer needed to incorporate a charitable corporation, but PGT approval is still required for amendments to Articles by registered charities; charitable objects (purposes) are still required in the Articles. Articles must state purposes and, where applicable, classes of members and number/range of directors. — [Miller Thomson, ONCA Fast Facts: Incorporation as of right](https://millerthomson.com/en/insights/health-communique/incorporation-as-of-right)
- **[Commentary]** LawyerInfo.ca guide (dated 24 June 2026): steps are (1) Ontario NUANS name report (~$22–$40, reserves name 90 days); (2) draft Articles including name, number of directors and not-for-profit purposes (purposes matter for a later CRA charity application); (3) file via Ontario Business Registry with Ontario registered office address, initial directors and NAICS code; (4) organizational meeting to adopt bylaws, appoint officers, issue memberships — ONCA default bylaws apply automatically if custom bylaws are not adopted within 60 days. Certificate can arrive in 1–2 business days; custom bylaws and first board meeting typically add 2–4 weeks. Directors must be 18+ and not undischarged bankrupts; "for registered charities, paying directors is prohibited without a court order"; directors may be reimbursed reasonable expenses. Default bylaws provide a single membership class; custom bylaws may create voting/non-voting tiers. — [LawyerInfo.ca, How to incorporate a not-for-profit under ONCA](https://lawyerinfo.ca/guides/ontario/business-law-ontario/business-formation-ontario/how-to-incorporate-a-not-for-profit-corporation-under-the-onca-in-ontario/)
- **[Verified]** The Ministry's Notice of Intention form asks the owner's legal type (corporation, charity, not-for-profit, sole proprietorship, partnership, First Nation-operated, other), CRA Business Number (or reason for not having one), and for corporations/charities: name, establishment date, number and a director contact. — [NOI form 52-0147E (2025/06)](https://forms.mgcs.gov.on.ca/dataset/4b1662b4-a7a4-49dd-9ae7-773e86fa40aa/resource/05e988ac-a51b-4f28-b4b7-dd3722a851d8/download/52-0147e.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** For a tuition-free school funded by donations, the PBC audit thresholds will bite quickly: with an opening cohort of 8–20 students and a full-time teaching staff, annual revenue will almost certainly exceed $100k from year one (review engagement at minimum) and plausibly exceed $500k (mandatory audit). Budget for a review engagement or audit from the first fiscal year (costs are a funding-stream matter).
- **[Inference]** The one-third employee-director cap plus the charity-law bar on paying directors means founders who intend to be paid staff (e.g., principal) should plan to be officers/employees reporting to a majority-independent board rather than voting directors — see Q12.
- **[Inference]** ONCA's as-of-right incorporation makes the corporate step fast (days) and cheap; the critical path is CRA charitable registration (Q2), not incorporation. Incorporating in early 2027 leaves ~18 months for CRA registration before a September 2028 opening.

### Gaps
- Federal CNCA process, fees and timelines were not researched (no search budget remained). Not verified in this session.
- Two-entity structure (operating charity + separate foundation holding bursary/endowment): no primary source was read. General charity-law considerations (a public foundation is subject to its own disbursement quota and a stricter business-activity rule; see CPS-019 note in Q2 that private foundations may not carry on any business) suggest the question for counsel is whether a separate foundation adds asset-protection or donor-confidence value sufficient to justify two sets of governance, T3010 filings and audits. **[Confirm with charity counsel.]**
- Charities Accounting Act (Ontario) and O. Reg. 4/01 (director remuneration rules for Ontario charities) were not read directly; the "no pay without court order" statement rests on the LawyerInfo commentary only. **[Confirm.]**

## 2. Charitable registration with the CRA

### Takeaway
Operating a school is an established "advancement of education" charitable purpose (CRA Guidance CG-030, 27 Nov 2020), restricting beneficiaries to students with a disability is an expressly recognised permissible restriction, and charging no tuition raises no issue because education charities have no poverty-relief requirement. The CRA's published 2025–26 service standard is a final decision within 9 months (met ~80% of the time per commentary); plan on 6–12 months and apply online via My Business Account (fax retired 1 April 2026).

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** CRA Guidance CG-030 "Advancement of education and charitable registration" (issued 27 Nov 2020): operating a school is a charitable purpose, including a private secondary school (paras 25, 63); the school must be "not for private profit" (para 63); education must meet a content test (useful, educational value, not promoting a point of view, paras 27–35) and a process test (structured format, legitimate targeted attempt to educate, teaching/learning component, paras 37–50); purpose must benefit the public or a sufficient section of the public (paras 7–15). Fees: "the presence of fees does not mean a program is not charitable" if public benefit is met (para 63); there is no poverty requirement for education purposes, so recipients' financial means need not be considered (paras 61, 65). Private benefit must be incidental — "necessary, reasonable, and proportionate to the resulting public benefit" (para 15); salaries above fair market value are given as an example of unacceptable private benefit (fn 6). Beneficiary restrictions must not be "unjustifiably restricted" (para 54) and cannot be limited to named individuals or persons connected to a person/entity (para 57); permitted restrictions include those courts have recognised, "historically including students at a particular school and restrictions based on disability," and restrictions linked to the purpose, e.g., "a school for the hearing impaired limited to students with hearing impairment" (para 58); restrictions must not be illegal or contrary to public policy, particularly on prohibited grounds of discrimination (para 59); selection may be by scholastic achievement, demonstrated ability, financial need, or a combination (para 60). Non-educational activities such as recreation may be acceptable if incidental (para 44). — [CRA CG-030](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/advancement-education-charitable-registration.html)
- **[Commentary]** Carters Charity & NFP Law Bulletin No. 483 (28 Jan 2021) on CG-030: the guidance's example of structured education is "operating a private secondary school"; tuition fees appear in the list of court-recognised education purposes; public-benefit requirement cross-references CPS-024 and CG-017. — [Carters Bulletin 483](https://www.carters.ca/pub/bulletin/charity/2021/chylb483.pdf)
- **[Commentary]** Carters webinar slides (11 Feb 2021): CG-030 examples include "operating a private secondary school and providing educational facilities, teachers, equipment, and supplies"; scholarships, bursaries, prizes and student financial assistance are also listed; CG-027 (public policy dialogue) was finalised in the same 27 Nov 2020 release. — [Carters Key Takeaways](https://www.carters.ca/pub/seminar/charity/2021/Key-Takeaways-CRAs-New-Guidances-2021-Feb-11.pdf)
- **[Verified]** CRA CG-017 "General requirements for charitable registration" (issued 2 Nov 2012): each purpose must be charitable at law; activities must further those purposes; purposes must deliver benefit to the public or a sufficient section of it; a registered charity "must maintain direction and control over its resources" including through intermediaries. CRA notes it is reviewing related guidance after the 23 June 2022 Budget Implementation Act. — [CRA CG-017](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/guidance-017-general-requirements-charitable-registration.html)
- **[Verified]** CRA CG-027 "Public policy dialogue and development activities" (issued 21 Jan 2019, revised 27 Nov 2020): no cap on resources devoted to PPDDAs that further a stated charitable purpose; charities may seek to keep, oppose or change any law or policy; a charity may never directly or indirectly support or oppose a political party or candidate. — [CRA CG-027](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/public-policy-dialogue-development-activities.html)
- **[Verified]** CRA CPS-019 "What is a related business?" (effective 31 Mar 2003): related business = (a) run substantially all (~90%) by unpaid volunteers, or (b) linked to the charity's purpose and subordinate to it (four linkage types: usual and necessary concomitant of programs; off-shoot of a program; use of excess capacity; sales that promote the charity). Charitable organizations and public foundations carrying on an unrelated business risk loss of registration; applicants running an unrelated business are denied registration; CRA normally first invites the charity to wind up the business or move it to a separate taxable corporation. Private foundations may not carry on any business. — [CRA CPS-019](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/policy-statement-019-what-a-related-business.html)
- **[Commentary]** B.I.G. Charity Law Group (published 26 May 2021, updated 27 Aug 2026): "CRA's published 2025–26 service standard is to issue a final decision within 9 months," met roughly 80% of the time; typical review times: simple well-documented applications 4–6 months, standard 6–9 months, complex 12–18 months; application submitted online through My Business Account; CRA information requests typically allow 30 days to respond; CRA assesses against CG-017. — [Charity Law Group, Charity registration timeline](https://www.charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/charity-registration-timeline-canada-what-to-expect)
- **[Commentary]** Miller Thomson (20 Mar 2026): CRA Charities Directorate fax line retired effective 1 April 2026; online submission via CRA accounts is "the primary (and expected) method"; applies to applications, T3010s, amendments and inquiries; My Business Account onboarding described as "essential." — [Miller Thomson, CRA retiring fax line](https://www.millerthomson.com/en/insights/social-impact/update-from-the-canada-revenue-agency-for-charities-retiring-the-fax-line-effective-april-1-2026/)
- **[Commentary]** Carters "Other CRA Update" (items dated to 26 May 2026): the Charities Directorate no longer pre-approves or reviews in advance proposed changes to charitable purposes or activities — the charity is responsible for ensuring it continues to operate exclusively for charitable purposes; CRA has updated CG-019, a "short guide" and an expanded list of model purposes; CRA's top compliance priorities are aggressive tax planning, offshore activities and ineligible individuals; on revocation, assets must go to an eligible donee within one year. — [Carters, Other CRA Update](https://www.carters.ca/other-cra-update/)
- **[Commentary]** Carters (25 June 2026): T3010 must be filed within six months of fiscal year-end; CRA plans mandatory online T3010 filing from 2027. — [Carters](https://www.carters.ca/?p=15984)
- **[Commentary]** B.I.G. Charity Law Group (published 20 May 2024, updated 26 Aug 2026): CRA model-purpose example — "To advance education by operating a public school providing specialized programs for students with learning disabilities"; a purpose should state what (head of charity), how (activities) and who (beneficiaries); CRA released a "Short Guide: How to Write a Charitable Purpose" in April 2026 alongside CG-019. — [Charity Law Group, Drafting purposes](https://www.charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/how-to-draft-corporate-purposes-for-charity-registration-in-canada)
- **[Commentary]** B.I.G. Charity Law Group (10 Sept 2026) on why CRA rejects preschool/daycare applications: the application must show a teaching plan with defined educational goals built by qualified people, not childcare; private benefit to parents (e.g., enabling them to work) must be incidental; governing documents must match the activities described. — [Charity Law Group, Preschool rejections](https://www.charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/why-cra-rejects-preschool-daycare-charity-applications)
- **[Verified]** Of 85,639 Canadian registered charities, 11,134 (13%) had advancement of education as their object in 2023; Ontario had 4,057 of these; CRA category 10 "Teaching Institutions" (3,190 charities) includes sub-categories for independent elementary, middle and secondary schools. — [Carleton PARG research note, May 2024](https://carleton.ca/profbrouard/wp-content/uploads/PARGnote202443RNRegisteredcharitiesEducation20240525FBML.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** A purpose drafted as "to advance education by operating a school providing specialized programs for children and youth with intellectual developmental disabilities" sits squarely within CG-030's recognised "school for the hearing impaired" analogy (para 58) and the CRA model purpose for learning-disability schools. Selection by disability and need is a *permitted* restriction; selection must still not be on a prohibited Human Rights Code ground beyond the purpose-linked disability restriction (para 59).
- **[Inference]** Zero tuition is not a registration risk; the risk areas CRA will probe are (a) private benefit to founders through above-market salaries or founder-controlled boards, (b) whether any revenue activities (e.g., a student-run café, bus advertising revenue being researched elsewhere) are "related businesses" under CPS-019, and (c) direction and control over any funds routed to or through other organisations.
- **[Inference]** Timeline planning: incorporate (days) → draft purposes/bylaws (weeks) → CRA application early 2027 → 6–12 months review → registration by late 2027/early 2028, leaving a year of receipted fundraising before opening. If registration slips, the school can still open as a non-profit private school (Q3) — Ministry private-school status does not depend on charitable status.
- **[Inference]** Because education charities face no 10% political-activity limit any more (CG-027), the school may advocate for special-education policy, but may not support any trustee or other candidate — relevant given the founder's interest in school-board politics; charity resources cannot be used for that.

### Gaps
- Current disbursement quota rates/thresholds: the CRA "Annual spending requirement" page (modified 27 Jan 2025) was read but only describes the DQ conceptually; the calculation sub-page could not be fetched. Widely reported rules (3.5% on property not used in charitable activities above $100k for charitable organizations / $25k for foundations, 5% on the portion above $1M) were **not verified** in this session. **[Confirm on CRA "Calculate the disbursement quota" page.]**
- CRA's own "Apply for registration" and "Service standards" pages could not be fetched (fetch errors); the 9-month standard rests on commentary only. **[Confirm on canada.ca.]**
- Receiving gifts via a community foundation (e.g., Community Foundation for Lennox & Addington / Quinte-area foundation) or donor-advised fund before registration: not researched (no search budget). General principle from CPS-019/CG-017 is that a registered charity can only grant to "qualified donees" or, since 2022, make "qualifying disbursements" to non-qualified grantees with accountability; an unregistered school would be a non-qualified donee. **[Confirm with the community foundation and charity counsel.]**
- "Ineligible individual" rules (ITA s.149.1(1)) were not read directly.

## 3. Private school status in Ontario (Education Act s.1(1), s.16, s.21)

### Takeaway
Ontario does not license, accredit, fund or inspect the operations of private schools; the only Ministry gate is an annual Notice of Intention (NOI) due 1 September, a one-time $300 fee, an unannounced validation visit in the first ~30 days to confirm the school meets the statutory definition (instruction 9 a.m.–4 p.m. on school days to five or more pupils of or over compulsory school age, a principal in charge, central student records), after which a BSID is issued. There is no Ministry minimum-days, hours, curriculum, teacher-qualification or age-cap requirement for a non-inspected school; falling below five compulsory-age pupils can cost the BSID.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** NOI form 52-0147E (2025/06) quotes Education Act s.1(1): a private school is "an institution at which instruction is provided at any time between the hours of 9 a.m. and 4 p.m. on any school day" for five or more pupils of or over compulsory school age; s.16(1): "No private school shall be operated in Ontario unless a Notice of Intention (NOI)" has been submitted, annually on or before 1 September; s.16(4): a person concerned in management of a school operated in contravention "is liable to a fine of not more than $50 for every day such school is so operated"; schools missing 1 September cannot operate as a private school that year; changes to school-information sections 1–5 must be reported within 30 days; the NOI covers a single location; school name cannot use "College" or "Institute"; form asks for principal qualifications (Ontario PQP or other), number of teachers and how many are OCT members, daily hours of instruction, grade range, whether OSSD credits/inspection sought, whether the school holds a CCEYA child-care licence; operator declares agreement to follow the Private Schools Policy and Procedures Manual and (if using OSRs) to abide by the OSR Guideline, forward OSRs to the Ministry on closure and not charge transfer fees. — [NOI form 52-0147E](https://forms.mgcs.gov.on.ca/dataset/4b1662b4-a7a4-49dd-9ae7-773e86fa40aa/resource/05e988ac-a51b-4f28-b4b7-dd3722a851d8/download/52-0147e.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Ontario.ca "Private schools: Notice of intention" (updated 29 May 2024): NOI due 1 September each year; new schools pay a non-refundable $300 fee (first year only) by money order or certified cheque to the Minister of Finance, mailed to the Private School and International Education Unit, 315 Front St W, 12th floor, Toronto M7A 0B8; new operators must email onsis_sison@ontario.ca for OnSIS access and a new-school NOI account, then submit electronically; NOI collects owner, principal, address, hours of instruction, and latitude/longitude; pre-inspection materials for credit courses are due 1 Sept (September start), 1 Dec (February start), 1 May (July start); the Ministry "does not inspect health equipment or safety and staffing practices." — [Ontario.ca NOI page](https://ontario.ca/page/private-schools-notice-intention-operate-private-school)
- **[Verified]** Ontario.ca "Private schools" (published 20 Jan 2022, updated 2 Oct 2026): private schools are businesses or non-profit organizations operating independently of the Ministry; the Ministry provides no funding; it "does not regulate, license, or accredit private schools, and it does not oversee their operations"; it "does not inspect or approve premises, health and safety practices, or staffing"; it confirms schools meet the Education Act definition, maintains a public list, collects the NOI annually, and inspects private high schools that seek to grant OSSD credits; new schools are inspected "as early as possible in their first year"; non-inspected schools (all private elementary schools and private high schools offering no credit courses) are not required to follow the Ontario curriculum but should offer general subjects (math, science, language, social studies); all private schools must provide statistics on request; "Principals and teachers at private schools do not have to be certified by the Ontario College of Teachers"; parents are advised to ask whether a school conducts criminal reference checks, carries liability insurance for student accidents, has a code of conduct, complaint procedures and records-access policy; schools with credit authority must state so on their website. — [Ontario.ca Private schools](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools)
- **[Verified]** Private Schools Policy and Procedures Manual (2013 edition, PDF dated 25 Nov 2021; landing page updated 20 Mar 2026 and warns it "does contain some outdated links and information"): p.6 definition (five or more pupils of or over compulsory school age); p.7 "principals are not required to have Ontario principal's qualifications" and "teachers are not required to be members of the Ontario College of Teachers (OCT)"; p.9 the principal is responsible for compliance with the manual and legislation; p.10 NOI due 1 September; p.11 failure to submit statistical information within 60 days of request is an offence with a fine up to $200, a school may lose its BSID if it does "not have at least 5 students of compulsory school age"; p.12 on BSID removal any OSRs must be sent to the Ministry; pp.16–17 the validation visit is unscheduled, usually within the first 30 days, on a weekday between 9 a.m. and 4 p.m., confirming the school is operating, has at least five children of compulsory school age, a principal in charge and a central office for student records; if validation succeeds PSAU issues a BSID ("for the exclusive use of the school"); if it fails the school must stop operating for the rest of that school year; if the inspector finds children under JK age, PSAU notifies the Early Learning Division (child-care licensing). The manual does not specify minimum instructional days or hours beyond the 9–4 window and does not mention a 194-day requirement. Non-inspected schools report aggregate student data once a year and educator data three times a year; inspected schools submit elemental OnSIS data due 31 Dec, 31 May and 30 Sept. Schools must have an attendance policy and record absences; report cards must show absences. p.36 requires that community-involvement participants "are adequately covered by the school's insurance." The "Ministry does not regulate, licence, accredit or otherwise oversee the day-to-day operation of private schools." Adult student = 18 or older; mature student = 18+ and out of day school ≥ one school year. Private elementary schools are all non-inspected, must offer instruction in elementary subjects but need not follow the Ontario curriculum, and may (not must) use the Provincial Report Card. — [Manual PDF](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf); [Manual landing page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools-policy-and-procedures-manual-0)
- **[Verified]** Auditor General of Ontario 2013 (s.3.06, data to June 2013): "Ontario has one of the least regulated private school sectors in Canada"; s.16 penalties are $50/day for operating without an NOI, up to $200 for not providing statistics, up to $500 for knowingly false statements; the Ministry had never prosecuted; non-inspected schools' only Ministry check is the one-time validation visit; schools with fewer than five students have continued to operate and the Ministry could not show any BSID revoked for that reason; validation visits do not evaluate curriculum or check health and safety; teachers need not be OCT-certified; owners/principals/teachers need no minimum qualifications; "Ontario has no legislative health and safety requirements for private schools comparable to some other provinces"; criminal background checks are not required for private school operators, principals, teachers or staff. — [AG 2013, s.3.06](https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en13/306en13.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Auditor General 2015 follow-up (s.4.06, data to Oct 2015): validation process strengthened with new criteria (June 2015); education officers must now file complaints with the appropriate authorities for issues such as fire-code violations and contact a Children's Aid Society about child-safety concerns; the Ministry will *not* revalidate existing non-inspected schools and relies on self-reporting; 1,092 private schools in 2014/15 (446 credit-granting, 646 non-credit), ~110,000 students; six schools charged in summer 2015, four convicted; the revised NOI asks for principal qualifications and number of OCT-member teachers. — [AG 2015, s.4.06](https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en15/4.06en15.pdf)
- **[Commentary]** Ontario Federation of Independent Schools (OFIS) "How to register a school": NOI is updated each April and must be submitted before 1 September; at the validation visit (within the first month) the inspector confirms a principal in charge; control over instruction quality, student evaluation and program content; a common assessment policy, reporting procedure and attendance policy; a central office maintaining attendance, OnSIS and OSR files; and "compliance with municipal zoning, fire, health, and building code requirements"; success yields a BSID and listing. OFIS offers a Principals Course Part 1 open to non-members. — [OFIS](https://ofis.ca/how-to-register-a-school-in-ontario)
- **[Verified]** Grand Erie DSB Procedure P107 (received 24 Feb 2020) quotes Education Act s.21(2)(a): a child is excused from attendance if "the child is receiving satisfactory instruction at home or elsewhere," notes that "elsewhere" commonly refers to a school setting, and cites PPM 131 (Home Schooling). — [Grand Erie P107](https://sites.granderie.ca/download_file/view/13536/2374)
- **[Commentary]** Ontario Federation of Teaching Parents: children are excused from compulsory attendance by receiving satisfactory instruction at home "just as they are excused when they attend a private school"; PPM 131 directs boards to accept a parent's letter of intent to home-school as sufficient evidence absent reasonable grounds otherwise (editor's note 2013). — [OFTP](https://ontariohomeschool.org/?page_id=85)
- **[Verified]** Ontario College of Teachers Council memo GC20200305-25 (14 Feb 2020): the Education Act definition covers "all non-government, non-board schools … regardless of ownership"; OCT membership is required only in publicly funded schools; in Dec 2019 Council referred (did not adopt) a motion to recommend that the Education Act require OCT membership for all private-school teachers as an NOI condition. — [OCT memo](https://solstice.oct.ca/about-the-college/council/-/media/E1BEE26F137E4115A7EBEBA247CE44D9.ashx)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** "Private school" status is the only Ontario regulatory category under which an independent organisation can provide full-time schooling that satisfies compulsory attendance for 5+ students; the alternative, s.21(2)(a) "satisfactory instruction … elsewhere," is the same exemption that private-school attendance relies on, so a non-registered "co-op" of 5+ compulsory-age pupils instructed 9–4 would itself meet the s.1(1) definition and be operating unlawfully without an NOI (s.16(1)).
- **[Inference]** The five-pupil floor is measured on pupils "of or over compulsory school age" (6–17 inclusive under s.21(1) as generally understood — statute not read directly). An opening cohort of 8–20 students aged 4–21 must include at least five aged 6+ on validation day; students aged 4–5 and 18–21 count toward the "or over" limb only if 18+ (18–21 are "over" compulsory age and so do count), while 4–5-year-olds do not. **[Confirm wording of s.21(1) on e-Laws.]**
- **[Inference]** Critical path for a September 2028 opening: NOI window opens ~June 2028 in OnSIS (existing schools) / new-school account requested in spring 2028, NOI submitted by 1 Sept 2028, school must actually be operating with ≥5 compulsory-age pupils, a principal and a records office when the inspector arrives unannounced in September 2028. A school that is not yet operating on validation day fails and cannot operate for the rest of that school year — so do not file an NOI for a year in which opening is uncertain.
- **[Inference]** Admitting students 18–21 alongside minors: the Education Act definition is satisfied by pupils "of or over" compulsory age and the Manual expressly contemplates adult students (18+) in private schools. No source was found that prohibits mixed-age enrolment. The practical constraints are safeguarding/consent (adult students are their own decision-makers unless a substitute decision-maker is in place) and Ontario Health atHome eligibility (Q6). **[Confirm with Ministry PSIE Unit and counsel; also confirm the Day Nurseries/CCEYA exemption for 4-year-olds, Q9.]**
- **[Inference]** The Ministry's own position that it does not inspect health, safety, premises or staffing shifts the entire compliance burden for those matters to municipal, public-health, fire, labour and child-protection regimes (Q5–Q9); the OFIS statement that validation checks zoning/fire/health compliance is best read (consistent with AG 2015) as officers now *referring* visible problems to the responsible authorities rather than certifying compliance.

### Gaps

## 4. Credit-granting authority, OSSD/OSSC and Certificate of Accomplishment

### Takeaway
Credit-granting authority comes only from a Ministry inspection under Education Act s.16(7) (fee-based; last publicly reported flat fee $2,450 in 2012/13 rising to a planned $4,050 by 2014/15 — current fee not verified) and is confirmed in writing to the principal; the Ministry has stated that OCT membership is *not* required for private-school teachers, and no source read imposes an OCT requirement even for credit courses. Diplomas and certificates (OSSD, OSSC, Certificate of Accomplishment) are granted by the Minister on the recommendation of the principal; the Certificate of Accomplishment has no credit requirement, but whether a non-inspected private school may issue it is not stated in policy and must be confirmed with the Ministry.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** Denial and Revocation of Authority to Grant Credits (effective 1 Sept 2023; replaces Manual pp.28–31): a school seeking credit authority must submit an NOI annually and be validated in its first year; "An inspection under subsection 16(7) … determines credit authority. A validation visit does not"; the school "may not grant credits toward the OSSD … until it receives written authorization from the ministry"; inspections continue cyclically and ad hoc inspections may occur under s.16(6); requirements include complete pre-inspection materials, scheduling courses for at least 110 hours, delivering curriculum expectations, assessment/evaluation practices, OSR management, prerequisite verification, OSSLT/OSSLC compliance, and OnSIS submissions; denial/revocation follows a notice-of-consideration and written-response process with deadlines set in each letter; denied/revoked schools must stop using Ontario course codes, stop advertising credit authority, transfer OSRs on request and return unused OSSD forms. The document does not mention OCT-certified teachers or the inspection fee. — [Denial and revocation (Sept 2023)](https://www.ontario.ca/files/2023-09/edu-denial-revocation-authority-grant-credits-2022-2023-en-2023-09-19.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Manual (2013): schools request inspection on the NOI; pre-inspection materials (course outlines, School Course Calendar) due at least two months before credit courses start; an inspection fee is charged under s.16(7) and unpaid fees jeopardise credit authority and future NOIs; inspectors examine pre-inspection materials, classroom instruction, assessment/evaluation against *Growing Success*, student records and the principal's role; inspectors must be OCT members with supervisory-officer qualifications (s.16(6)); the manual "does not set OCT requirements for private school teachers in credit courses"; OSSD = 30 credits, literacy requirement, 40 community-involvement hours; a credit = minimum 110 scheduled hours (55 for a half credit); inspected schools must follow the OSR Guideline. — [Manual PDF](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Private School Inspection Report form 3003E (2025/06): inspection ascertains "whether the instruction in secondary school courses" complies with Ministry requirements; checks course calendar completeness, cheating/plagiarism and late-assignment policies, 70%/30% evaluation split, attendance policy and absence recording, OSR storage/security, OST handling, principal's authority to grant credits; the form does not address OCT membership, fees, zoning/fire/building, insurance or police checks. — [Form 3003E](https://forms.mgcs.gov.on.ca/dataset/42c5adf8-a92c-4e30-8c94-3635f7a152a3/resource/10e21520-1064-427c-b9f0-c66bc30081dc/download/3003e_static_nonfillable.pdf)
- **[Verified]** AG 2013: flat inspection fee $2,450 in 2012/13, planned increase to $4,050 by 2014/15; inspections of the 408 credit-granting schools only. — [AG 2013](https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en13/306en13.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Ontario.ca Private schools (updated 2 Oct 2026): inspection frequency depends on the previous report — return in 2–3 years, next school year, or same year; unannounced inspections possible on complaints; credit-granting schools need a principal in charge, complete course calendar, Ministry course titles/codes, assessment and attendance policies, parent reporting procedure, and OSRs/transcripts stored securely at a central office. — [Ontario.ca Private schools](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools)
- **[Commentary]** OurKids (undated): the province assigns 28 inspectors to visit credit-granting schools every two years; "Anyone can start a school with just a Notice of Intention." — [OurKids](https://www.ourkids.net/school/the-truth-about-private-schools-regulation-and-credit-mills-15374)
- **[Verified]** Ontario Schools K–12 Policy and Program Requirements (published 23 Aug 2024; s.6 page): s.6.3 OSSC = minimum 14 credits (7 compulsory: 2 English, 1 math, 1 science, 1 Canadian history/geography, 1 health & PE, 1 arts/computer studies/tech; 7 optional), granted "on request" to students leaving at 18 without the OSSD; s.6.4 Certificate of Accomplishment "may be granted" to students leaving secondary school at 18 without OSSD/OSSC — no credit requirement stated — accompanied by the OST and optionally a copy of the IEP; a returning student's transcript is updated for additional credit and non-credit courses "including modified or alternative special education courses" but no new Certificate of Accomplishment is issued; s.6.5 "On the recommendation of the principal, the Minister of Education grants diplomas and certificates"; s.6.1.5 private schools "are not able to offer adjudication for the Literacy Graduation Requirement" but may adjudicate the Financial Literacy requirement; s.6.1.4 inspected private schools may administer the Financial Literacy requirement by online proctoring. — [OS K–12, s.6](https://www.ontario.ca/document/ontario-schools-kindergarten-grade-12-policy-and-program-requirements/diploma-and-certificate-requirements-related-procedures)
- **[Verified]** OS K–12 s.7.4 (page updated 12 May 2026): alternative (non-credit) courses are "individualized courses, documented in a student's Individual Education Plan," usually preparing students for daily living, supported/independent employment or community living; course codes begin with "K"; s.7.3.1 "The policy for locally developed courses also applies to inspected private schools" (Ministry approval required); s.7.2.4 PLAR is mandatory, directed by the principal, and inspected private schools are named among transfer routes; s.7.3.2.2 inspected private schools may develop religious-education credit courses with Ministry approval (max 4 credits). ### Inferences
- **[Inference]** For an IDD/MID cohort whose programming will be largely alternative ("K") non-credit expectations, credit-granting authority is probably unnecessary at opening and may never be needed; the relevant end-of-school document is the Certificate of Accomplishment. No source gave the current fee or the typical wait between request and inspection. **[Confirm with Ministry.]**

## 5. Staffing qualifications and employment law

### Takeaway
No teacher, principal or EA qualification is legally required in a non-inspected private school, and police checks are not legally mandated for private-school staff — but general employment statutes (ESA, OHSA, Human Rights Code, AODA, CYFSA duty to report) apply in full, the Ontario College of Teachers Act's employer-reporting duty applies to any private school that employs an OCT member, and a vulnerable-sector check is obtainable only through the local police service for positions of trust over children or vulnerable persons. WSIB status for private schools could not be confirmed from WSIB pages read.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** OCT membership "applies only to publicly funded elementary and secondary schools"; principals, VPs and teachers in private schools need not be Ontario Certified Teachers; many larger private schools require OCT as a condition of employment; a private school employing a College member must report to the College under OCT Act s.43.2 when the member's employment is terminated, suspended or restricted for professional misconduct. — [OCT memo, 14 Feb 2020](https://solstice.oct.ca/about-the-college/council/-/media/E1BEE26F137E4115A7EBEBA247CE44D9.ashx)
- **[Verified]** "Criminal background checks are not required for private school operators, principals, teachers or staff. Public schools must complete them." — [AG 2013](https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en13/306en13.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Ontario.ca Police record checks (updated 27 Apr 2026): three check types under the Police Record Checks Reform Act, 2015 — criminal record check; criminal record and judicial matters check; vulnerable sector check (adds certain NCR findings, sexual-offence pardons and limited non-conviction information); a vulnerable sector check can be requested for people in a role of trust or authority over children (under 18) or vulnerable persons (dependent due to age, disability or other circumstance); individual consent and stated purpose required; "Vulnerable sector checks must be completed by your local police service" (not third-party providers); police may require an employer letter describing the position, organisation name, agency code and fingerprints; volunteers get free CRC/CRJMC from police but still pay for VSCs; fees and processing times vary by police service. — [Ontario.ca Police record checks](https://www.ontario.ca/page/police-record-checks)

## 6. Student safeguarding and health policies

### Takeaway
Sabrina's Law, Ryan's Law, PPM 161 (prevalent medical conditions), PPM 158 (concussion), PPM 144 (bullying), PPM 128 (code of conduct) and related safe-schools instruments are all framed as duties of *school boards* (and in PPM 158's case, school authorities and Provincial/Demonstration schools) and do not on their face bind private schools; a private school should nonetheless adopt equivalent policies as the standard of care. Ontario Health atHome's School Health Support Services are expressly available to students enrolled in private schools (fact sheet April 2026), which is a significant operational finding for an IDD cohort. Immunization of School Pupils Act applicability to private schools was not confirmable from the statute text.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** OS K–12 s.1 "The learning environment" (updated 4 Sept 2026): s.1.1.3 PPM 161 — "Boards must have a policy" for asthma, diabetes, epilepsy and anaphylaxis; s.1.1.3.1 Sabrina's Law (2005, c.7) — boards must establish and maintain an anaphylaxis policy; s.1.1.3.2 Ryan's Law (2015, c.3) — boards must establish and maintain an asthma policy; s.1.1.4 PPM 158 — boards must maintain a concussion safety policy; s.1.1.5 PPM 128 code of conduct "for the publicly funded school system"; s.1.1.8 PPM 144 bullying prevention plans (boards); s.1.1.11 PPM 120 violent-incident reporting; s.1.1.12 PPM 166 anti-sex-trafficking protocols (boards). "The page never mentions private schools … it refers to the publicly funded school system." — [OS K–12, s.1](https://www.ontario.ca/document/ontario-schools-kindergarten-grade-12-policy-and-program-requirements/learning-environment)
- **[Verified]** Rowan's Law page (updated 14 Aug 2026): Rowan's Law makes concussion requirements "mandatory for sports organizations" (annual review of concussion awareness resources by athletes under 26/parents of under-18s/coaches/officials; a Concussion Code of Conduct; removal-from-sport and return-to-sport protocols — in force 1 July 2019 and 1 Jan 2022); PPM 158 applies to "school boards, school authorities and Provincial and Demonstration schools" (in force 31 Jan 2020); private schools are not named. — [Ontario.ca Rowan's Law](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rowans-law-concussion-safety)
- **[Commentary]** Food Allergy Canada: Sabrina's Law "affected all publicly funded schools in the province" and requires every school board to maintain an anaphylaxis policy and principals to develop individual plans; private schools not mentioned. — [Food Allergy Canada](https://foodallergycanada.ca/resources/national-school-policies)
- **[Verified]** Ontario Health atHome School Health Support Services fact sheet (dated 04-2026): services include care coordination, nursing, dietitian, and (not in public schools via OHaH) physiotherapy, OT, speech-language therapy, medical equipment and PSWs; eligibility requires a valid OHIP card, enrolment "in a publicly funded school, private school, or home-school program," assessment by a Care Coordinator, a need for professional or personal support to attend school or receive adequate instruction, and a setting with the necessary physical features; a referral from the school principal or special-education representative is required; parents may contact the school or OHaH. - **[Verified]** The AG 2015 follow-up records that Ministry education officers must now contact a Children's Aid Society about child-safety concerns observed at private schools. — [AG 2015](https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en15/4.06en15.pdf)
- **[Verified]** The local public health unit's website (formerly hpepublichealth.ca) now redirects to "Southeast Public Health," which lists three service areas: Hastings & Prince Edward; Kingston, Frontenac, Lennox & Addington; Lanark, Leeds & Grenville (page updated 7 Oct 2026). — [Southeast Public Health](https://southeastph.ca/health-topics/food-and-healthy-eating/food-safety/)

- **[Inference]** Although Sabrina's Law, Ryan's Law and the PPMs do not bind private schools, courts assess negligence against the standard of a reasonably prudent school; a private school that fails to maintain anaphylaxis, asthma, medication-administration, concussion, safe-arrival and anti-bullying policies equivalent to board policies would struggle to defend a claim, and insurers (Q10) will expect them. - **[Inference]** The Immunization of School Pupils Act's definition of "school" is widely understood to include private schools (the public-facing page says "all children attending primary or secondary school"), which would mean the school must cooperate with Southeast Public Health on immunisation records and exclusion orders. **[Confirm on e-Laws s.1 "school" definition — statute could not be read.]**

## 11. Board-linked comparator models (legal mechanics)

### Takeaway
Ontario's board-partnership programs were restructured for 2025–26: Education and Community Partnership Programs (ECPP) are now confined to youth-justice/custody and specified provincial facilities, while a new Care and Treatment Education Programs (CTEP) stream covers students who "cannot attend school because of their primary need for care and/or treatment," with the partner facility expected to be funded by MCCSS or the Ministry of Health and to provide daily care/treatment, space and treatment staff while the board funds teachers and EAs under an MOU; new CTEP programs no longer need Ministry approval. A community organisation without a treatment mandate or MCCSS/MOH funding does not fit the stated model, and the 2024–25 guidelines state that students' special-education needs "should be accommodated through programs and services offered through the school board." Ontario provides no funding to independent schools, and no Education Act provision enabling boards to contract education delivery to third parties was found (statute not readable).

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** Directives for ECPP 2025–26 (Spring 2025, ISBN 978-1-4868-8811-5): ECPP funds education programs for students who cannot attend school because they are court-ordered into a youth-justice secure/open custody or detention facility, an adult custodial facility serving youth, the Syl Apps Youth Centre, or the CPRI program; an "ECPP Facility" is one "approved, designated, established, licensed, or registered by the Government of Ontario, or otherwise deemed appropriate by the board"; the board funds teacher and EA salaries/benefits, liaison/administrator where warranted, instructional supplies, furniture and equipment; accommodation via School Operations Allocation; transportation via Student Transportation Fund; the facility must "provide adequate and appropriate space for the Education Program," deliver treatment/rehabilitation daily, hire and pay its own staff, and maintain treatment/transition and safety plans; new programs only where a new youth-justice or provincial facility opens, case by case with Ministry approval via the regional office; MOU minimum components (staffing plan, responsibilities, number of spaces) set by the annual Core Education Funding regulation; teachers should have at least Special Education Part 1 AQ; pupil–teacher ratio set by the board (O. Reg. 132/12 class sizes do not apply); CTEP is now a separate component with its own Directives; funding is a table amount, not by application; closures need Ministry permission; students in ECPP cannot be added to the Enrolment Register. Some elements take effect only if regulations are made. — [ECPP Directives 2025–26](https://efis.fma.csc.gov.on.ca/faab/Section_23/25-26/EN/edu-2526-ecpp-directives-en-2025-06-24.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Directives for CTEP 2025–26 (Spring 2025, ISBN 978-1-4868-8813-9): CTEP provides education programs for students who "cannot attend school because of their primary need for care and/or treatment"; starts 2025–26, replacing the former ECPP model for care/treatment; boards may also run their own care/treatment supports while seeking partners; a "CTEP Facility" is one approved/designated/established/licensed/registered by the Government of Ontario "or otherwise deemed appropriate by the board"; "CTEP Services should be delivered by facilities funded by the Ministry of Children, Community and Social Services or the Ministry of Health"; "treatment" is not defined; care/treatment must be provided daily; boards fund teacher/EA/liaison salaries and supplies (board-run services may also fund health professionals, regulated social-service professionals and paraprofessionals); facilities provide care/treatment, hire and pay their staff and provide space under the MOU; "Ministry approval is no longer required for new programs, program changes, or closures" — boards notify the regional office, keep records and request a BSID where required; an MOU is required with a facility partner (staffing plan, responsibilities, number of spots, remote learning, conflict resolution, privacy, transitions); boards should consult legal counsel; funding is formula-based — base amount the higher of $300,000 or the board's 2024–25 ECPP care/treatment amount, plus $3.70 × ADE; students 21+ on 31 Dec 2025 are excluded from reported enrolment; teachers should have at least Special Education Part 1 AQ. — [CTEP Directives 2025–26](https://efis.fma.csc.gov.on.ca/faab/Section_23/25-26/EN/edu-2526-ctep-directives-en-2025-06-24.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Directives for ECPP 2026–27 (French edition, Spring 2026, ISBN 978-1-4868-9638-0): eligibility unchanged (court-ordered custody/detention, Syl Apps, CPRI); new programs and changes still require Ministry approval case by case; funding = 2025–26 allocation adjusted for labour costs; admin/supplies capped at $2,666 per teacher FTE and $1,302 per EA FTE; start-up amount up to $3,523 for new/expanded programs; students 21+ on 31 Dec 2026 excluded from enrolment; MOU requirements as above with an Annex 4 checklist; regulations under the Education Act referenced as the funding basis; reporting deadline 30 Sept 2027. — [ECPP Directives 2026–27 (FR)](https://www.ontario.ca/files/2026-05/edu-ecpp-directives2627-fr-2026-05-01.pdf)
- **[Verified]** ECPP Guidelines 2024–25 (the last pre-restructuring guideline): ECPPs are "collaborative partnerships between Ontario district school boards and government-approved facilities"; an ECPP Facility is "approved, designated, established, licensed or registered by the Government of Ontario"; partner ministries funding facilities are MCCSS, Citizenship & Multiculturalism, Health and the Solicitor General; eligible facility types listed include psychiatric facilities, children's aid societies, youth detention/custody, homes for special care, approved hospitals, correctional institutions, long-term care homes and "certain residences for persons with developmental disabilities"; students must be unable to attend "their regular school because of their primary need for care, treatment and/or rehabilitation services"; "The government of Ontario expects all students to attend school"; where a student has special-education needs, "these needs should be accommodated through programs and services offered through the school board"; MOU must set admission and discharge criteria; the board's supervisory officer presents students to facilities for admission; new-program applications were not accepted in some years. — [ECPP Guidelines 2024–25](https://efis.fma.csc.gov.on.ca/faab/Section_23/24-25/EN/edu-2425-ecpp-guidelines-en-2024-04-26.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Ontario.ca Private schools (updated 2 Oct 2026): "The Ministry provides no funding or financial support to private schools." — [Ontario.ca Private schools](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools)
- **[Verified]** Bill 33, Supporting Children and Students Act, 2025 (S.O. 2025, c.12; Royal Assent 20 Nov 2025; Schedule 2 in force on Royal Assent): amends the Education Act to add Ministerial investigations, directions and vesting orders over boards, Ministry/internal auditors, Minister-set expense policies, mandatory board cooperation with police (school resource officer programs in prescribed circumstances), and Ministerial approval of school names; no provisions on private schools, ECPP/CTEP, or third-party delivery were found in the portion read (first 100,000 of 106,447 characters). — [OLA Bill 33](https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-44/session-1/bill-33)
- **[Verified]** PPM 131 and Education Act s.21(2)(a) govern home schooling; boards accept a parental letter of intent absent reasonable grounds; "elsewhere" commonly refers to a school setting. — [Grand Erie P107](https://sites.granderie.ca/download_file/view/13536/2374); [OFTP](https://ontariohomeschool.org/?page_id=85)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** (a) CTEP/ECPP: an independent community organisation serving IDD/MID students who *can* attend school does not meet the CTEP threshold ("cannot attend school because of their primary need for care and/or treatment") and is unlikely to be an MCCSS/MOH-funded "facility"; the "otherwise deemed appropriate by the board" clause gives HPEDSB/ALCDSB discretion, but the 2024–25 guideline's statement that special-education needs "should be accommodated through programs … offered through the school board" signals Ministry resistance to using CTEP as a congregated special-education alternative. A CTEP partnership would also hand the board control of admission (supervisory officer presents students), curriculum, staffing and records, and would exclude 21+ students from funded enrolment.
- **[Inference]** (b) Board-operated congregated class in a community partner's building: the CTEP directives contemplate boards "running their own care/treatment supports" and accommodation being funded through School Operations; outside CTEP, a board placing its own special-education class in leased community space is a board accommodation/lease decision under its general powers — the students remain board pupils, with board teachers, IEPs and funding; the community organisation would be a landlord/program partner, not an educator. No Education Act text could be read to confirm the lease/agreement power. **[Confirm with board counsel; Education Act s.171 and related provisions not readable this session.]**
- **[Inference]** (c) No Education Act mechanism was found (and Bill 33 added none) that lets a board contract out the delivery of instruction for its pupils to a third-party organisation using non-board teachers; board pupils must be taught by board-employed OCT teachers. Treat as "generally no" pending statute review. **[Confirm.]**
- **[Inference]** (d) A home-schooling co-op avoids NOI/validation only while it has fewer than five compulsory-age pupils receiving instruction 9–4; at 8–20 students it would meet the private-school definition and have to register anyway, while forfeiting the clarity of private-school status for OHaH eligibility and parent confidence. Not suitable as a primary structure.
- **[Inference]** (e) Ontario funds no independent school; the only public money that could touch the proposed model is indirect — OHaH health services to eligible students (Q6), SNP food if a site is approved (Q7), and any board-partnership arrangement under (a)/(b). No "inspected private school with a special agreement" funding exception was found in any source read.

- Education Act s.23 (if any), s.171 board powers, and any section on agreements with other entities: e-Laws/CanLII unreadable; not verified.
- Whether HPEDSB or ALCDSB currently operate CTEP sites with developmental-services agencies in Hastings–Prince Edward (HPEDSB special-education page failed to fetch).

## 12. Governance good practice

### Takeaway
Minimum legal governance is set by ONCA (three directors, ≤1/3 employee-directors in a PBC, annual members' meeting, review/audit by revenue band) and by charity law (no director remuneration without court order; private benefit must be incidental; "ineligible individual" screening is a CRA compliance priority). Imagine Canada's Standards Program (board governance, financial accountability and transparency, fundraising, staff management, volunteer involvement) is the recognised voluntary benchmark, open to organisations of any size.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** ONCA Guide: ≥3 directors; PBC ≤ one-third employees as directors; directors' duty of honesty, good faith and reasonable care; annual meeting; members appoint auditor/reviewer; audit/review thresholds ($100k / $500k for PBCs); members may remove directors. — [ONCA Guide](https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-not-profit-corporations-act-2010)
- **[Commentary]** "For registered charities, paying directors is prohibited without a court order"; directors may be reimbursed reasonable expenses. — [LawyerInfo.ca](https://lawyerinfo.ca/guides/ontario/business-law-ontario/business-formation-ontario/how-to-incorporate-a-not-for-profit-corporation-under-the-onca-in-ontario/)
- **[Verified]** CG-030 para 15 and fn 6: private benefit must be incidental; salaries above fair market value are a private benefit. — [CRA CG-030](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/advancement-education-charitable-registration.html)
- **[Commentary]** CRA's current compliance priorities include "ineligible individuals"; charities are now responsible for self-assessing changes to purposes/activities. — [Carters, Other CRA Update](https://www.carters.ca/other-cra-update/)
- **[Verified]** Imagine Canada Standards Program covers five areas — board governance, financial accountability and transparency, fundraising, staff management, volunteer involvement; "Accreditation is open to every charity and nonprofit in Canada, big or small"; fees and level thresholds not on the page read. — [Imagine Canada Standards Program](https://www.imaginecanada.ca/en/standards-program)
- **[Verified]** Carters (25 June 2026): T3010 due within six months of fiscal year-end; mandatory online filing planned from 2027. — [Carters](https://www.carters.ca/?p=15984)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Founders can be paid staff (principal, executive director) of the charity provided they are not directors, or — if ONCA's one-third rule and Ontario charity law permit — are non-voting on their own compensation and the arrangement is approved by an independent majority at fair-market value; the cleaner structure is founder-as-employee with a majority-independent board and a written conflict-of-interest policy under which the employee-founder does not vote on budget lines affecting their pay. Ontario's O. Reg. 4/01 under the Charities Accounting Act (not read) sets conditions under which a charity may pay a director for services other than as a director; **[Confirm with counsel.]**
- **[Inference]** Recommended minimum policy set for a CRA application and insurers: conflict of interest; board code of conduct; financial controls/signing authority; gift acceptance and receipting; privacy (student records, PHIPA where health information is held); safeguarding (VSC, two-adult rule, reporting); whistleblower/complaints; and a student-admissions policy that documents disability-and-need-based selection consistent with CG-030 paras 58–60.

- O. Reg. 4/01 (Charities Accounting Act) director-compensation conditions: not read.
- CRA "ineligible individual" definition (ITA s.149.1(1)): not read.
