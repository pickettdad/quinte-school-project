# Phase 2 summary — 50-student design, first pass (2026-10-15)

Discussion summary only. Evidence, labels and sources are in the topic files. Figures are 2026 prices, capital donor-funded without debt (planning assumption).

## 1. What is feasible, promising, uncertain, expensive

- **Feasible:** the legal route (v1), a 50-student structure of six age-banded homerooms with per-subject pathway regrouping, two staggered cohorts preserving ~305 instructional minutes each, a non-credit Certificate-of-Accomplishment base pathway, OHRC-compliant individualized admissions.
- **Promising:** Ontario Health atHome now **verified** as serving private-school students with nursing, OT, PT, SLP, dietitian **and personal support** (public-school students get less) — avoided cost $31K–$123K/yr at 50; one sponsored lift bus on two runs serves ~2/3 of students for ~$95K/yr; Belleville zoning by-law 2024-100 read in full — "school" is as-of-right in CF zones (former school sites, the 253 Dundas St W church), not in commercial/mixed zones; local philanthropy is larger than v1 implied (regional flow ≈ $35–50M/yr; one Belleville foundation with $109M assets grants $5.6M/yr to Quinte quality-of-life causes).
- **Uncertain:** the three surplus schools (Minister's 120-day decision ≈ Feb 2027; open-market availability, if ever, late 2027–28 — not a basis for a 2028 opening); whether a private school's daily bus must be chrome-yellow and what sponsor lettering is allowed (Reg. 612 still unread); OHaH *local* hours; SNP site eligibility; family propensity to enrol.
- **Expensive:** people (74% of cost — $1.97M at 50, 27.2 FTE); replacement reserve on an old building ($56–170K/yr is the hidden line); QSS partial occupancy (a wing carries 45–70% of utilities unless physically isolated at $0.5–1.5M).

## 2. Proposed 50-student structure (PROPOSAL)
Six homerooms: E1 (4–7) 7, E2 (8–10) 7, E3 (11–13) 6, S1 (14–16) 10, S2 (17–18) 10, S3 (19–21) 10; three pathways (formal/semi-formal/informal) regrouped per subject; literacy/numeracy at 1–4 per adult. Cohort A 8:30–2:30, Cohort B 9:15–3:15; all shared activity in the overlap. Two staffing designs on the table — education file 23.75 FTE vs staffing file 27.2 FTE (adds a rotation teacher, two EAs, more clinician hours). **Needs a founder decision.**

## 4. Ranked property and transport configurations (PROPOSAL)
Property: (1) Sir Winston Churchill PS whole property — right scale, condition and availability unknown; (2) a separable QSS wing under lease from a public-body acquirer — best facilities (greenhouse, cafeteria, foods room, elevator), lowest availability; (3) 253 Dundas St W church + modular cluster — the only path the project controls for 2028, ~$2.9–4.6M ($5.3M with soft costs); (4) Queen Victoria (1912, FCI 50–72%) not recommended. Two-track plan: secure the controllable path; pursue surplus schools through the government questions.
Transport: (1) one sponsored lift bus, two runs, West (Trenton/Bayside) and East (Shannonville/Belleville-east) pickup points, 65-min stagger, $95K/yr, 32–36 seats; (2) contracted operator $102–121K; (3) family-only + charters $22–25K but expect 10–20% enrolment loss; (4) second bus +$65–85K/yr when demand or far corridors require. Stagger is ~$25K/yr cheaper than a second bus.

## 5. Qualified opportunities
Prospect register: 0 committed / 29 plausible / 37 speculative. OTF moves to speculative (schools and primary-education organizations excluded). Capacity: a $2.3–2.5M/yr operating ask is 5–7% of regional philanthropic flow — larger than any local charity raises; every regional precedent for a new institution combined a $0.75–1.5M lead gift, several hundred donors and municipal support over 3–5 years. Restricted service funding (Passport, OAP, SSAH, SNP) $55–155K; no interest-free institutional lender exists — interest-free money is philanthropic debt (donor loans, forgivable loans, guarantees; no personal guarantees).
